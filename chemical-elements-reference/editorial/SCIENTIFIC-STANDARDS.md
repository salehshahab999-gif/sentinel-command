# Scientific and Editorial Standards

## Intended readership
Advanced graduate students, doctoral researchers, postdoctoral researchers, and specialists in chemistry, physics, materials science, and nuclear science.

## Required fields for each element
1. Identity, classification, discovery, and etymology.
2. Atomic structure, electron configuration, term symbols, and quantum properties.
3. Ionization energies, electron affinity, and atomic spectroscopy.
4. Nuclear structure, isotopes, decay modes, half-lives, and evaluated nuclear data.
5. Physical properties with measurement conditions and phase.
6. Thermodynamic properties and chemical reactivity.
7. Bonding, compounds, oxidation states, and coordination chemistry.
8. Materials properties, crystal structures, and applications.
9. Toxicology, radiological hazards, and handling precautions.
10. References, data provenance, uncertainty, and limitations.

## Evidence labels
- **MEASURED:** directly measured experimental result.
- **EVALUATED:** critically evaluated value from a recognized data authority.
- **CALCULATED:** result obtained from a stated theoretical or computational method.
- **ESTIMATED:** extrapolated or inferred value.
- **UNKNOWN:** no sufficiently reliable value identified.

## Citation requirements
Cite the primary dataset or publication, version where available, DOI or stable URL, and access date. Secondary aggregators must not be treated as the ultimate authority when the underlying source can be identified.

## Units and conditions
Use SI units where practical. State temperature, pressure, phase, allotrope, and sample conditions whenever they affect a value. Preserve the source's uncertainty and significant figures.

## Conflict handling
Do not average incompatible values. Explain differences in definitions, measurement conditions, or evaluation methods and identify the preferred value with a reason.

## Special caution
For short-lived and superheavy elements, distinguish experimentally observed properties from theoretical predictions. Do not fill gaps with fabricated or unqualified values.


## Source authority and update policy (verified 18 November 2026)

There is no single publication year that is "the latest" for every property of every element. The project must select the newest authoritative release for each data class, record its version and access date, and avoid replacing a specialist evaluated dataset with a newer but less authoritative general-purpose table.

| Data class | Preferred authority | Verified release/status |
|---|---|---|
| Standard atomic weights and isotopic composition | CIAAW | The current definitive Standard Atomic Weights table is labelled 2024. CIAAW states that it is based on the 2021 report, with Gd, Lu, and Zr revised in 2024. |
| Atomic spectra, energy levels, ionization energies | NIST Atomic Spectra Database (SRD 78) | Version 5.12; data content last updated November 2024. Check version history at each project release. |
| Evaluated nuclear levels, decay modes, half-lives | NNDC ENSDF | Continuously revised. The archive exposed a dataset dated 5 November 2026 at the verification date. Cite the actual nuclide evaluation/dataset, not merely the database homepage. |
| Nuclear reaction and neutron data | CSEWG ENDF/B | ENDF/B-VIII.1 was reported as the newest recommended evaluated nuclear data library in August 2026. Use only for relevant reaction quantities, not as a substitute for ENSDF structure/decay data. |
| Periodic-table cross-check and element overview | IUPAC; Royal Society of Chemistry | Useful secondary cross-checks and context. They do not supersede specialist primary/evaluated databases for numerical research values. |
| Physical, thermodynamic, and materials properties | Primary peer-reviewed literature and recognized evaluated handbooks/databases | Select by property and measurement conditions; record edition, DOI, uncertainty, and sample conditions. Do not assume that one general periodic-table website is authoritative for every field. |

### Release verification rules
1. At each manuscript release, re-check the source's official version history or dataset timestamp. Record the access date and the exact release identifier.
2. If a source has not published a newer release, retain its latest verified release even if its year is earlier than the current calendar year.
3. Where a newer paper reports a value that differs from an evaluated database, present the discrepancy and assess the paper's methods, uncertainty, and peer-review status. Do not silently overwrite the evaluated value.
4. For each numeric property, record: value, unit, uncertainty, temperature/pressure and phase where relevant, evidence label, source/version, DOI or stable URL, and access date.
5. Distinguish the date of a web-page update from the date of the underlying scientific evaluation. A recently updated webpage does not automatically mean every datum on it was newly measured or re-evaluated.

### Official source endpoints
- CIAAW Standard Atomic Weights: https://ciaaw.org/atomic-weights.htm
- CIAAW Abridged Standard Atomic Weights: https://ciaaw.org/abridged-atomic-weights.htm
- NIST Atomic Spectra Database: https://physics.nist.gov/asd
- NIST ASD version history: https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/ASD/Html/verhist.shtml
- NNDC ENSDF archive: https://www.nndc.bnl.gov/ensdfarchivals/
- IAEA ENSDF information: https://nucleus.iaea.org/Pages/evaluated-nuclear-structure-data-file.aspx
- CSEWG ENDF/B-VIII.1 publication: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nds.2026.04.001
- IUPAC Periodic Table: https://iupac.org/what-we-do/periodic-table-of-elements/
- Royal Society of Chemistry Periodic Table: https://periodic-table.rsc.org/

**Editorial rule:** the phrase "latest data" must always mean latest verified authoritative release for the specific property, not simply the newest year printed on a source.
