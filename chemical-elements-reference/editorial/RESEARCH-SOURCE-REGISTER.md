# Research Source Register

**Snapshot:** 2026-11-18  
**Purpose:** Supplement the authoritative evaluated databases with peer-reviewed primary research, doctoral theses, and active university/national-laboratory research outputs.  
**Scope at this snapshot:** Initial discovery for elements 1–20 and cross-cutting nuclear-data methodology. A source is not automatically authoritative for every property of an element; each citation must be attached only to the claim it actually supports.

## Source-selection policy

1. **Controlling numerical authorities remain property-specific.** CIAAW controls standard atomic weights and representative isotope compositions; NIST ASD controls the adopted atomic-spectroscopy records cited for ionization energies and level designations; ENSDF/NuDat and NUBASE are evaluated nuclear-data resources.
2. **Primary research papers** are used for novel measurements, methods, and element-specific findings. Record the sample, instrument, calibration, conditions, uncertainty budget, and whether the result has been independently reproduced.
3. **Doctoral theses** are valuable for detailed apparatus descriptions, calibration protocols, systematic-error budgets, and extensive bibliographies. A thesis is not treated as peer-reviewed journal evidence merely because it was awarded a PhD. Its claims must be checked against later papers and evaluated datasets.
4. **Research-group pages** identify active investigators and current work but are discovery sources, not evidence for numerical claims unless they link to a citable paper or dataset.
5. **Citation counts are not a quality verdict.** Check correction/retraction notices, later contradictory work, and the exact scope of each cited result.
6. **Date discipline:** distinguish the publication date of a paper from the version/date of the dataset used in the manuscript. Do not describe a 2021 evaluation as the latest 2026 dataset unless a new release is verified.

## Search limitations and quality notes

- OpenAlex topic searches can return semantically adjacent but irrelevant papers, especially for broad phrases such as “materials properties.” Such results are not admitted to the bibliography until title, abstract, source, and property relevance are checked.
- A doctoral thesis may be excellent evidence for method and experimental detail, but its numerical result must be reconciled with later peer-reviewed work and current evaluated databases.
- The Scite MCP literature-verification service was not accessible during this snapshot because the connected account requires a paid plan or active trial. Therefore no claim is made that Scite has checked the cited papers for retractions, corrections, or contrasting citations. Publisher pages and DOI records must be checked directly in the relevant audit phase.
- This register is a discovery and provenance aid, not a substitute for the element-by-element property bibliography. Only sources actually used in a monograph should be cited in that monograph's claim-level references.

## Transition-metal block: selected scholarly records for elements 21–30

### Scandium (Z = 21)

**Dean, J. W., Melia, H. A., Nguyen, T. V. B., and Chantler, C. T. (2024).** “Scandium Kα and Kβ x-ray spectra with ab initio satellite intensities and energy eigenvalues.” *Physical Review A* 109, 022809. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.109.022809.  
**Use:** multiconfiguration Dirac–Hartree–Fock calculations of the characteristic Sc Kα/Kβ spectrum and satellite intensities. These are theoretical spectral predictions, not measurements of ordinary bulk properties.

**Dean, J. W., Chantler, C. T., Smale, L. F., and Melia, H. A. (2020).** “An absolute energy characterisation of scandium Kβ to 2 parts per million.” *Journal of Physics B: Atomic, Molecular and Optical Physics* 53, 205004. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6455/abb1ff.  
**Use:** experimental absolute Sc Kβ emission energy and uncertainty, useful for an X-ray spectroscopy subsection.

### Titanium (Z = 22)

**Saloman, E. B. (2012).** “Energy Levels and Observed Spectral Lines of Neutral and Singly Ionized Titanium, Ti I and Ti II.” *Journal of Physical and Chemical Reference Data*. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4754694.  
**Use:** NIST critical compilation of Ti I and Ti II energy levels and observed spectral lines; preferred over general periodic-table summaries for the compiled atomic spectral record.

**Li, W., Hartman, H., Wang, K., and Jönsson, P. (2020).** “Theoretical investigation of oscillator strengths and lifetimes in Ti II.” *Astronomy & Astrophysics* 643, A156. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202038909.  
**Use:** theoretical oscillator strengths and radiative lifetimes for Ti II. Keep theoretical predictions labelled CALCULATED, not MEASURED.

**Millet, M. and Dauphas, N. (2014).** “Ultra-precise titanium stable isotope measurements by double-spike high resolution MC-ICP-MS.” *Journal of Analytical Atomic Spectrometry* 29, 1444–1458. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1039/C4JA00096J.  
**Use:** stable Ti-isotope ratio metrology, double-spike methodology, precision and reproducibility.

**Hoare, L. et al. (2022).** “Empirical and experimental constraints on Fe-Ti oxide-melt titanium isotope fractionation factors.” *Geochimica et Cosmochimica Acta* 326, 253–272. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gca.2022.02.011.  
**Use:** isotope fractionation during mineral/melt processes; not the source for representative natural isotope fractions.

### Vanadium (Z = 23)

**Holmes, C. E. et al. (2016).** “Experimentally measured radiative lifetimes and oscillator strengths in neutral vanadium.” *The Astrophysical Journal Supplement Series* 224, 35. DOI: https://doi.org/10.3847/0067-0049/224/2/35.  
**Use:** experimental V I radiative lifetimes for 25 levels and oscillator strengths for 208 lines, using time-resolved laser-induced fluorescence and Fourier-transform spectroscopy.

**Holmes, C. E. (2015/2016).** “Experimentally measured oscillator strengths and associated radiative lifetimes of neutral vanadium using Fourier Transform Spectroscopy and Laser-Induced Fluorescence.” PhD thesis, Imperial College London. DOI: https://doi.org/10.25560/34943.  
**Use:** detailed experimental setup, analysis, branching fractions, lifetime methods and hyperfine-structure work. Prefer the 2016 peer-reviewed article for the core published results; use the dissertation for extended methodological detail.

### Chromium (Z = 24)

**Saloman, E. B. (2012).** “Energy Levels and Observed Spectral Lines of Neutral and Singly Ionized Chromium, Cr I and Cr II.” *Journal of Physical and Chemical Reference Data*. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4754694.  
**Use:** NIST critical compilation of Cr I/Cr II levels, spectral lines and ionization energies, including the literature basis of the evaluated values.

**Zeeshan, F. (2017).** “Laboratory based XAS measurements, electron-induced double K-shell ionization of Sc, Cr and Cu, and chemical sensitivity of photoinduced Kβ hypersatellite transition in Cr compounds.” PhD thesis, University of Fribourg. Repository: https://folia.unifr.ch/unifr/documents/308155.  
**Use:** experimental X-ray absorption and high-resolution emission/hypersatellite measurements for Cr in multiple chemical states, plus Sc and Cu inner-shell work. Treat as doctoral dissertation, not a peer-reviewed article.

### Manganese (Z = 25)

**Liggins, F. S., Pickering, J. C., Nave, G., Ward, J. W., and Tchang-Brillet, W.-U. L. (2021).** “The Spectrum and Term Analysis of Singly-Ionized Manganese.” *The Astrophysical Journal Supplement Series* 252, 10. DOI: https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4365/abc75f.  
**Use:** peer-reviewed high-resolution FT and grating measurements of Mn II, including thousands of spectral lines and revised/evaluated energy levels.

**Liggins, F. S. (2018).** “The spectrum and term analysis of singly-ionised manganese.” PhD thesis, Imperial College London. DOI: https://doi.org/10.25560/59134.  
**Use:** detailed thesis-level methodological and analysis material. The 2021 journal article is the preferred peer-reviewed source for the main results.

### Iron (Z = 26) and nickel (Z = 28)

**Ward, J. W. (2021).** “Experimental Atomic Spectroscopy of Iron Group Elements for Astrophysics.” PhD dissertation, University of Maryland. DOI: https://doi.org/10.13016/vgfe-sg1f. Institutional record: https://drum.lib.umd.edu/items/e991cae4-ded6-4f6d-8dd4-a7af6431e35c.  
**Use:** laboratory/evaluated Ni V spectral data, Fe V cross-checking, and Fe II branching-fraction measurements in UV/VUV. Cite only the ion stage and measurement actually treated in the dissertation; this is not a neutral Fe I/Ni I data source.

### Cobalt (Z = 27)

**Wang, X., Yu, Q., Li, Q., Gao, Y., and Dai, Z. (2018).** “Experimental radiative lifetimes, branching fractions, and oscillator strengths of some levels in Co I.” *Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society* 475(2), 1881–1886. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stx3336.  
**Use:** experimental lifetimes for 24 Co I levels and branching fractions/oscillator strengths for selected transitions; the paper reports its uncertainty treatment and measurement conditions.

### Copper (Z = 29)

**Zeeshan, F. (2017).** PhD thesis, University of Fribourg, DOI/repository record above.  
**Use:** high-resolution Cu inner-shell X-ray absorption and double-K-shell ionization experiments; distinguish the Cu results from Sc and Cr results in the same dissertation.

**Nguyen, T. V. B., Melia, H. A., Janssen, F. I., and Chantler, C. T. (2022).** “Multiconfiguration Dirac-Hartree-Fock theory for copper Kα and Kβ diagram lines, satellite spectra and ab initio determination of single and double shake probabilities.” *Physical Review A* 105, 022811. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.105.022811.  
**Use:** theoretical MCDHF calculations for Cu characteristic X-ray spectra and satellite processes; label results CALCULATED.

### Zinc (Z = 30)

**Dong, P. H., Nguyen, T. V. B., Krstevski, S. M., and Chantler, C. T. (2026).** “Zinc Kβ ab initio diagram and satellite transitions.” *Journal of Physics B: Atomic, Molecular and Optical Physics* 59, 045002. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6455/ae43d7.  
**Use:** peer-reviewed theoretical MCDHF investigation of Zn Kβ diagram and satellite transitions, with reported eigenvalue and transition-rate convergence metrics.

**Bowman, P., Rijal, R., Sier, D., Tran, C. Q., Dao, M. H., Diaz-Moreno, S., Hayama, S., Aramini, M., Ramanan, N., and Chantler, C. T. ## Active research groups and investigator leads

These groups are discovery pathways for current papers, theses and data provenance. Their webpages identify expertise and active topics; an institutional page alone must not be cited as evidence for a numerical property.

- **University of Melbourne, X-ray Optics and Synchrotron Science / Professor Christopher T. Chantler:** https://www.ph.unimelb.edu.au/~chantler/opticshome/papers.html. Work includes high-accuracy atomic/X-ray physics, transition-metal K-shell spectra and theoretical/experimental comparisons. The 2024 Sc K spectra and 2026 Zn Kβ paper are direct examples.
- **NIST Atomic Spectra / Laboratory Astrophysics contributors (including Gillian Nave):** the NIST atomic spectra bibliographic database and project outputs are key pathways for evaluated wavelengths, energy levels and transition probabilities: https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-atomic-spectra-bibliographic-databases.
- **Imperial College London and Lund University atomic spectroscopy teams (including Juliet Pickering, Hampus Nilsson and collaborators):** the V I and Mn II records above illustrate combined Fourier-transform spectroscopy, time-resolved laser-induced fluorescence, branching-fraction and lifetime methods.
- **Malmö University theoretical atomic-structure work (including P. Jönsson and collaborators):** the Ti II calculations above provide a path for oscillator-strength and radiative-lifetime data; calculated results must remain distinct from measurements.
- **Jilin University atomic spectroscopy work (including Z. Dai and collaborators):** the Co I paper above provides experimental radiative-lifetime and branching-fraction data.
