# Element 75: Rhenium

**Symbol:** Re  
**Atomic number:** 75  
**Group / period / block:** 7 / 6 / d  
**CIAAW standard atomic weight:** 186.207(1)  
**Neutral ground-state configuration:** [Xe] 4f¹⁴ 5d⁵ 6s²  
**Ground-state term:** ⁶S₅/₂  
**First ionization energy:** 7.83352 eV (NIST)  
**Naturally occurring isotopes:** ¹⁸⁵Re and ¹⁸⁷Re  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; evaluated nuclear-decay and high-temperature alloy datasets remain to be consolidated.

## 1. Atomic and electronic structure
Rhenium is a group-7 transition metal with neutral ground configuration [Xe]4f¹⁴5d⁵6s² and ground term ⁶S₅/₂. The half-filled 5d subshell is associated with the high-spin atomic ground state. Rhenium chemistry spans several oxidation states, most notably +4, +6 and +7. In oxo-chemistry, Re(VII) forms perrhenate, ReO₄⁻, a tetrahedral oxoanion with strong chemical and analytical relevance.

## 2. Isotopic composition and nuclear properties
CIAAW representative amount fractions are ¹⁸⁵Re 0.3740(5) and ¹⁸⁷Re 0.6260(5), with standard atomic weight 186.207(1). ¹⁸⁷Re is a long-lived beta-minus emitter that decays to ¹⁸⁷Os, with a half-life of approximately 4.12×10¹⁰ years. The ¹⁸⁷Re–¹⁸⁷Os decay system is used in geochronology and isotope geochemistry, especially for dating sulfide minerals and tracing mantle evolution. Exact decay constants, branching and uncertainty budgets must be taken from evaluated nuclear datasets and isotope-geochemistry standards.

## 3. Physical and thermophysical properties
At ambient conditions rhenium is a dense, hard, silvery metal with a hexagonal close-packed structure. The RSC compilation lists melting point 3185 °C, boiling point 5590 °C and density 20.8 g cm⁻³. Its melting point is exceeded among metals only by tungsten according to the RSC compilation. Refractory behaviour, high-temperature strength and resistance to wear make it valuable in specialised alloys. Creep, recrystallisation and oxidation depend on temperature, atmosphere, grain size and alloy composition.

## 4. Chemical behaviour
Rhenium forms compounds in oxidation states from negative values in selected complexes to +7 in oxo-species. Perrhenate is relatively stable in oxidising aqueous environments, while reduced rhenium species may form under other conditions. Rhenium sulfides, oxides, halides and organometallic complexes are known. Redox potential and solution speciation depend on pH, ligand, ionic strength and redox environment; oxidation state alone is not a complete speciation description.

## 5. Materials science and applications
Rhenium is added to tungsten- and molybdenum-based alloys to improve ductility, high-temperature performance and resistance to embrittlement in selected compositions. Nickel-based superalloys use rhenium additions to improve creep resistance in turbine blades. Rhenium-containing catalysts are used in hydrogenation and petroleum-reforming processes. It is also used in electrical contacts and specialised X-ray or high-temperature components. Supply is constrained because rhenium is rare and is commonly recovered as a by-product of molybdenum processing.

## 6. Safety and environmental considerations
Rhenium has no established biological role. Toxicological data for many compounds are limited; risk assessment should distinguish metal, soluble salts, oxides and process aerosols. High-temperature processing, catalyst residues and rhenium-bearing smelter dust require industrial hygiene and waste controls. Do not infer the safety of a particular rhenium compound from the relative inertness of the bulk metal.

## 7. Discovery and nomenclature
Rhenium was isolated in 1925 by Walter Noddack, Ida Tacke and Otto Berg. Its name derives from *Rhenus*, the Latin name for the Rhine. It was among the last naturally occurring stable elements to be identified, reflecting its low abundance and difficult extraction.
