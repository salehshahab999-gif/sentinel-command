# Element 87: Francium

**Symbol:** Fr  
**Atomic number:** 87  
**Group / period / block:** 1 / 7 / s  
**Standard atomic weight:** none (no stable isotopes); representative mass number [223]  
**Neutral ground-state configuration:** [Rn] 7s  
**Ground-state term:** ²S₁/₂  
**First ionization energy:** 4.0727 eV (NIST/PDG compilation)  
**Representative longest-lived isotope:** ²²³Fr  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; most bulk physical properties are estimates because only atom-scale quantities are available.

## 1. Atomic and electronic structure
Francium is the heaviest group-1 alkali element. Its neutral ground configuration is [Rn]7s¹ and its ground term is ²S₁/₂. The expected and observed chemical behaviour is dominated by the +1 oxidation state. Relativistic effects are substantial in this heavy atom, so simple extrapolation from caesium is not sufficient for high-precision atomic modelling. Francium is of interest in atomic parity-violation and precision spectroscopy experiments because its large nuclear charge enhances certain electroweak effects.

## 2. Nuclear properties and isotopes
Francium has no stable isotopes and therefore no standard atomic weight. The bracketed mass number [223] is a conventional representative value. ²²³Fr is the longest-lived commonly studied isotope, with a half-life of about 22 minutes. It occurs transiently in the natural uranium-235 decay series and can be produced in nuclear reactions. Its decay modes and nuclear levels are isotope-specific and must be quoted from current ENSDF/NuDat evaluations. The extremely short half-life means that francium cannot accumulate as a bulk natural metal.

## 3. Physical properties and measurement limitations
The RSC fact box lists an estimated melting point near 21 °C and an estimated boiling point near 650 °C; density is unknown. These are not direct measurements on a bulk sample. Francium is available only in minute atom-scale quantities, so many macroscopic properties are theoretical or extrapolated from periodic trends and atomic calculations. Such values must be labelled as estimates and should not be used as precision reference constants.

## 4. Chemical behaviour
Francium is expected to form Fr⁺ and highly ionic salts. Its chemistry is predicted to resemble caesium, with strong reactivity toward water and oxygen. Direct bulk reaction measurements are not feasible because of the element's scarcity and radioactivity. Any quantitative chemical claim should be tagged as theoretical, trace-scale experimental, or inferred from homologous-element behaviour.

## 5. Research significance
Francium has no practical commercial applications. Its principal value is in fundamental atomic physics, spectroscopy, tests of atomic theory and nuclear structure. Experiments use trapped or produced atoms in highly specialised facilities; the data are not representative of bulk chemical handling or industrial materials.

## 6. Radiological safety
Francium is intensely radioactive and can only be produced and studied in licensed specialist facilities. Its short-lived isotopes and decay products require isotope-specific containment, shielding, contamination control and dosimetry. It is not an ordinary laboratory chemical and should not be assigned routine bulk-metal handling procedures.

## 7. Discovery and nomenclature
Francium was discovered in 1939 by Marguerite Perey at the Curie Institute. The name derives from France. It was the last naturally occurring element to be discovered and isolated, though naturally occurring atoms are present only in minute transient quantities.

## 8. Evidence quality and unresolved work
- **Atomic data:** NIST/PDG tables provide configuration, term and first ionization energy; current ASD level extraction and isotope hyperfine data remain necessary for specialist use.
- **Nuclear data:** ENSDF/NuDat must supply ²²³Fr half-life, decay branches and daughter levels.
- **Physical properties:** RSC melting/boiling values are estimates; density is not established.
- **Pending:** direct trace-scale chemical-speciation reports; updated calculated thermophysical properties with uncertainty; atomic parity-violation and hyperfine reference datasets.
