# Element 34: Selenium

**Symbol:** Se  
**Atomic number:** 34  
**Group:** 16  
**Period:** 4  
**Block:** p-block  
**Classification:** Post-transition metal / metalloid classification depends on convention  
**Standard atomic weight (CIAAW):** 78.971(8)  
**Ground-state electron configuration:** [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p⁴  
**Physical state at approximately 298 K and 1 bar:** Solid

## 1. Atomic and periodic context
Selenium is a period-4 p-block element. Its valence-shell configuration is represented by [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p⁴. The element's bonding and chemistry depend on oxidation state, coordination environment, allotrope, and phase; a single periodic classification does not capture all of these behaviours.

## 2. Isotopic and nuclear context
Natural selenium has six isotopes commonly listed in the CIAAW representative composition: ⁷⁴Se, ⁷⁶Se, ⁷⁷Se, ⁷⁸Se, ⁸⁰Se, and ⁸²Se. Their representative amount fractions are 0.0086(3), 0.0923(7), 0.0760(7), 0.2369(22), 0.4980(36), and 0.0882(15). ⁸²Se is radioactive by double-beta decay with an extraordinarily long half-life, around 10²⁰ years, and is commonly treated as stable in ordinary chemical reference tables.

**Evidence status:** Natural isotope masses and representative amount fractions are attributed to CIAAW. A complete evaluated nuclear chart, decay modes, branching ratios, and half-life uncertainties have not yet been compiled for this element.

## 3. Physical properties
Selenium has several allotropes, including red and grey forms. At approximately 298 K and 1 bar it is solid. RSC lists a melting point near 494 K, boiling point near 958 K, and density around 4.809 g cm⁻³; these values should be tied to the specified allotrope and measurement convention in a critical compilation.

**Evidence status:** Reference values are drawn from the RSC periodic-table fact box. These are secondary reference values and are not yet a critical compilation of primary measurements. Phase, allotrope, pressure, purity, and uncertainty must be specified before final release.

## 4. Chemical behaviour and bonding
Selenium commonly exhibits oxidation states −2, +4, and +6. It forms selenides, elemental allotropes, selenites, and selenates, with extensive covalent and redox chemistry. Selenium is chemically related to sulfur and tellurium, but differences in bond strengths, polarizability, and accessible oxidation states produce distinct compound behaviour.

## 5. Materials science and applications
Selenium is used in glass manufacture and decolourization, pigments, photovoltaic and photoconductive materials, and selected electronic applications. Its allotropy and photoconductivity are important in materials science. Selenium is also a micronutrient in tightly controlled biological amounts and is used in some metallurgical formulations.

## 6. Toxicology and handling
Selenium has a narrow margin between nutritional requirement and toxicity. Excess exposure can cause selenosis, with effects depending on chemical species and route. Hydrogen selenide and some selenium compounds are acutely hazardous. Occupational controls and compound-specific safety data are essential.
