# Element 43: Technetium

**Symbol:** Tc  
**Atomic number:** 43  
**Group:** 7  
**Period:** 5  
**Block:** d-block  
**Standard atomic weight:** No standard atomic weight; conventional mass number [98]  
**Ground-state electron configuration:** [Kr] 4d⁵ 5s²  
**Ground-state term:** ⁶S₅/₂  
**First ionization energy:** 7.1194 eV  
**State at 20 °C and 1 bar:** Solid

## 1. Atomic structure and quantum properties
Technetium is a period-5 d-block element. Its tabulated ground-state electron configuration is [Kr] 4d⁵ 5s². The ground-state term and first ionization energy are taken from the NIST-reviewed periodic-table compilation. These summary values do not replace a level-resolved treatment: a research-grade spectral appendix should query NIST ASD for level energies, J values, transition probabilities, wavelengths, isotope shifts, and uncertainties.

## 2. Nuclear properties and isotopic composition
Technetium has no stable isotopes and therefore no standard atomic weight. CIAAW uses the bracketed mass number [98] as a conventional indication, not a standard atomic weight. Important nuclides include ⁹⁷Tc (t₁/₂ ≈ 4.21 × 10⁶ y), ⁹⁸Tc (≈ 4.2 × 10⁶ y), ⁹⁹Tc (≈ 2.111 × 10⁵ y; β⁻), and metastable ⁹⁹ᵐTc (≈ 6.0066 h; predominantly isomeric transition to ⁹⁹Tc). Values here are selected evaluated/compiled values; a complete decay scheme requires direct ENSDF record extraction.

**Data status:** CIAAW provides the current standard atomic-weight and natural-isotope framework for stable elements. For technetium, no standard atomic weight exists because there are no stable isotopes. For radioactive nuclides, the values stated here are selected evaluated/compiled data, not a complete decay evaluation. Full ENSDF extraction is required for adopted levels, branching fractions, beta spectra, gamma intensities, internal conversion, Q values, and uncertainties.

## 3. Physical properties and phase behaviour
- **Melting point:** 2430 K (2157 °C)
- **Boiling point:** 4535 K (4262 °C)
- **Density:** approximately 11 g cm⁻³ (secondary reference value)
- **Ambient crystal structure:** hcp, space group P6₃/mmc (No. 194); reported lattice parameters a ≈ 273.5 pm and c ≈ 438.8 pm

These are reference values, not a critical thermodynamic compilation. Density and transition temperatures depend on purity, porosity, isotopic composition where relevant, pressure, and measurement convention. The hcp/fcc assignments describe the ambient elemental metal, not all compounds or metastable thin-film phases.

## 4. Chemical thermodynamics, oxidation states, and coordination chemistry
Technetium exhibits oxidation states spanning a broad range, commonly discussed from −I to +VII, with +VII particularly stable in pertechnetate, TcO₄⁻. The tetrahedral pertechnetate ion is chemically analogous to permanganate and perrhenate in several respects. Reduction of Tc(VII) produces lower-valent species whose ligand environment, hydrolysis, redox potential, and kinetic stability are strongly dependent on pH and coordinating ligands. Technetium coordination chemistry is central to radiopharmaceutical design; trace-level ⁹⁹ᵐTc chemistry must be distinguished from bulk-metal chemistry.

A final thermodynamic dataset should provide standard-state formation enthalpies, Gibbs energies, entropies, heat capacities, redox potentials, aqueous speciation constants, and temperature/pH dependence with traceable primary or critically evaluated sources. No unverified numerical thermodynamic constants are inserted in this draft.

## 5. Materials science and catalytic function
⁹⁹ᵐTc is the workhorse radionuclide of diagnostic nuclear medicine. It is commonly supplied by a ⁹⁹Mo/⁹⁹ᵐTc generator as pertechnetate and incorporated into radiopharmaceutical complexes. Its approximately 140.5 keV gamma emission and approximately six-hour half-life are well matched to gamma-camera imaging. Technetium also has research relevance in corrosion inhibition, radiochemistry, nuclear-fuel-cycle monitoring, and environmental transport studies.

For engineering use, catalytic activity must be reported with reaction conditions, support composition, loading, particle-size distribution, space velocity, feed composition, conversion, selectivity, and deactivation history. Bulk elemental properties alone cannot predict supported-catalyst performance.

## 6. Toxicology, radiological safety, and handling
All technetium isotopes are radioactive. Handling requires isotope-specific radiological controls, contamination prevention, shielding and waste management determined by activity, emission spectrum, chemical form, and applicable regulations. Volatile or mobile pertechnetate species can behave differently from reduced, ligand-bound Tc species in environmental and biological systems.
