# Element 014: Silicon

**Symbol:** Si  
**Atomic number:** 14  
**Group:** 14  
**Period:** 3  
**Block:** p-block  
**Classification:** Metalloid  
**Standard atomic weight (CIAAW 2024):** [28.084, 28.086]  
**Ground-state electron configuration:** [Ne] 3s² 3p²  
**Physical state at approximately 298 K and 1 bar:** Solid

## 1. Atomic and periodic context
Silicon is element 14. Its valence configuration, [Ne] 3s² 3p², provides the first-order framework for understanding bonding, oxidation-state preferences, and periodic trends. Group trends are guides rather than substitutes for compound-specific thermodynamic and spectroscopic evidence.

## 2. Isotopic and nuclear context
The stable isotopes are ²⁸Si, ²⁹Si, and ³⁰Si. Electronic-grade silicon purity, defect density, dopant concentration, and crystal orientation are critical variables in semiconductor performance. The standard atomic weight is not a nuclide mass and must not be used as a substitute for isotope-specific nuclear data. A complete nuclear appendix will report nuclide masses, natural abundances, decay modes, and half-lives separately.

## 3. Chemical and materials significance
Silicon is foundational to semiconductor devices, photovoltaic cells, integrated circuits, glass, ceramics, and silicone materials.

## 4. Data quality and interpretation
The standard atomic weight is taken from the CIAAW 2024 table. An interval denotes the range expected for normal terrestrial materials; parenthetical digits indicate uncertainty in the final quoted digits. The electron configuration is the conventional ground-state configuration. Physical state is stated at approximately 298 K and 1 bar.
