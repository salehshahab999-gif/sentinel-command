# Element 89: Actinium

**Symbol:** Ac  
**Atomic number:** 89  
**Group / period / block:** actinoid / 7 / d  
**Standard atomic weight:** none (no stable isotopes); representative mass number [227]  
**Neutral ground-state configuration:** [Rn] 6d 7s²  
**Ground-state term:** ²D₃/₂  
**First ionization energy:** 5.17 eV (NIST Atomic Data Handbook)  
**Representative natural isotope:** ²²⁷Ac  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; isotope-specific nuclear, radiochemical and high-precision material data remain incomplete.

## 1. Atomic and electronic structure
Actinium is the first actinoid by atomic-number convention. Its neutral ground configuration is [Rn]6d7s² and its ground term is ²D₃/₂. The dominant oxidation state is +3, producing Ac³⁺ with a radon-like closed-shell core. Its chemistry resembles lanthanum in several respects, although actinium's radioactivity and scarcity make detailed coordination chemistry difficult to study.

## 2. Nuclear properties and isotopes
Actinium has no stable isotopes and therefore no standard atomic weight. The bracketed mass number [227] is representative of the principal naturally occurring isotope. ²²⁷Ac has a half-life of about 21.77 years and occurs in the ²³⁵U decay series. It decays through a complex chain involving beta and alpha branches. ²²⁵Ac, with a half-life of about 9.92 days, is an important alpha-emitting radionuclide in targeted alpha-therapy research. Exact decay branching, emissions and daughter redistribution must be taken from current ENSDF/NuDat and radiopharmaceutical references.

## 3. Physical and condensed-matter properties
Actinium is a soft, silvery radioactive metal. The RSC compilation lists melting point about 1050 °C, boiling point about 3200 °C and density about 10 g cm⁻³. These are compiled values; direct measurement is constrained by scarcity, radioactivity and self-heating. The metal is expected to have a face-centred cubic structure under ambient conditions. All bulk property values should be labelled reference estimates unless supported by primary measurement.

## 4. Chemical behaviour
Actinium is predominantly trivalent and forms compounds such as Ac₂O₃, halides and coordination complexes. Ac³⁺ is a large, hard Lewis acid with a preference for oxygen-donor ligands. Hydrolysis, complexation and redox behaviour are not as extensively mapped as those of more common lanthanoids because of limited isotope supply and radiological constraints. The close chemical similarity between Ac³⁺ and La³⁺ is important in separation and radiochemical processing.

## 5. Nuclear medicine and applications
Actinium has no broad bulk industrial use. ²²⁵Ac is of major interest for targeted alpha therapy because its decay chain releases high-linear-energy-transfer alpha particles over a short tissue range. Radiopharmaceutical development must control chelator stability, daughter recoil, biodistribution, dosimetry and off-target organ exposure. This is an isotope- and molecule-specific research field; it should not be equated with the chemical properties of elemental actinium.

## 6. Radiological safety
Actinium is radioactive and requires licensed radiochemical handling. ²²⁷Ac and ²²⁵Ac have different decay chains and dose implications. Internal contamination, daughter radionuclide redistribution and the management of contaminated materials are central concerns. Bulk-metal handling assumptions are inappropriate for high-activity actinium sources.

## 7. Discovery and nomenclature
Actinium was discovered in 1899 by André-Louis Debierne in pitchblende residues; Friedrich Oskar Giesel independently reported it in 1902. Its name derives from Greek *aktis*, meaning ray, referring to radioactivity. It was the first actinoid discovered and later served as the chemical starting point for the actinoid series.
