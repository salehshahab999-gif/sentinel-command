# Element 61: Promethium

**Symbol:** Pm | **Atomic number:** 61 | **Group / period / block:** lanthanoid / 6 / f  
**Representative mass number:** [145] (no standard atomic weight; no stable isotopes)  
**Ground-state configuration:** [Xe] 4f⁵ 6s²  
**Ground-state term:** ⁶H°₅/₂  
**First ionization energy:** 5.5819 eV  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; isotope-specific nuclear evaluation is essential and remains incomplete.

## Electronic structure
Promethium is the only lanthanoid with no stable isotope. Its neutral ground configuration is [Xe]4f⁵6s²; the common ionic state is Pm³⁺ with a 4f⁴ configuration. Its chemistry is predominantly trivalent and resembles neighboring lanthanoids, while its radioactivity determines handling, availability and applications.

## Nuclear properties
All promethium isotopes are radioactive. ¹⁴⁵Pm is a commonly cited long-lived isotope (half-life about 17.7 years); ¹⁴⁷Pm has a half-life of about 2.62 years and decays by beta-minus emission. ¹⁴⁷Pm has been used in compact beta-voltaic and thickness-gauge applications. These rounded values are orientation only; evaluated half-lives, beta spectra, branching and uncertainties must be retrieved isotope-by-isotope from ENSDF/NuDat. The bracketed mass [145] is a representative mass number, not an atomic weight.

## Chemistry and materials
Pm³⁺ forms salts and coordination compounds analogous to other trivalent lanthanoids. Because promethium is scarce and radioactive, bulk physical-property measurements are limited. Many tabulated macroscopic properties are estimated or extrapolated; they must be labeled as such rather than presented as measured constants.

## Applications and radiological considerations
The beta emission of ¹⁴⁷Pm has supported specialized sealed-source applications. Source design requires containment, shielding against bremsstrahlung where relevant, activity accounting and regulatory controls. Pm should not be treated as an ordinary commodity rare-earth metal.

## Verification priorities
ENSDF/NuDat is the controlling source for isotope half-lives, decay energies and emissions. NIST ASD and evaluated atomic-structure literature should be used for spectroscopy. CIAAW should be cited for the absence of a standard atomic weight. Any bulk density, melting point or thermodynamic value must be labeled measured, estimated or extrapolated with its source.
