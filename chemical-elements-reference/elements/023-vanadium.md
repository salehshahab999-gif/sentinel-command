# Element 023: Vanadium

**Symbol:** V  
**Atomic number:** 23  
**Group:** 5  
**Period:** 4  
**Block:** d-block  
**Classification:** Transition metal  
**Standard atomic weight (CIAAW 2024):** 50.9415(1)  
**Ground-state electron configuration:** [Ar] 3d³ 4s²  
**Physical state at approximately 298 K and 1 bar:** Solid

## 1. Atomic and periodic context
Vanadium is element 23. Its ground-state electron configuration, [Ar] 3d³ 4s², is the conventional starting point for interpreting valence, bonding, and periodic behavior. Transition-metal chemistry is particularly sensitive to oxidation state, coordination environment, ligand field, and phase.

## 2. Isotopic and nuclear context
Natural vanadium consists mainly of ⁵¹V with a small amount of ⁵⁰V, a long-lived radionuclide. Multiple oxidation states, especially +2 through +5, enable rich redox chemistry. Standard atomic weight summarizes naturally occurring material and is not interchangeable with the mass of any individual nuclide. Isotope-specific masses, abundances, and decay data belong in the dedicated nuclear-data tables.

## 3. Chemical and materials significance
Vanadium strengthens alloy steels and is used in catalysts, specialty alloys, and vanadium redox-flow battery electrolytes.

## 4. Data quality and interpretation
The standard atomic weight is reproduced from the CIAAW 2024 table. Parenthetical digits indicate uncertainty in the final quoted digits. The electron configuration is the conventional ground-state configuration. Physical state is stated at approximately 298 K and 1 bar.
