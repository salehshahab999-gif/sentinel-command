# Element 107: Bohrium

**Symbol:** Bh  
**Atomic number:** 107  
**Group / period / block:** 7 / 7 / d  
**Standard atomic weight:** none; representative mass number [270]  
**Predicted/compiled ground-state configuration:** [Rn] 5f¹⁴ 6d⁵ 7s²  
**Ground-state term:** ⁶S₅/₂  
**First ionization energy:** approximately 7.7 eV (PDG 2024 review of NIST/atomic-theory data)  
**Representative isotopes:** ²⁷⁰Bh and ²⁷⁴Bh  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; chemistry is based on atom-at-a-time experiments and bulk physical properties are unknown.

## 1. Atomic and electronic structure
Bohrium is a transactinoid in group 7, below manganese, technetium and rhenium. Its predicted neutral configuration is [Rn]5f¹⁴6d⁵7s², with ground term ⁶S₅/₂. The +7 oxidation state is expected to be important by group analogy, but relativistic effects can modify stability and volatility relative to lighter group-7 elements. Atomic configuration and ionization energy are compiled in the PDG review of NIST data.

## 2. Nuclear properties and isotopes
Bohrium has no stable isotopes and no standard atomic weight. [270] is a representative mass number. CIAAW/NUBASE2020 list ²⁷⁰Bh with a half-life of 3.8(3.0) minutes and ²⁷⁴Bh with 57(27) seconds. These values have large uncertainties, reflecting limited event counts. Decay chains, alpha energies and branching must be taken from the latest evaluated nuclear data.

## 3. Physical properties and measurement limits
No macroscopic sample of bohrium has been produced. RSC lists melting point, boiling point and density as unknown. Crystal structure, thermal conductivity, mechanical behaviour and bulk phase transitions have not been measured. The correct scientific status for bulk properties is “unknown”; values inferred from group trends or computational models must be labelled theoretical.

## 4. Chemical behaviour
Chemical investigations have studied only a few atoms of bohrium. Experiments separated and characterised a volatile oxychloride species, commonly assigned as BhO₃Cl. The measured volatility was lower than that of analogous oxychlorides of lighter group-7 elements, consistent with relativistic predictions and deviations from simple periodic extrapolation. The limited number of observed atoms makes the result highly specialised and uncertainty-sensitive.

## 5. Research relevance
Bohrium has no commercial or technological application. Its principal importance is the experimental study of group-7 chemistry at very high atomic number and the role of relativistic effects. Research uses accelerator-produced isotopes, rapid gas-phase chemistry and correlated alpha-decay detection.

## 6. Radiological safety
Bohrium isotopes are radioactive and very short-lived. Research is conducted only in specialised nuclear facilities using licensed containment, target and separator contamination controls, and isotope-specific radiation protection. No conventional bulk chemical handling conditions exist.

## 7. Discovery and nomenclature
Bohrium was first reported in 1976 by research teams at the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research in Dubna; later work at GSI in Darmstadt contributed to confirmation. IUPAC adopted the name bohrium in 1997, honouring physicist Niels Bohr.

## 8. Evidence quality and unresolved work
- **Atomic configuration, term and ionization energy:** PDG 2024 review of NIST/atomic-theory data.
- **Nuclear data:** CIAAW radioactive-elements table and NUBASE2020; use the latest ENSDF evaluation for details.
