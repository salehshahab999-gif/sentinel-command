# Element 105: Dubnium

**Symbol:** Db  
**Atomic number:** 105  
**Group / period / block:** 5 / 7 / d  
**Standard atomic weight:** none; representative mass number [268]  
**Predicted/compiled ground-state configuration:** [Rn] 5f¹⁴ 6d³ 7s²  
**Ground-state term:** ⁴F₃/₂  
**First ionization energy:** approximately 6.8 eV (PDG 2024 review of NIST/atomic-theory data)  
**Representative isotope:** ²⁶⁸Db  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; data are limited to nuclear properties and atom-at-a-time chemistry.

## 1. Atomic and electronic structure
Dubnium is a transactinoid in group 5, below vanadium, niobium and tantalum. Its predicted neutral ground configuration is [Rn]5f¹⁴6d³7s², with term ⁴F₃/₂. The +5 oxidation state is expected to be the most important, consistent with group-5 chemistry, although relativistic effects may modify stability and complexation relative to lighter homologues. Ground-state and ionisation values are taken from the PDG review of NIST atomic data and should be considered compiled or model-supported rather than conventional bulk measurements.

## 2. Nuclear properties and isotopes
Dubnium has no stable isotopes and no standard atomic weight. [268] is a representative mass number. CIAAW lists ²⁶⁸Db with half-life 29(3) hours. Other isotopes have different half-lives and decay chains. Exact alpha-decay energies, spontaneous-fission branches and daughter assignments require current NUBASE/ENSDF evaluation.

## 3. Physical properties and measurement limits
No macroscopic sample of dubnium has been produced. Density, melting point, boiling point, crystal structure and transport properties are not experimentally established. Any periodic-table values for bulk properties are theoretical predictions and should be identified as such. Experiments typically produce a few atoms and identify them through correlated radioactive decay.

## 4. Chemical behaviour
Atom-at-a-time experiments have studied dubnium's chemical behaviour in group-5 systems. The +5 state and oxyhalide formation are of interest because they allow comparison with niobium and tantalum. Volatility and adsorption behaviour in gas-phase and chromatographic systems provide evidence about relativistic bonding. Results must be interpreted with uncertainty due to very low atom counts, short measurement windows and radioactive decay during transport.

## 5. Research relevance
Dubnium has no commercial or technological use. It is important for understanding the periodic trends of group 5 at high atomic number and for testing relativistic quantum-chemical predictions. The element is produced and studied only in specialised accelerator and radiochemistry facilities.

## 6. Radiological safety
All dubnium isotopes are radioactive and short-lived. Research requires licensed nuclear facilities, target and separator contamination control, alpha-radiation management and isotope-specific dosimetry. No bulk chemical handling assumptions are appropriate.

## 7. Discovery and nomenclature
The discovery of element 105 was claimed independently by teams in Berkeley and Dubna, and the priority was disputed. IUPAC adopted the name dubnium in 1997 in honour of Dubna, the Russian research centre that made major contributions to the synthesis of the transfermium elements.
