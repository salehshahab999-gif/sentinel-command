# Element 59: Praseodymium

**Symbol:** Pr | **Atomic number:** 59 | **Group / period / block:** lanthanoid / 6 / f  
**Standard atomic weight:** 140.90766(1)  
**Ground-state configuration:** [Xe] 4f³ 6s²  
**Ground-state term:** ⁴I°₉/₂  
**First ionization energy:** 5.4702 eV  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; detailed spectroscopy, isotope evaluation and materials-property audit remain open.

## Atomic and electronic structure
Praseodymium is a trivalent lanthanoid with a 4f³ ground-state configuration. The 4f shell is shielded by 5s and 5p electrons, giving localized, strongly spin-orbit-coupled states and a rich multiplet spectrum. Pr³⁺ is the dominant oxidation state; +4 occurs in selected compounds and is stabilized in particular oxide and fluoride environments.

## Isotopes and nuclear data
Natural praseodymium is monoisotopic: ¹⁴¹Pr is the only naturally occurring stable isotope. Other isotopes are radioactive and have applications or significance in nuclear-reaction studies. Evaluated half-lives, decay modes, branching fractions and radiation energies must be taken from ENSDF/NuDat for each nuclide, not extrapolated from neighboring lanthanoids.

## Physical and magnetic behavior
The metal is silvery and relatively soft, with crystal structure and magnetic response dependent on temperature and sample condition. Praseodymium exhibits complex magnetic ordering at low temperatures; any quoted ordering temperature or susceptibility must identify phase, purity and measurement conditions.

## Chemistry and materials
Praseodymium forms oxides with multiple oxidation states, notably Pr₂O₃ and mixed-valence Pr₆O₁₁. Its compounds are used in specialty glass and ceramic colorants, catalysts, permanent-magnet alloys and optical materials. Pr-containing magnets and alloys must be characterized by composition and microstructure; their properties are not those of elemental Pr.

## Lanthanoid trends
Pr³⁺ ionic radii continue the lanthanoid contraction relative to La³⁺ and Ce³⁺ for comparable coordination. Comparisons require a consistent coordination-number convention. The 4f electrons contribute localized magnetic moments and sharp optical transitions.
