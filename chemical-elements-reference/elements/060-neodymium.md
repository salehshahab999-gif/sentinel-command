# Element 60: Neodymium

**Symbol:** Nd | **Atomic number:** 60 | **Group / period / block:** lanthanoid / 6 / f  
**Standard atomic weight:** 144.242(3)  
**Ground-state configuration:** [Xe] 4f⁴ 6s²  
**Ground-state term:** ⁵I₄  
**First ionization energy:** 5.5250 eV  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; full spectroscopic and isotope-data audit remains open.

## Electronic structure
Neodymium has a 4f⁴6s² neutral ground configuration and a ⁵I₄ ground term. The common oxidation state is +3, yielding Nd³⁺ with a 4f³ configuration. Its partly filled 4f shell produces strong paramagnetism and rich optical and magnetic spectra.

## Isotopic composition
Natural neodymium comprises several isotopes: ¹⁴²Nd, ¹⁴³Nd, ¹⁴⁴Nd, ¹⁴⁵Nd, ¹⁴⁶Nd, ¹⁴⁸Nd and ¹⁵⁰Nd. ¹⁴⁴Nd and ¹⁴⁵Nd are primordial radioactive nuclides with extremely long half-lives; ¹⁴⁶Nd, ¹⁴⁸Nd and ¹⁵⁰Nd are conventionally treated as stable in many applications, although ¹⁴⁸Nd and ¹⁵⁰Nd undergo double-beta decay on very long timescales. Isotopic abundances and nuclear uncertainties must be taken from CIAAW and ENSDF/NuDat respectively.

## Magnetic and solid-state physics
Nd metal has temperature-dependent allotropy and magnetic ordering. Nd³⁺ moments in compounds are strongly affected by crystal-field splitting and exchange interactions. Magnetic data must specify temperature, crystallographic phase and sample composition.

## Chemistry and materials
Nd is predominantly +3 in aqueous and solid-state chemistry. Nd₂O₃ is a basic rare-earth oxide. Neodymium is technologically central to Nd-Fe-B permanent magnets, whose high energy product arises from the combined anisotropy and magnetization of the Nd₂Fe₁₄B phase and microstructural engineering. Nd-doped glasses and crystals are important laser gain media, including Nd:YAG; optical transition assignments and cross-sections depend on host lattice and wavelength.
