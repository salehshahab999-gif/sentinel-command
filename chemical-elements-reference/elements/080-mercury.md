# Element 80: Mercury

**Symbol:** Hg  
**Atomic number:** 80  
**Group / period / block:** 12 / 6 / d  
**CIAAW standard atomic weight:** 200.592(3)  
**Neutral ground-state configuration:** [Xe] 4f¹⁴ 5d¹⁰ 6s²  
**Ground-state term:** ¹S₀  
**First ionization energy:** 10.4375 eV (NIST/PDG compilation)  
**Naturally occurring isotopes:** ¹⁹⁶Hg, ¹⁹⁸Hg, ¹⁹⁹Hg, ²⁰⁰Hg, ²⁰¹Hg, ²⁰²Hg and ²⁰⁴Hg  
**State at 20 °C and 1 bar:** Liquid  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; speciation, isotope and environmental-risk datasets require detailed evaluation.

## 1. Atomic and electronic structure
Mercury is a group-12 element with neutral ground configuration [Xe]4f¹⁴5d¹⁰6s² and ground term ¹S₀. Relativistic contraction and stabilisation of the 6s orbital contribute to mercury's unusually weak metallic cohesion and its liquid state near room temperature. The principal oxidation states in common chemistry are +1 and +2. Mercury(I) compounds generally contain the dimeric cation Hg₂²⁺ rather than isolated Hg⁺ ions; mercury(II) is widespread in halides, oxides and coordination complexes.

## 2. Isotopic composition and nuclear properties
CIAAW representative amount fractions are: ¹⁹⁶Hg 0.0015(1), ¹⁹⁸Hg 0.1004(3), ¹⁹⁹Hg 0.1694(12), ²⁰⁰Hg 0.2314(9), ²⁰¹Hg 0.1317(9), ²⁰²Hg 0.2974(13) and ²⁰⁴Hg 0.0682(4). The standard atomic weight is 200.592(3). ¹⁹⁹Hg and ²⁰¹Hg have non-zero nuclear spin and are important in mercury NMR and isotope tracing. ¹⁹⁶Hg is an extremely long-lived alpha emitter. Isotope-specific decay properties and uncertainties must be checked against ENSDF/NuDat.

## 3. Physical and thermophysical properties
At 20 °C and 1 bar elemental mercury is a dense, silvery liquid. The RSC compilation lists melting point −38.829 °C, boiling point 356.619 °C and density 13.5336 g cm⁻³. Mercury's low melting point is associated with relativistic effects and weak metallic bonding. Vapour pressure, evaporation rate and dissolved concentration depend strongly on temperature and chemical environment; a mercury exposure assessment must distinguish liquid metal from vapour and chemically bound species.

## 4. Chemical behaviour and environmental speciation
Mercury forms inorganic Hg(I) and Hg(II) compounds and organomercury species. Oxidation state and ligand environment control solubility, volatility, mobility and biological uptake. In aquatic systems, microbial methylation can produce methylmercury, which bioaccumulates and biomagnifies in food webs. Total mercury concentration alone does not describe risk; speciation, dissolved organic matter, redox conditions, sulfide, pH and food-web structure are essential variables.

## 5. Materials science and historical applications
Mercury was historically used in thermometers, barometers, fluorescent lamps, electrical switches, rectifiers, chlor-alkali electrolysis and dental amalgams. Many uses have been restricted, replaced or phased down because of health and environmental concerns. Modern uses are limited to specialised instruments, selected chemical processes and research applications under controlled conditions. Amalgam formation with several metals is chemically distinctive, but mercury amalgam use requires exposure and lifecycle assessment.

## 6. Toxicology and safety
Mercury is a major environmental toxicant. Inhaled elemental mercury vapour can be absorbed through the lungs and affect the nervous system and kidneys. Methylmercury is a potent neurotoxicant that can cross the placenta and accumulate in aquatic food chains. Inorganic mercury salts have different absorption and toxicity profiles. Never treat liquid mercury, mercury vapour, inorganic salts and organomercury compounds as a single exposure category. Handling requires appropriate containment, spill procedures and regulated waste management.

## 7. Discovery and nomenclature
Mercury has been known since antiquity and was associated with the planet and Roman deity Mercury. Its symbol Hg derives from Greek *hydrargyros*, meaning water-silver. Alchemical and metallurgical practices used mercury in amalgamation long before its toxicology was understood.
