# Element 018: Argon

**Symbol:** Ar  
**Atomic number:** 18  
**Group:** 18  
**Period:** 3  
**Block:** p-block  
**Classification:** Noble gas  
**Standard atomic weight (CIAAW 2024):** [39.792, 39.963] [1]
**Ground-state electron configuration:** [Ne] 3s² 3p⁶   [3]
**Ground-state term symbol:** ¹S₀ [3]  
**Physical state at approximately 298 K and 1 bar:** Gas [4]

## 1. Atomic and periodic context
Argon is element 18. Its valence configuration, [Ne] 3s² 3p⁶, provides the first-order framework for understanding bonding, oxidation-state preferences, and periodic trends. Group trends are guides rather than substitutes for compound-specific thermodynamic and spectroscopic evidence.

## 2. Isotopic and nuclear context
Argon has several stable isotopes, principally ⁴⁰Ar, with ³⁶Ar and ³⁸Ar also present. Radiogenic ⁴⁰Ar produced by ⁴⁰K decay underpins potassium–argon and argon–argon geochronology. The standard atomic weight is not a nuclide mass and must not be used as a substitute for isotope-specific nuclear data. A complete nuclear appendix will report nuclide masses, natural abundances, decay modes, and half-lives separately.

## 3. Chemical and materials significance
Argon is used for inert shielding in welding and metallurgy, semiconductor processing, incandescent and specialty lighting, and scientific instrumentation. [4]

## 4. Data quality and interpretation
The standard atomic weight is taken from the CIAAW 2024 table. An interval denotes the range expected for normal terrestrial materials; parenthetical digits indicate uncertainty in the final quoted digits. The electron configuration is the conventional ground-state configuration. Physical state is stated at approximately 298 K and 1 bar.
