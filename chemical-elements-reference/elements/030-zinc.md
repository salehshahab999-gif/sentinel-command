# Element 030: Zinc

**Symbol:** Zn  
**Atomic number:** 30  
**Group:** 12  
**Period:** 4  
**Block:** d-block  
**Classification:** Group 12 metal (d-block; classification terminology varies among references)  
**Standard atomic weight (CIAAW 2024):** 65.38(2)  
**Ground-state electron configuration:** [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² [3]  
**Ground-state term symbol:** ¹S₀ [3]
**Physical state at approximately 298 K and 1 bar:** Solid [4]

## 1. Atomic and periodic context
Zinc is a fourth-period d-block element. Its ground-state electron configuration is [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s². Chemical behaviour cannot be inferred from the configuration alone: oxidation state, ligand field, coordination geometry, phase, and bonding environment all influence observed properties. For group 12 zinc, the filled d subshell gives chemistry that differs from the more conventionally redox-active transition metals.

## 2. Isotopic and nuclear context
Naturally occurring zinc has five stable isotopes: ⁶⁴Zn, ⁶⁶Zn, ⁶⁷Zn, ⁶⁸Zn, and ⁷⁰Zn. CIAAW lists amount fractions of 0.4917(75), 0.2773(98), 0.0404(16), 0.1845(63), and 0.0061(10), respectively. The CIAAW 2024 standard atomic weight is 65.38(2) with footnote r, which flags variation in natural isotopic composition among normal materials; it is not printed as a bracketed interval.

## 3. Physical properties
At approximately 298 K and 1 bar, zinc is a bluish-silver solid. RSC gives a reference melting point of 692.677 K, boiling point of 1180 K, and density of 7.134 g cm⁻³. Zinc is comparatively brittle at room temperature but becomes more ductile over a moderate elevated-temperature range; phase and temperature therefore matter in mechanical descriptions.

## 4. Chemical behaviour and bonding
Zinc overwhelmingly forms the +2 oxidation state in ordinary compounds, corresponding to a filled 3d shell in Zn²⁺. It is not a typical redox-active transition metal in routine aqueous chemistry. Zinc oxide and zinc sulfide are technologically important, while zinc salts and organozinc reagents have distinct coordination and synthetic chemistry.

## 5. Materials science and applications
The largest application is galvanizing steel to inhibit corrosion. Zinc is also used in die-cast alloys, brass, sacrificial anodes, and battery chemistries. Zinc oxide is important in rubber, ceramics, pigments, and functional materials. Corrosion protection involves both barrier behaviour and sacrificial electrochemical action.

## 6. Toxicology and handling
Zinc is an essential nutrient, but excessive intake can cause adverse effects and disrupt copper homeostasis. Inhalation of freshly generated zinc-oxide fumes can cause metal-fume fever. Environmental assessments should distinguish dissolved zinc ions, particulate forms, and compound-specific bioavailability.
