# Element 37: Rubidium

**Symbol:** Rb  
**Atomic number:** 37  
**Group / period / block:** 1 / 5 / s  
**CIAAW standard atomic weight:** 85.4678(3)  
**Neutral ground-state configuration:** [Kr] 5s  
**Ground-state term:** ²S₁/₂  
**First ionization energy:** 4.1771 eV (PDG 2025 review of NIST data)  
**Naturally occurring isotopes:** ⁸⁵Rb and ⁸⁷Rb  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; isotope-geochronology and precision-frequency datasets require additional audit.

## 1. Atomic and electronic structure
Rubidium is an alkali metal with one 5s valence electron. Its neutral ground configuration is [Kr]5s and ground term ²S₁/₂. The dominant oxidation state is +1, producing Rb⁺. Rubidium is highly electropositive and reacts readily with water and oxygen. Its atomic transition structure is important in laser cooling, magneto-optical trapping and atomic frequency standards.

## 2. Isotopic composition and nuclear properties
CIAAW representative amount fractions are ⁸⁵Rb 0.7217(2) and ⁸⁷Rb 0.2783(2), with standard atomic weight 85.4678(3). ⁸⁵Rb is stable. ⁸⁷Rb is a long-lived beta-minus emitter with a half-life of approximately 4.923×10¹⁰ years and decays to stable ⁸⁷Sr. The ⁸⁷Rb–⁸⁷Sr isotope system is widely used in geochronology and isotope geochemistry. The decay constant and uncertainty should be taken from current evaluated nuclear data and standard geochronological references.

## 3. Physical and thermophysical properties
At 20 °C and 1 bar rubidium is a soft, silvery metal that melts close to room temperature. The RSC compilation lists melting point 39.31 °C, boiling point 688 °C and density 1.53 g cm⁻³. Rubidium oxidises rapidly in air and reacts vigorously with water; sample surface condition and purity strongly affect material handling and measurement. Physical values should specify temperature, pressure and sample history.

## 4. Chemical behaviour
Rubidium forms Rb⁺ in ordinary compounds. It reacts with water to form rubidium hydroxide and hydrogen, and forms halides, oxides and other salts. Rb⁺ is a large monovalent cation with weak hydration compared with smaller alkali ions. Solubility and complex formation depend on counterion, solvent and ionic strength.

## 5. Materials science and applications
Rubidium is used in atomic clocks, frequency references, research lasers and specialised vacuum or photoelectric devices. Rubidium frequency standards use defined hyperfine transitions of ⁸⁷Rb or ⁸⁵Rb; the transition, buffer gas and cell design are part of the instrument specification. The ⁸⁷Rb–⁸⁷Sr isotope system is important in geochronology. Commercial handling is limited by reactivity and the need for sealed systems.

## 6. Safety and environmental considerations
Rubidium metal is highly reactive and can ignite or react violently with water. It must be stored under suitable inert conditions and handled with procedures designed for reactive alkali metals. Rubidium salts generally have a different hazard profile from the metal. Radioactive ⁸⁷Rb has a very long half-life and low specific activity, but nuclear-data and contamination context still matter in specialised work.

## 7. Discovery and nomenclature
Rubidium was discovered in 1861 by Robert Bunsen and Gustav Kirchhoff using flame spectroscopy. Its name derives from Latin *rubidus*, meaning deep red, referring to the spectral lines used in its identification.
