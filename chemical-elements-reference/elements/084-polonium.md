# Element 84: Polonium

**Symbol:** Po  
**Atomic number:** 84  
**Group / period / block:** 16 / 6 / p  
**Standard atomic weight:** none (no stable isotopes); representative mass number [209]  
**Neutral ground-state configuration:** [Xe] 4f¹⁴ 5d¹⁰ 6s² 6p⁴  
**Ground-state term:** ³P₂  
**First ionization energy:** 8.418070 eV (NIST ASD; neutral Po I)  
**Representative naturally occurring radionuclide:** ²¹⁰Po  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; physical data are largely compiled or estimated and isotope-specific nuclear data are essential.

## 1. Atomic and electronic structure
Polonium is a highly radioactive chalcogen. Its neutral ground configuration is [Xe]4f¹⁴5d¹⁰6s²6p⁴ and the tabulated ground term is ³P₂. Polonium chemistry is less well characterised than that of stable chalcogens because macroscopic samples are difficult to obtain. Oxidation states +2 and +4 are important, with +6 represented in selected compounds. Relativistic effects and increasing metallic character down group 16 influence bonding and speciation.

## 2. Nuclear properties and isotopes
Polonium has no stable isotopes and therefore no standard atomic weight. CIAAW uses [209] as a conventional representative mass number for element tables, not as an atomic weight. ²¹⁰Po is an alpha-emitting radionuclide with a half-life of about 138.4 days and is present in trace amounts in natural uranium decay chains. Other isotopes, including ²⁰⁹Po and ²⁰⁸Po, have different half-lives and decay properties. Every nuclear claim must specify the isotope and be supported by ENSDF/NuDat or an equivalent evaluated nuclear-data source.

## 3. Physical and condensed-matter properties
Polonium is a silvery-grey radioactive solid. The RSC compilation lists an approximate melting point of 254 °C, boiling point of 962 °C and density of 9.20 g cm⁻³. These values are compiled reference estimates; direct bulk measurements are limited by radioactivity, scarcity and radiolytic heating. Polonium has multiple allotropes, commonly described as alpha (simple cubic) and beta (rhombohedral) forms, with conditions and sample history affecting phase assignment. Treat tabulated physical properties as provisional rather than universal high-precision constants.

## 4. Chemical behaviour
Polonium forms oxides, halides, chalcogenides and polonide compounds. Its solution chemistry includes multiple oxidation states and is strongly influenced by redox conditions and complexing ligands. Because isotopes decay continuously, chemical experiments can involve radiolysis and daughter products. Speciation data should identify isotope, oxidation state, solvent, pH and carrier concentration.

## 5. Applications and research relevance
Polonium-210 has been used as an alpha-particle source in specialised static-elimination devices and research applications. Historically, Po-210 was used in compact heat sources, including on early lunar rovers; this use was limited by its relatively short half-life. Any application requires licensed radiological controls and careful containment. Polonium is not a routine industrial commodity.

## 6. Radiological safety and toxicology
Polonium-210 is both highly radioactive and chemically toxic. Alpha particles have low penetration through intact outer skin, but internal contamination by inhalation, ingestion or wounds can deliver a severe radiation dose to tissues. The IAEA notes that internal uptake is the central hazard for Po-210. Handling requires specialist radiological facilities, contamination control, dosimetry, secure containment and regulated waste management. It must never be treated as an ordinary laboratory metal.

## 7. Discovery and nomenclature
Polonium was discovered by Marie and Pierre Curie in 1898 during investigations of pitchblende radioactivity. Marie Curie named it after Poland, her homeland. Its discovery was an early milestone in radioactivity research and the identification of naturally occurring radioactive elements.

## 8. Evidence quality and unresolved work
- **Atomic weight status:** no standard atomic weight; [209] is a representative mass number only.
- **Atomic structure:** NIST/PDG tabulations provide compact ground-state information; complete ASD level data remain to be audited.
- **Physical properties:** RSC values are compiled and should be labelled approximate because direct measurement is difficult.
- **Pending:** isotope-by-isotope ENSDF/NuDat decay schemes and emission probabilities; verified phase boundaries and thermophysical properties; compound-specific speciation and radiotoxicity data.
