# Element 45: Rhodium

**Symbol:** Rh  
**Atomic number:** 45  
**Group:** 9  
**Period:** 5  
**Block:** d-block  
**Standard atomic weight:** 102.905 49(2)  
**Ground-state electron configuration:** [Kr] 4d⁸ 5s¹  
**Ground-state term:** ⁴F₉/₂  
**First ionization energy:** 7.4589 eV  
**State at 20 °C and 1 bar:** Solid

## 1. Atomic structure and quantum properties
Rhodium is a period-5 d-block element. Its tabulated ground-state electron configuration is [Kr] 4d⁸ 5s¹. The ground-state term and first ionization energy are taken from the NIST-reviewed periodic-table compilation. These summary values do not replace a level-resolved treatment: a research-grade spectral appendix should query NIST ASD for level energies, J values, transition probabilities, wavelengths, isotope shifts, and uncertainties.

## 2. Nuclear properties and isotopic composition
Natural rhodium is monoisotopic: ¹⁰³Rh has representative isotopic amount fraction 1. CIAAW standard atomic weight is 102.905 49(2). The element's naturally occurring isotopic composition is therefore not a multi-isotope mixture in ordinary terrestrial samples.

**Data status:** CIAAW provides the current standard atomic-weight and natural-isotope framework for stable elements. For technetium, no standard atomic weight exists because there are no stable isotopes. For radioactive nuclides, the values stated here are selected evaluated/compiled data, not a complete decay evaluation. Full ENSDF extraction is required for adopted levels, branching fractions, beta spectra, gamma intensities, internal conversion, Q values, and uncertainties.

## 3. Physical properties and phase behaviour
- **Melting point:** 2236 K (1963 °C)
- **Boiling point:** 3968 K (3695 °C)
- **Density:** 12.4 g cm⁻³
- **Ambient crystal structure:** fcc, space group Fm-3m (No. 225) under ambient conditions

These are reference values, not a critical thermodynamic compilation. Density and transition temperatures depend on purity, porosity, isotopic composition where relevant, pressure, and measurement convention. The hcp/fcc assignments describe the ambient elemental metal, not all compounds or metastable thin-film phases.

## 4. Chemical thermodynamics, oxidation states, and coordination chemistry
Rhodium most commonly forms compounds in +1 and +3 oxidation states, with other states accessible in specialized coordination environments. Rh(I) d⁸ complexes are often square-planar and are central to homogeneous catalytic cycles; Rh(III) complexes commonly show octahedral coordination. Ligand identity, oxidation state, and reaction conditions govern catalytic selectivity and mechanism.

A final thermodynamic dataset should provide standard-state formation enthalpies, Gibbs energies, entropies, heat capacities, redox potentials, aqueous speciation constants, and temperature/pH dependence with traceable primary or critically evaluated sources. No unverified numerical thermodynamic constants are inserted in this draft.

## 5. Materials science and catalytic function
Rhodium is a key component of three-way automotive catalysts, particularly for reduction of nitrogen oxides under exhaust conditions. It is also used in chemical-process catalysts, high-temperature and corrosion-resistant components, electrical contacts, reflective coatings, and platinum-rhodium alloys used in high-temperature thermocouples and glass-industry equipment.

For engineering use, catalytic activity must be reported with reaction conditions, support composition, loading, particle-size distribution, space velocity, feed composition, conversion, selectivity, and deactivation history. Bulk elemental properties alone cannot predict supported-catalyst performance.

## 6. Toxicology, radiological safety, and handling
Bulk rhodium is relatively corrosion resistant, but dusts, soluble salts, and catalyst-processing residues require substance-specific industrial hygiene controls. Rhodium salts can cause sensitization or other adverse effects; hazard statements for soluble compounds should not be transferred uncritically to bulk metal.
