# Element 005: Boron

**Symbol:** B  
**Atomic number:** 5  
**Group:** 13  
**Period:** 2  
**Block:** p-block [4]
**Classification:** Metalloid  
**Standard atomic weight (CIAAW 2024):** [10.806, 10.821] [1]
**Ground-state electron configuration:** [He] 2s² 2p¹   [3]
**Physical state at approximately 298 K and 1 bar:** Solid [4]

## 1. Atomic and periodic context
Boron is element 5 in the periodic table. Its ground-state configuration, [He] 2s² 2p¹, provides the starting point for interpreting its valence structure, bonding patterns, and characteristic chemistry. Group placement is a useful organizing framework, but observed chemistry also depends on molecular environment, oxidation state, and thermodynamic conditions.

## 2. Isotopic and nuclear context
The principal stable isotopes are ¹⁰B and ¹¹B. Their different neutron-capture behavior makes isotopic composition important in nuclear applications. A complete isotope dossier requires nuclide-by-nuclide evaluation of atomic masses, natural abundances, decay modes, and half-lives. Values are not inferred from the standard atomic weight.

## 3. Chemical and materials significance
Borosilicate glass, boron carbide, neutron-absorbing materials, semiconductor doping, and boron-containing high-strength compounds.

## 4. Data quality and interpretation
The standard atomic weight is reproduced from the CIAAW 2024 table. Bracketed values are intervals reflecting the accepted range for normal terrestrial materials, not a symmetric measurement uncertainty. Parenthetical digits denote uncertainty in the final quoted digits. The electron configuration is the conventional ground-state configuration; exceptional configurations and spectroscopic term data require element-specific evaluation.
