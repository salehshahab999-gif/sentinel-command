# Element 117: Tennessine

**Symbol:** Ts  
**Atomic number:** 117  
**Group / period / block:** 17 / 7 / p  
**Standard atomic weight:** none; representative mass number [294]  
**Predicted ground-state configuration:** [Rn] 5f¹⁴ 6d¹⁰ 7s² 7p⁵  
**Ground-state term:** ²P°₃/₂  
**First ionization energy:** not experimentally established; theoretical values are model-dependent  
**Representative isotope:** ²⁹⁴Ts  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; physical properties and chemical behaviour are not established by bulk measurement.

## 1. Atomic and electronic structure
Tennessine is a transactinoid p-block element in group 17, below astatine. Its predicted neutral ground configuration is [Rn]5f¹⁴6d¹⁰7s²7p⁵, with ground term ²P°₃/₂. Relativistic effects are expected to produce stronger metallic character than in lighter halogens and to alter oxidation-state stability. Halogen-like chemistry is a periodic prediction; no robust experimental compound chemistry has been established.

## 2. Nuclear properties and isotopes
Tennessine has no stable isotopes and no standard atomic weight. [294] is a representative mass number. CIAAW/NUBASE2020 list ²⁹⁴Ts with a half-life of 70(30) milliseconds. ²⁹²Ts has also been reported, but its nuclear properties are less well constrained in current summary tables. Decay modes, branching and daughter-chain assignments require current ENSDF/NuDat evaluation.

## 3. Physical properties and measurement limits
No macroscopic sample of tennessine has been produced. RSC lists melting point, boiling point and density as unknown. Its solid state on some periodic-table displays is a prediction rather than a direct experiment. Bulk phase, conductivity, elastic behaviour and thermal properties remain unknown.

## 4. Chemical behaviour
Tennessine is expected to be the heaviest halogen, but relativistic effects may make it more metallic and less strongly electronegative than iodine or astatine. Formal oxidation states such as −1, +1, +3, +5 and +7 are possible predictions by group analogy, but these have not been experimentally confirmed for tennessine. The distinction between predicted periodic behaviour and observed chemistry is especially important for this element.

## 5. Research relevance
Tennessine has no commercial or technological use. Its importance is in nuclear structure, superheavy-element synthesis and tests of relativistic chemistry in the p block. Research depends on accelerator production and detection of extremely short-lived decay chains.

## 6. Radiological safety
All tennessine isotopes are radioactive and very short-lived. Work is restricted to specialised accelerator laboratories under licensed radiological controls. There are no bulk sample handling or occupational exposure studies.

## 7. Discovery and nomenclature
Tennessine was produced in experiments involving the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research in Dubna, Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, Oak Ridge National Laboratory, Vanderbilt University and the University of Tennessee. IUPAC approved the name in November 2016. It recognises the contribution of the Tennessee region to superheavy-element research.

## 8. Evidence quality and unresolved work
- **Atomic configuration and ground term:** RSC/PDG compiled predictions; direct atomic spectrum is unavailable.
- **Nuclear data:** CIAAW/NUBASE2020 lists ²⁹⁴Ts at 70(30) ms; current ENSDF is required for detailed decay information.
- **Physical properties:** no direct measurements; melting point, boiling point and density are unknown.
- **Pending:** atom-at-a-time chemical study; electron affinity and ionisation energy evaluation; improved isotope and decay-chain measurements; relativistic calculations of molecular bonding.
