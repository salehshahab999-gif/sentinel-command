# Element 56: Barium

**Symbol:** Ba  
**Atomic number:** 56  
**Group / period / block:** 2 / 6 / s  
**Standard atomic weight:** 137.327(7)  
**Ground-state configuration:** [Xe] 6s²  
**Ground-state term:** ¹S₀  
**First ionization energy:** 5.2116646 eV (NIST evaluated value)  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; isotope-specific nuclear and high-pressure phase data require further audit.

## 1. Atomic structure
Barium is an alkaline-earth element. Its two 6s valence electrons give a closed-shell ¹S₀ neutral ground state. The first ionization energy is 5.2116646 eV; the second ionization energy is substantially higher and must be treated separately when quantifying Ba²⁺ formation. The chemically dominant oxidation state is +2.

## 2. Isotopic and nuclear properties
Natural barium is a mixture of several stable isotopes, including ¹³⁰Ba, ¹³²Ba, ¹³⁴Ba, ¹³⁵Ba, ¹³⁶Ba and ¹³⁸Ba; ¹³⁸Ba is the most abundant. ¹³⁰Ba and ¹³²Ba are extremely long-lived double-electron-capture (ECEC) nuclides, not ordinary β⁻β⁻ emitters. A geochemical result supports 2νECEC in ¹³⁰Ba; the ¹³²Ba evidence is more tentative and should be reported with its experimental qualification rather than as an equally established observation. Isotopic abundance values and uncertainties should be transcribed from the current CIAAW isotopic-composition table. ¹⁴⁰Ba is a short-lived fission product and must be distinguished from natural stable-isotope composition.

## 3. Solid-state physics
At ambient conditions metallic barium has a body-centered cubic structure. Its phase behavior changes under pressure, so crystal structure and density must be tied to stated thermodynamic conditions. Barium is a relatively soft, reactive metal; oxidation and surface contamination complicate measurements of intrinsic properties.

## 4. Inorganic chemistry
Ba²⁺ forms largely ionic compounds. Barium oxide and hydroxide are strongly basic; barium carbonate and sulfate are sparingly soluble, with BaSO₄'s low solubility central to analytical gravimetry and radiological contrast applications. Soluble barium salts can be toxic because Ba²⁺ interferes with potassium-channel physiology. BaSO₄ used as a medical contrast agent is poorly soluble and should not be conflated toxicologically with soluble barium salts.

## 5. Materials and applications
Barium compounds are used in specialty glass, ceramics, ferrites, pyrotechnic green colorants and drilling fluids. BaTiO₃ is a technologically important ferroelectric and high-permittivity ceramic; its dielectric response depends strongly on temperature, microstructure, defects and frequency. The properties of BaTiO₃ are compound properties, not elemental-barium properties.
