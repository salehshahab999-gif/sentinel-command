# Element 109: Meitnerium

**Symbol:** Mt  
**Atomic number:** 109  
**Group / period / block:** 9 / 7 / d  
**Standard atomic weight:** none; representative mass number [277]  
**Predicted ground-state configuration:** [Rn] 5f¹⁴ 6d⁷ 7s²  
**Compiled ground-state term:** ⁴F₉/₂  
**First ionization energy:** not experimentally established; theoretical estimates require model-specific citation  
**Representative isotopes:** ²⁷⁷Mt and ²⁷⁸Mt  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; no chemical experiments or bulk physical measurements have been established.

## 1. Atomic and electronic structure
Meitnerium is a transactinoid in group 9, below cobalt, rhodium and iridium. Its electronic configuration is predicted to involve [Rn]5f¹⁴6d⁷7s², with a compiled ground term ⁴F₉/₂. Relativistic effects are expected to influence orbital energies, spin-orbit coupling and chemical bonding. Since spectroscopic data are extremely limited, the configuration and term should be treated as compiled or predicted assignments rather than as a fully measured atomic spectrum.

## 2. Nuclear properties and isotopes
Meitnerium has no stable isotopes and no standard atomic weight. [277] is a representative mass number. CIAAW/NUBASE2020 list ²⁷⁷Mt with half-life 9(6) seconds and ²⁷⁸Mt with 6(3) seconds. These values have substantial uncertainties and are not readily distinguishable in longevity. Nuclear identification relies on correlated decay chains. Current ENSDF/NuDat evaluations should be used for exact decay branches and daughter assignments.

## 3. Physical properties and measurement limits
No macroscopic sample of meitnerium has ever been produced. Density, melting point, boiling point, crystal structure, elastic constants and transport properties are unknown experimentally. Theoretical calculations may estimate these quantities, but values vary with relativistic and correlation methods. They should not be presented as measured constants in a professional reference.

## 4. Chemical behaviour
No chemical experiments have been performed on meitnerium because of the short half-lives and atom-production limits. Its chemistry is expected to resemble group 9, but this remains a periodic prediction, not a demonstrated experimental fact. Any projected oxidation state or volatility should be labelled theoretical.

## 5. Research relevance
Meitnerium has no commercial or technological use. It is of interest in nuclear structure, superheavy-element synthesis and relativistic atomic theory. Experimental work is limited to production of a few atoms and identification through their radioactive decay chains.

## 6. Radiological safety
Meitnerium isotopes are radioactive and extremely short-lived. Any experimental work is restricted to specialised accelerator facilities with licensed radiological controls, containment, contamination monitoring and isotope-specific dosimetry. No ordinary laboratory or bulk-material handling properties are available.

## 7. Discovery and nomenclature
Meitnerium was first reported in 1982 by a team at GSI in Darmstadt, Germany. It was named after physicist Lise Meitner, who made major contributions to nuclear physics and the understanding of nuclear fission. IUPAC adopted the name in 1997.
