# Element 68: Erbium

**Symbol:** Er  
**Atomic number:** 68  
**Group / period / block:** lanthanoid / 6 / f  
**CIAAW standard atomic weight:** 167.259(3)  
**Neutral ground-state configuration:** [Xe] 4f¹² 6s²  
**Ground-state term:** ³H₆  
**First ionization energy:** 6.1077 eV (NIST Atomic Data Handbook)  
**Naturally occurring isotopes:** ¹⁶²Er, ¹⁶⁴Er, ¹⁶⁶Er, ¹⁶⁷Er, ¹⁶⁸Er and ¹⁷⁰Er  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; full isotope, spectroscopic and photonic-material data audit remains open.

## 1. Atomic and electronic structure
Erbium has neutral ground configuration [Xe]4f¹²6s² and ground term ³H₆. The 4f electrons are shielded by outer 5s and 5p shells, producing narrow optical transitions and strong spin-orbit and crystal-field effects. Er³⁺ is the dominant oxidation state in common compounds and has a 4f¹¹ configuration. Er²⁺ is known in selected compounds but is substantially less common. Spectroscopic values for Er³⁺ are host-dependent because the surrounding crystal or glass splits the free-ion multiplets.

## 2. Isotopic composition and nuclear properties
CIAAW representative amount fractions are: ¹⁶²Er 0.00139(5), ¹⁶⁴Er 0.01601(3), ¹⁶⁶Er 0.33503(36), ¹⁶⁷Er 0.22869(9), ¹⁶⁸Er 0.26978(18) and ¹⁷⁰Er 0.14910(36). CIAAW gives 167.259(3) as the standard atomic weight. ¹⁶⁷Er has nuclear spin I = 7/2, while the other listed even-mass isotopes have spin zero in their ground states. Some naturally occurring heavy nuclides have extremely long radioactive lifetimes; their exact classification, decay modes and uncertainties should be verified against evaluated nuclear databases rather than inferred from terrestrial abundance.

## 3. Physical and condensed-matter properties
At ambient conditions erbium is a solid silvery metal with a hexagonal close-packed structure. The RSC compilation lists melting point 1529 °C, boiling point 2868 °C and density 9.07 g cm⁻³. The metal slowly tarnishes in air and reacts with water. Magnetic ordering and optical properties are strongly temperature- and phase-dependent. For precision datasets, density, heat capacity, elastic constants and magnetic susceptibility should be tied to sample purity and measurement temperature.

## 4. Chemical behaviour
Erbium chemistry is dominated by +3. Er₂O₃ is a common sesquioxide; erbium halides and oxygen-donor coordination complexes are also well characterised. Er³⁺ is a hard Lewis acid with high coordination numbers. In aqueous systems, hydrolysis and ligand complexation depend on pH and ionic strength. The chemical similarity between Er³⁺ and neighbouring lanthanoid ions is exploited in solvent extraction and ion-exchange separation.

## 5. Materials science and photonic applications
Erbium is particularly important in photonics. Er³⁺-doped silica fibre provides optical amplification near 1.55 μm, a key low-loss telecommunications wavelength. Erbium-doped glasses and crystals are also used in lasers and eye-protection or infrared-absorbing glass. The pink coloration of some erbium-containing glasses is associated with characteristic optical absorption bands. Amplifier gain, bandwidth, lifetime and thermal loading depend on dopant concentration, glass composition, co-dopants and waveguide geometry; these are properties of the engineered host material.

## 6. Safety and environmental considerations
Erbium has no known biological role. RSC characterises its toxicity as low, but risk assessment still depends on compound form, particle size, dose and exposure route. Metallic dust and processing powders require standard industrial hygiene controls. Rare-earth separation and optical-fibre manufacturing waste should be assessed for co-contaminants and process reagents, not only for erbium content.

## 7. Discovery and nomenclature
Erbium was identified in 1843 by Carl Gustaf Mosander in material associated with yttria. Its name, like terbium and ytterbium, derives from the Swedish village of Ytterby, where rare-earth-bearing minerals were first studied. Nineteenth-century nomenclature changed as the initially impure rare-earth fractions were progressively separated.
