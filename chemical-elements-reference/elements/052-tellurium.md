# Element 52: Tellurium

**Symbol:** Te  
**Atomic number:** 52  
**Group / period / block:** 16 / 5 / p  
**Standard atomic weight:** 127.60(3)  
**Ground-state electron configuration:** [Kr] 4d¹⁰ 5s² 5p⁴  
**Ground-state term:** ³P₂  
**First ionization energy:** 9.0098 eV  
**Ambient structure:** trigonal crystalline tellurium (helical-chain structure; space group P3₁21 or enantiomorphic P3₂21)  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; numerical data require condition-specific critical review.

## 1. Atomic and quantum properties
Tellurium is a group-16 chalcogen. Its neutral ground configuration is 5s²5p⁴ outside the [Kr]4d¹⁰ core, with ground term ³P₂. Spin-orbit coupling is significant in this heavy p-block atom. A publication-grade spectroscopic appendix requires NIST ASD level-by-level extraction, including fine-structure splittings, transition probabilities, isotope shifts and uncertainty metadata.

## 2. Isotopic composition and nuclear data
CIAAW's representative 2024 isotopic composition lists eight naturally occurring tellurium nuclides: **¹²⁰Te 0.0009(1), ¹²²Te 0.0255(12), ¹²³Te 0.0089(3), ¹²⁴Te 0.0474(14), ¹²⁵Te 0.0707(15), ¹²⁶Te 0.1884(25), ¹²⁸Te 0.3174(8), and ¹³⁰Te 0.3408(62)**. CIAAW gives 127.60(3) as the standard atomic weight. The natural isotope set includes nuclides whose nuclear stability requires care: notably ¹²³Te and ¹²⁸Te are radioactive with extremely long half-lives, so “naturally occurring” must not be equated with “strictly stable.” Exact half-lives, decay modes and evaluated uncertainties must be taken from current ENSDF/NUBASE evaluations.

## 3. Solid-state physics and thermodynamics
Crystalline tellurium consists of helical covalent chains packed in a trigonal lattice. Its anisotropic bonding produces direction-dependent electrical and thermal properties. Tellurium is a narrow-gap semiconductor/semimetal depending on temperature, pressure and structural state; any quoted band gap must specify method and conditions. Frequently tabulated melting and boiling points are approximately 722.7 K and 1261 K, respectively, but reference conditions and uncertainty should accompany final values.

## 4. Chemical behavior
Common oxidation states include −2, +2, +4 and +6, with +4 and +6 important in oxo-chemistry. Tellurium forms tellurides, tellurites and tellurates; speciation depends on redox potential, pH and ligand environment. The distinction between tellurite Te(IV) and tellurate Te(VI) is essential in aqueous geochemistry and environmental chemistry. Organotellurium compounds require separate treatment because bonding, reactivity and toxicity vary widely.

## 5. Materials science and applications
Tellurium is used in thermoelectric materials, phase-change alloys, selected photovoltaic compounds and alloy modification. Its role in CdTe photovoltaics is as a constituent of the semiconductor compound, not as elemental Te alone. Thermoelectric performance depends on carrier concentration, defects, texture and phonon scattering; a meaningful comparison must state composition and measurement temperature.
