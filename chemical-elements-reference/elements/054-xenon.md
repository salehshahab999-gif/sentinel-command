# Element 54: Xenon

**Symbol:** Xe  
**Atomic number:** 54  
**Group / period / block:** 18 / 5 / p  
**Standard atomic weight:** 131.293(6)  
**Ground-state electron configuration:** [Kr] 4d¹⁰ 5s² 5p⁶  
**Ground-state term:** ¹S₀  
**First ionization energy:** 12.1298 eV  
**Ambient structure:** face-centered cubic (fcc) solid at cryogenic conditions; gas at 20 °C and 1 bar  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; full spectroscopy and nuclear-data audit remains open.

## 1. Atomic and quantum structure
Xenon is a closed-shell noble gas. Its ground configuration terminates in 5p⁶ and its ground term is ¹S₀. The first ionization energy, 12.1298 eV, is relatively high but lower than that of lighter noble gases such as neon and helium. Excited xenon has a rich spectrum and is central to excimer and discharge physics; a full appendix must retrieve level classifications, wavelengths, transition probabilities and uncertainties from NIST ASD.

## 2. Isotopic composition
CIAAW gives standard atomic weight 131.293(6). Its representative 2024 natural isotopic composition includes **¹²⁴Xe 0.00095(5), ¹²⁶Xe 0.00089(3), ¹²⁸Xe 0.01910(13), ¹²⁹Xe 0.26401(138), ¹³⁰Xe 0.04071(22), ¹³¹Xe 0.21232(51), ¹³²Xe 0.26909(55), ¹³⁴Xe 0.10436(35), and ¹³⁶Xe 0.08857(72)**. Xenon isotope patterns vary substantially among geological reservoirs and can encode radiogenic, fissiogenic and atmospheric histories. ¹²⁴Xe and ¹³⁶Xe are double-beta-decay nuclides with extremely long half-lives; natural occurrence must not be mistaken for strict nuclear stability.

## 3. Physical properties and cryogenic phase behavior
At ambient conditions xenon is a monatomic gas. On cooling it forms a molecularly nonbonded, close-packed solid with an fcc structure; the solid-state cohesion is dominated by dispersion interactions. Its critical behavior and phase boundaries are pressure-dependent. Any density, heat capacity, sound speed or dielectric value must specify phase, temperature and pressure. A single “density of xenon” without conditions is not a useful reference datum.

## 4. Chemical reactivity and compounds
Although chemically inert under many ordinary conditions, xenon forms stable compounds, especially with highly electronegative elements. Important classes include xenon fluorides (XeF₂, XeF₄ and XeF₆) and oxofluorides/oxo-species derived from them. Oxidation states +2, +4 and +6 are represented in xenon fluorides. Their structures and bonding require molecular-orbital descriptions; the obsolete simple d-orbital-expansion explanation should not be presented as a modern account. XeF₂ is a linear molecule, XeF₄ is square planar, and XeF₆ has a fluxional, distorted structure.

## 5. Nuclear, medical and industrial relevance
¹³³Xe is a radioactive noble-gas isotope used in specialized medical imaging and pulmonary ventilation studies; ¹³⁵Xe is a strong neutron absorber important in reactor kinetics and fission-product behavior. Their half-lives, gamma emissions, branching fractions and neutron-capture cross sections are isotope-specific and must be drawn from evaluated nuclear data. Stable xenon isotopes are also used in geochemistry, isotope tracing and noble-gas mass spectrometry.
