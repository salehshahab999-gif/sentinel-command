# Element 114: Flerovium

**Symbol:** Fl  
**Atomic number:** 114  
**Group / period / block:** 14 / 7 / p  
**Standard atomic weight:** none; representative mass number [289]  
**Predicted ground-state configuration:** [Rn] 5f¹⁴ 6d¹⁰ 7s² 7p²  
**Ground-state term:** ³P₀  
**First ionization energy:** not experimentally established; theoretical estimates are model-dependent  
**Representative isotopes:** ²⁸⁹Fl and ²⁹⁰Fl  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; atom-at-a-time chemistry is preliminary and bulk physical properties are unknown.

## 1. Atomic and electronic structure
Flerovium is a transactinoid p-block element in group 14, below lead. Its predicted ground configuration is [Rn]5f¹⁴6d¹⁰7s²7p², with ground term ³P₀. Relativistic stabilisation of the 7s² pair may affect oxidation states and bonding, potentially giving flerovium more volatile or less reactive behaviour than a simple lead analogue. These predictions need to be distinguished from experimental observations.

## 2. Nuclear properties and isotopes
Flerovium has no stable isotopes and no standard atomic weight. [289] is a representative mass number. CIAAW/NUBASE2020 list ²⁸⁹Fl with half-life 2.1(0.6) seconds and ²⁹⁰Fl with 80(60) seconds. The much longer-lived ²⁹⁰Fl value has large uncertainty and should be checked against current evaluated nuclear data. Decay branches and daughter assignments require ENSDF/NuDat.

## 3. Physical properties and measurement limits
No macroscopic sample of flerovium has been produced. RSC lists melting point, boiling point and density as unknown. Its solid or liquid state at room temperature cannot be directly determined. Predictions of volatility and possible noble-gas-like behaviour are theoretical or based on very small atom-at-a-time datasets.

## 4. Chemical behaviour
Flerovium is expected to resemble group-14 elements, but strong relativistic effects may stabilise the 7s² pair and reduce metallic reactivity. Atom-at-a-time adsorption experiments on gold surfaces have suggested relatively weak interaction and high volatility compared with a simple lead-like prediction. The evidence is preliminary and is based on very few atoms, so it should not be treated as a definitive bulk chemical classification. Common oxidation states are not experimentally established.

## 5. Research relevance
Flerovium has no commercial or technological application. It is important for studying relativistic chemistry, the inert-pair effect in the heaviest p-block elements and the limits of periodic trends. Research requires accelerator production, rapid gas transport and decay-chain identification.

## 6. Radiological safety
Flerovium isotopes are radioactive and very short-lived. Work is limited to specialised nuclear-research facilities with licensed containment, target and separator controls, contamination monitoring and isotope-specific dosimetry. No bulk material handling data exist.

## 7. Discovery and nomenclature
Flerovium was first synthesised in 1998 by a collaboration involving the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research in Dubna and Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory. IUPAC approved the name in 2012, honouring the Flerov Laboratory of Nuclear Reactions at Dubna, named after physicist Georgy Flerov.

## 8. Evidence quality and unresolved work
- **Atomic structure:** PDG/NIST compilations provide the predicted configuration and term; reliable experimental ionization data are unavailable.
- **Nuclear data:** CIAAW/NUBASE2020 half-lives; current ENSDF/NuDat required for detailed decay branches.
- **Physical properties:** RSC lists melting point, boiling point and density as unknown.
- **Pending:** repeatable adsorption chemistry and volatility data; oxidation-state determination; improved ²⁹⁰Fl half-life; model-specific electronic and phase predictions.
