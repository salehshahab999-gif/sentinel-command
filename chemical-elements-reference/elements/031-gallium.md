# Element 31: Gallium

**Symbol:** Ga  
**Atomic number:** 31  
**Group:** 13  
**Period:** 4  
**Block:** p-block  
**Classification:** Post-transition metal / metalloid classification depends on convention  
**Standard atomic weight (CIAAW):** 69.723(1)  
**Ground-state electron configuration:** [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p¹  
**Physical state at approximately 298 K and 1 bar:** Solid

## 1. Atomic and periodic context
Gallium is a period-4 p-block element. Its valence-shell configuration is represented by [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p¹. The element's bonding and chemistry depend on oxidation state, coordination environment, allotrope, and phase; a single periodic classification does not capture all of these behaviours.

## 2. Isotopic and nuclear context
Natural gallium consists of ⁶⁹Ga and ⁷¹Ga. CIAAW gives representative amount fractions 0.60108(50) and 0.39892(50), respectively. The standard atomic weight is 69.723(1). CIAAW documents measurable isotopic variation in some high-purity commercial lots, which can affect precision work and even the triple-point temperature.

**Evidence status:** Natural isotope masses and representative amount fractions are attributed to CIAAW. A complete evaluated nuclear chart, decay modes, branching ratios, and half-life uncertainties have not yet been compiled for this element.

## 3. Physical properties
At 20 °C gallium is solid, but its melting point is unusually low: 302.9146 K (29.7646 °C). RSC lists a boiling point of 2502 K and density of 5.91 g cm⁻³. The liquid can supercool below its melting point; phase and thermal history therefore matter in experiments.

**Evidence status:** Reference values are drawn from the RSC periodic-table fact box. These are secondary reference values and are not yet a critical compilation of primary measurements. Phase, allotrope, pressure, purity, and uncertainty must be specified before final release.

## 4. Chemical behaviour and bonding
Gallium commonly occurs in the +3 oxidation state, with +1 chemistry also known under suitable conditions. Gallium(III) compounds include halides, oxides, and organometallic species. Gallium forms amphoteric oxide/hydroxide chemistry and is important in III–V and III–N semiconductor compounds.

## 5. Materials science and applications
Gallium arsenide (GaAs) and gallium nitride (GaN) are technologically important semiconductors used in optoelectronics, high-frequency electronics, LEDs, and power devices. Elemental gallium also forms low-melting alloys and can wet or embrittle some metals, so compatibility must be considered in materials handling.

## 6. Toxicology and handling
Gallium metal is often described as relatively low toxicity, but this must not be generalized to all gallium compounds. Compound-specific toxicology, dust exposure, and semiconductor-manufacturing chemicals require separate assessment. Avoid contact with materials known to be susceptible to liquid-metal embrittlement.
