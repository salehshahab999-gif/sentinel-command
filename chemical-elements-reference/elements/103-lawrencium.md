# Element 103: Lawrencium

**Symbol:** Lr  
**Atomic number:** 103  
**Group / period / block:** actinoid / 7 / d (classification depends on periodic-table convention)  
**Standard atomic weight:** none; representative mass number [266]  
**Neutral ground-state configuration:** [Rn] 5f¹⁴ 7s² 7p  
**Ground-state term:** ²P°₁/₂  
**First ionization energy:** 4.96 eV (PDG 2024 review of NIST data)  
**Representative isotopes:** ²⁶²Lr and ²⁶⁶Lr  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; nuclear and atom-at-a-time chemical properties are still being evaluated.

## 1. Atomic and electronic structure
Lawrencium is the final actinoid by atomic-number convention. Its neutral ground-state configuration is [Rn]5f¹⁴7s²7p, with ground term ²P°₁/₂ in the PDG review of NIST data. The 7p character of the ground state is notable and differs from a simple extrapolation of earlier actinoid electron-filling patterns. The +3 oxidation state is expected to dominate its chemistry, with possible +1 behaviour under specialised conditions requiring further confirmation.

## 2. Nuclear properties and isotopes
Lawrencium has no stable isotopes and therefore no standard atomic weight. [266] is a representative mass number, not an atomic weight. CIAAW lists ²⁶²Lr with an approximate half-life of 4 hours and ²⁶⁶Lr with a half-life of 22(14) hours. These values are evaluated with substantial uncertainty; exact decay modes, branching and daughter products should be taken from current NUBASE and ENSDF records.

## 3. Physical properties and measurement limits
No macroscopic sample of lawrencium has been produced. Density, melting point, boiling point, crystal structure, thermal conductivity and mechanical properties are not experimentally established. Any values shown in periodic-table summaries are theoretical predictions and should not be presented as measured constants. The short half-lives and atom-at-a-time production regime prevent conventional bulk-material characterisation.

## 4. Chemical behaviour
Lawrencium chemistry is studied with extremely small numbers of atoms. Available experiments and periodic trends support a predominantly trivalent actinoid character, while the unusual 7p ground-state electron raises questions about the stability of Lr(I) relative to Lu(I). Chemical experiments must distinguish direct observations from relativistic electronic-structure predictions and homolog-based inference.

## 5. Research relevance
Lawrencium has no commercial or technological use. It is important for understanding the end of the actinoid series, relativistic effects in heavy atoms and the periodic-table boundary between actinoids and transactinoids. Chemical experiments rely on fast separation, nuclear identification and single-atom detection.

## 6. Radiological safety
All lawrencium isotopes are radioactive and short-lived. Work requires specialist accelerator and radiochemical facilities, licensed controls, containment and isotope-specific dosimetry. The tiny number of atoms used does not justify ignoring contamination pathways or radioactive daughter products.

## 7. Discovery and nomenclature
Lawrencium was first reported in 1961 by a team at the Lawrence Radiation Laboratory in Berkeley led by Albert Ghiorso. The name honours Ernest O. Lawrence, inventor of the cyclotron. IUPAC retained the name lawrencium in its final 1997 recommendations for the transfermium elements.

## 8. Evidence quality and unresolved work
- **Atomic configuration, term and ionization energy:** PDG 2024 review of NIST data; retain the reported 7p ground configuration and cite database version.
- **Nuclear properties:** CIAAW and NUBASE2020 provide evaluated half-life estimates; ENSDF/NuDat should control detailed decay schemes.
- **Physical properties:** no direct bulk measurements; model values must be identified as predictions.
- **Pending:** Lr(I)/Lr(III) chemical comparison; improved ²⁶⁶Lr half-life; direct atomic level and ionization-energy uncertainty review; relativistic solid-state calculations.
