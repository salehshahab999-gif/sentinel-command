# Element 006: Carbon

**Symbol:** C  
**Atomic number:** 6  
**Group:** 14  
**Period:** 2  
**Block:** p-block  
**Classification:** Nonmetal  
**Standard atomic weight (CIAAW 2024):** [12.0096, 12.0116]  
**Ground-state electron configuration:** [He] 2s² 2p²  
**Physical state at approximately 298 K and 1 bar:** Solid

## 1. Atomic and periodic context
Carbon is element 6 in the periodic table. Its ground-state configuration, [He] 2s² 2p², provides the starting point for interpreting its valence structure, bonding patterns, and characteristic chemistry. Group placement is a useful organizing framework, but observed chemistry also depends on molecular environment, oxidation state, and thermodynamic conditions.

## 2. Isotopic and nuclear context
Carbon exhibits extensive allotropy, including graphite, diamond, graphene, fullerenes, and amorphous forms. Its isotope system (¹²C, ¹³C, ¹⁴C) is central to geochemistry and radiocarbon dating. A complete isotope dossier requires nuclide-by-nuclide evaluation of atomic masses, natural abundances, decay modes, and half-lives. Values are not inferred from the standard atomic weight.

## 3. Chemical and materials significance
Organic chemistry, steelmaking, graphite electrodes, diamond tools, carbon fibers, activated carbon, and graphene-related materials research.

## 4. Data quality and interpretation
The standard atomic weight is reproduced from the CIAAW 2024 table. Bracketed values are intervals reflecting the accepted range for normal terrestrial materials, not a symmetric measurement uncertainty. Parenthetical digits denote uncertainty in the final quoted digits. The electron configuration is the conventional ground-state configuration; exceptional configurations and spectroscopic term data require element-specific evaluation.
