# Element 115: Moscovium

**Symbol:** Mc  
**Atomic number:** 115  
**Group / period / block:** 15 / 7 / p  
**Standard atomic weight:** none; representative mass number [290] (CIAAW/NUBASE2020 longest-lived known isotope)  
**Predicted ground-state configuration:** [Rn] 5f¹⁴ 6d¹⁰ 7s² 7p³  
**Ground-state term:** ⁴S°₃/₂  
**First ionization energy:** not experimentally established; theoretical predictions are model-dependent  
**Representative isotopes:** ²⁸⁸Mc, ²⁸⁹Mc and ²⁹⁰Mc  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; nuclear measurements exist, but bulk physical properties and chemistry remain unknown or theoretical.

## 1. Atomic and electronic structure
Moscovium is a transactinoid p-block element in group 15, below bismuth. Its predicted neutral ground configuration is [Rn]5f¹⁴6d¹⁰7s²7p³, with ground term ⁴S°₃/₂. The inert-pair effect is expected to stabilise the 7s² electrons, making +1 and +3 oxidation states of theoretical interest. Relativistic effects are significant and may alter bonding and oxidation-state stability relative to lighter group-15 elements.

## 2. Nuclear properties and isotopes
Moscovium has no stable isotopes and no standard atomic weight. The CIAAW radioactive-elements table, based on NUBASE2020, identifies ²⁹⁰Mc as the longest-lived known isotope, with half-life 840(360) milliseconds. Other measured isotopes include ²⁸⁸Mc (177(20) ms) and ²⁸⁹Mc (410(150) ms). Some periodic-table fact boxes still use [289] as a representative mass; the current CIAAW/NUBASE listing makes [290] the longer-lived known nuclide. Exact decay branches and daughter sequences must be taken from current ENSDF/NuDat.

## 3. Physical properties and measurement limits
No macroscopic sample of moscovium has been produced. RSC lists melting point, boiling point and density as unknown. Its solid state at 20 °C shown in some periodic-table displays is a prediction rather than a direct observation. Bulk phase, thermal conductivity, mechanical properties and crystal structure have not been measured.

## 4. Chemical behaviour
No reproducible bulk chemical behaviour is established. Periodic trends suggest a heavy group-15 element with relativistic stabilisation of the 7s² pair, potentially favouring +1 and +3 oxidation states. These are theoretical expectations, not validated chemistry. Atom-at-a-time chemical studies remain extremely difficult because isotope half-lives are well below a second.

## 5. Research relevance
Moscovium has no commercial or technological application. Its importance is in nuclear stability, heavy p-block theory and the role of relativistic effects in periodic trends. Its production requires specialised accelerator facilities and identification through correlated decay chains.

## 6. Radiological safety
All moscovium isotopes are radioactive and very short-lived. Experiments are restricted to specialised licensed nuclear facilities. Contamination control, target and separator handling, daughter-product monitoring and isotope-specific radiation protection are required. No conventional laboratory handling data exist.

## 7. Discovery and nomenclature
Moscovium was synthesised by a collaboration involving the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research in Dubna, Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory and Oak Ridge National Laboratory. IUPAC approved the name in November 2016. It honours the Moscow region, where the JINR research centre is located.

## 8. Evidence quality and unresolved work
- **Atomic configuration and ground term:** RSC/PDG compilations; direct atomic spectrum and ionization energy are not established.
- **Nuclear data:** CIAAW/NUBASE2020 provides ²⁸⁸–²⁹⁰Mc half-lives; current ENSDF should control decay branches and uncertainties.
- **Physical properties:** melting point, boiling point and density are unknown experimentally.
- **Pending:** atom-at-a-time chemistry; oxidation-state confirmation; improved isotope production and decay evaluation; model comparisons for phase and thermodynamic properties.
