# Element 58: Cerium

**Symbol:** Ce  
**Atomic number:** 58  
**Group / period / block:** lanthanoid / 6 / f  
**Standard atomic weight:** 140.116(1)  
**Ground-state configuration:** [Xe] 4f 5d 6s²  
**Ground-state term:** ¹G₄  
**First ionization energy:** 5.5386(4) eV (NIST ASD)  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; full 4f spectroscopy, nuclear evaluation and phase-diagram audit remain open.

## 1. Electronic structure
Cerium is notable for the close energetic competition among 4f, 5d and 6s states. The neutral ground configuration is [Xe]4f5d6s² with ground term ¹G₄. The 4f electron is strongly involved in the element's unusual valence behavior. The +3 and +4 oxidation states are both important, unlike most lanthanoids whose chemistry is dominated by +3.

## 2. Isotopes and nuclear properties
Natural cerium is composed of four stable isotopes: ¹³⁶Ce, ¹³⁸Ce, ¹⁴⁰Ce and ¹⁴²Ce, with ¹⁴⁰Ce the most abundant. The lighter naturally occurring nuclides ¹³⁶Ce and ¹³⁸Ce are extremely long-lived double-beta-decay nuclides. CIAAW notes geological variation in isotopic composition. Isotope fractions and uncertainties should be quoted from its current table; evaluated decay properties should be checked in ENSDF/NuDat.

## 3. Condensed matter and valence transition
Cerium metal exhibits complex allotropy and a celebrated isostructural gamma-to-alpha volume-collapse transition. The transition is pressure- and temperature-dependent and involves strong coupling between 4f electronic localization/delocalization and lattice volume. It is a canonical problem in correlated-electron physics; simplified explanations should not imply a purely classical structural transformation. Crystal structure, density and phase boundaries must be specified by thermodynamic conditions.

## 4. Redox and coordination chemistry
Ce(III) and Ce(IV) chemistry is central. CeO₂ (ceria) is a fluorite-structure oxide with oxygen-vacancy chemistry and reversible Ce⁴⁺/Ce³⁺ redox behavior. This underpins oxygen-storage capacity in automotive three-way catalysts, catalytic oxidation and solid-oxide materials research. Ce₂O₃ represents the trivalent oxide. Redox potentials and defect concentrations depend on pH, temperature, oxygen chemical potential and composition.

## 5. Materials applications
Cerium compounds are used in glass polishing, UV-absorbing glass, catalysts, glass decolorization and specialized alloys. Ceria nanoparticles and doped ceria are studied for oxygen-ion transport, catalysis and electrochemical devices. Particle size, surface oxidation state and defect chemistry can make nanoparticle behavior differ substantially from bulk CeO₂.
