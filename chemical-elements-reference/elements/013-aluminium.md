# Element 013: Aluminium

**Symbol:** Al  
**Atomic number:** 13  
**Group:** 13  
**Period:** 3  
**Block:** p-block  
**Classification:** Post-transition metal  
**Standard atomic weight (CIAAW 2024):** 26.981 5384(3) [1]
**Ground-state electron configuration:** [Ne] 3s² 3p¹   [3]
**Ground-state term symbol:** ²P°₁/₂ [3]  
**Physical state at approximately 298 K and 1 bar:** Solid [4]

## 1. Atomic and periodic context
Aluminium is element 13. Its valence configuration, [Ne] 3s² 3p¹, provides the first-order framework for understanding bonding, oxidation-state preferences, and periodic trends. Group trends are guides rather than substitutes for compound-specific thermodynamic and spectroscopic evidence.

## 2. Isotopic and nuclear context
Naturally occurring aluminium is effectively monoisotopic, ²⁷Al. Its protective oxide film explains much of its corrosion resistance; the amphoteric oxide and hydroxide dissolve in strongly acidic and alkaline media. The standard atomic weight is not a nuclide mass and must not be used as a substitute for isotope-specific nuclear data. A complete nuclear appendix will report nuclide masses, natural abundances, decay modes, and half-lives separately.

## 3. Chemical and materials significance
Transportation structures, electrical conductors, packaging, construction, and corrosion-resistant alloys dominate industrial use. [4]

## 4. Data quality and interpretation
The standard atomic weight is taken from the CIAAW 2024 table. An interval denotes the range expected for normal terrestrial materials; parenthetical digits indicate uncertainty in the final quoted digits. The electron configuration is the conventional ground-state configuration. Physical state is stated at approximately 298 K and 1 bar.
