# Element 104: Rutherfordium

**Symbol:** Rf  
**Atomic number:** 104  
**Group / period / block:** 4 / 7 / d  
**Standard atomic weight:** none; representative mass number [267]  
**Predicted/compiled ground-state configuration:** [Rn] 5f¹⁴ 6d² 7s²  
**Ground-state term:** ³F₂  
**First ionization energy:** approximately 6.02 eV (PDG 2024 review of NIST/atomic-theory data)  
**Representative isotope:** ²⁶⁷Rf  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; chemical evidence is atom-at-a-time and most bulk properties are theoretical.

## 1. Atomic and electronic structure
Rutherfordium is the first transactinoid element and belongs to group 4, below titanium, zirconium and hafnium. Its predicted neutral ground configuration is [Rn]5f¹⁴6d²7s², with ground term ³F₂ and first ionization energy about 6.02 eV in the PDG review. Relativistic effects are important and influence orbital energies and bonding. The +4 oxidation state is expected to be the most important, consistent with its group-4 homologues, but direct chemical evidence is based on very small atom counts.

## 2. Nuclear properties and isotopes
Rutherfordium has no stable isotopes and no standard atomic weight. [267] is a representative mass number. CIAAW lists ²⁶⁷Rf with half-life 2.5(1.5) hours. Other Rf isotopes have different half-lives and decay chains. The evaluated half-life uncertainty is substantial; exact alpha energies, spontaneous-fission branches and daughter assignments must be drawn from current NUBASE and ENSDF data.

## 3. Physical properties and measurement limits
No bulk sample of rutherfordium has been produced. Density, melting point, boiling point, crystal structure, electrical resistivity and mechanical properties are not experimentally established. Periodic trends and relativistic density-functional calculations can provide predicted values, but they must be labelled as theoretical. The practical experimental domain is single-atom production, gas-phase chemistry and rapid radiochemical separation.

## 4. Chemical behaviour
Trace-scale chemical experiments indicate that rutherfordium behaves broadly like a group-4 element and resembles hafnium and zirconium in selected complexation and extraction systems. The +4 state is the principal expectation. Volatile halide and oxyhalide formation has been studied to compare Rf with Zr and Hf. Because only very few atoms are produced, measured distribution coefficients and reaction times have large statistical and systematic uncertainties.

## 5. Research relevance
Rutherfordium has no commercial or technological application. Its importance is in testing relativistic chemistry, periodic trends in group 4 and the boundary between actinoids and superheavy elements. Studies require accelerators, fast chemical systems and decay-correlation detection.

## 6. Radiological safety
Rutherfordium isotopes are radioactive and short-lived. Work is performed only in specialist nuclear-research facilities under licensed radiological controls. The major considerations are target and separator contamination, daughter products, alpha radiation and source-specific shielding and dosimetry.

## 7. Discovery and nomenclature
Rutherfordium was first claimed by research teams in Dubna and Berkeley, with discovery priority disputed. IUPAC adopted the name rutherfordium in 1997 to honour Ernest Rutherford. The naming decision was part of the international resolution of the transfermium-element naming controversy.

## 8. Evidence quality and unresolved work
- **Atomic structure and ionization energy:** PDG 2024 review of NIST/atomic-theory data; exact uncertainty and method should be recorded in a final edition.
- **Nuclear properties:** CIAAW and NUBASE2020 provide evaluated half-life values; ENSDF should be used for decay branches.
- **Physical properties:** no direct bulk measurements; predicted values should not be represented as observations.
- **Pending:** Rf(IV) hydrolysis and complexation constants; halide/oxyhalide volatility measurements; improved isotope half-life and decay evaluations; relativistic solid-state calculations.
