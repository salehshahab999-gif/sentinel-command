# Element 40: Zirconium

**Symbol:** Zr  
**Atomic number:** 40  
**Group:** 4  
**Period:** 5  
**Block:** d-block  
**Standard atomic weight (CIAAW 2024):** 91.222(3)  
**Ground-state electron configuration:** [Kr] 4d² 5s²  
**Ground-state term (NIST tabulation):** ³F₂  
**First ionization energy (NIST tabulation):** 6.6341 eV  
**State at 20 °C and 1 bar:** Solid

## 1. Atomic and periodic context
Zirconium is a period-5 d-block transition metal. Its ground-state configuration is [Kr] 4d² 5s². The tabulated ground-state term and first ionization energy are reproduced from the NIST periodic-table compilation; the current NIST Atomic Spectra Database should be used for a full level-by-level spectroscopic treatment.

## 2. Isotopic and nuclear data
CIAAW's 2024 representative natural isotopic composition is: ⁹⁰Zr 0.5147(6), ⁹¹Zr 0.1123(6), ⁹²Zr 0.1716(4), ⁹⁴Zr 0.1736(7), and ⁹⁶Zr 0.0278(4). The standard atomic weight was revised in 2024 to 91.222(3), reflecting updated isotope-ratio measurements and geological variation. ⁹⁶Zr is an extremely long-lived double-beta-decay nuclide.

## 3. Physical and thermodynamic properties
- **Melting point:** 2127 K (1854 °C)
- **Boiling point:** 4679 K (4406 °C)
- **Density:** 6.52 g cm⁻³ (RSC reference value; temperature and pressure conventions should be checked against the underlying handbook before precision engineering use)
- **Crystal structure at ambient conditions:** Zirconium is hcp (α-Zr) at ambient pressure and temperature; it transforms to a body-centred cubic β phase at elevated temperature, near 863 °C. Phase and mechanical properties depend on alloying, oxygen content, and thermal history.

The values above are reference values, not a critical evaluation of all measurements. High-temperature phase transitions, purity, defects, and interstitial contamination can materially alter measured behaviour.

## 4. Chemical behaviour and bonding
The dominant oxidation state is +4, with highly stable ZrO₂ and zirconium(IV) halides. Zirconium is strongly oxophilic and forms a protective oxide film, giving excellent corrosion resistance in many environments. Its coordination chemistry is extensive, often involving high coordination numbers.

## 5. Materials science and applications
Zirconium alloys are used as nuclear-fuel cladding because suitably purified nuclear-grade zirconium combines corrosion resistance with a low thermal-neutron absorption cross-section. Hafnium is a critical impurity to remove for this application because it absorbs neutrons strongly. Zirconia is used in refractories, technical ceramics, dental materials, and oxygen sensors; zircon (ZrSiO₄) is an important mineral and ceramic feedstock.

## 6. Toxicology, safety, and handling
Fine zirconium powder can be pyrophoric. Bulk metal is generally corrosion resistant, but machining dust and finely divided material require fire and inhalation controls. Nuclear-grade applications require strict impurity specifications, especially for hafnium.
