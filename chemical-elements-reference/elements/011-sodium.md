# Element 011: Sodium

**Symbol:** Na  
**Atomic number:** 11  
**Group:** 1  
**Period:** 3  
**Block:** s-block  
**Classification:** Alkali metal  
**Standard atomic weight (CIAAW 2024):** 22.989 769 28(2) [1]
**Ground-state electron configuration:** [Ne] 3s¹   [3]
**Ground-state term symbol:** ²S₁/₂ [3]  
**Physical state at approximately 298 K and 1 bar:** Solid [4]

## 1. Atomic and periodic context
Sodium is element 11. Its valence configuration, [Ne] 3s¹, provides the first-order framework for understanding bonding, oxidation-state preferences, and periodic trends. Group trends are guides rather than substitutes for compound-specific thermodynamic and spectroscopic evidence.

## 2. Isotopic and nuclear context
Sodium has one stable nuclide, ²³Na, and its chemistry is overwhelmingly characterized by the +1 oxidation state. Metallic sodium reacts vigorously with water and moist air; fire response requires compatible Class D procedures. The standard atomic weight is not a nuclide mass and must not be used as a substitute for isotope-specific nuclear data. A complete nuclear appendix will report nuclide masses, natural abundances, decay modes, and half-lives separately.

## 3. Chemical and materials significance
Sodium compounds are central to bulk chemicals, glass manufacture, soaps, pulp processing, and sodium-vapor technologies. Metallic sodium is used in specialized heat-transfer and chemical applications. [4]

## 4. Data quality and interpretation
The standard atomic weight is taken from the CIAAW 2024 table. An interval denotes the range expected for normal terrestrial materials; parenthetical digits indicate uncertainty in the final quoted digits. The electron configuration is the conventional ground-state configuration. Physical state is stated at approximately 298 K and 1 bar.
