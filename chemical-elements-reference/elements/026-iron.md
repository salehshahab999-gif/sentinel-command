# Element 026: Iron

**Symbol:** Fe  
**Atomic number:** 26  
**Group:** 8  
**Period:** 4  
**Block:** d-block  
**Classification:** Transition metal  
**Standard atomic weight (CIAAW 2024):** 55.845(2) [1]
**Ground-state electron configuration:** [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s² [3]  
**Ground-state term symbol:** ⁵D₄ [3]
**Physical state at approximately 298 K and 1 bar:** Solid [4]

## 1. Atomic and periodic context
Iron is element 26. Its ground-state electron configuration, [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s², is the conventional starting point for interpreting valence, bonding, and periodic behavior. Transition-metal chemistry is particularly sensitive to oxidation state, coordination environment, ligand field, and phase.

## 2. Isotopic and nuclear context
Iron has four stable isotopes, ⁵⁴Fe, ⁵⁶Fe, ⁵⁷Fe, and ⁵⁸Fe. Its Fe(II)/Fe(III) redox chemistry is central to metallurgy, environmental chemistry, biology, and geochemistry. Standard atomic weight summarizes naturally occurring material and is not interchangeable with the mass of any individual nuclide. Isotope-specific masses, abundances, and decay data belong in the dedicated nuclear-data tables.

## 3. Chemical and materials significance
Steel and cast iron production, infrastructure, machinery, transport, magnetic materials, and industrial catalysts make iron one of the most economically important elements.

## 4. Data quality and interpretation
The standard atomic weight is reproduced from the CIAAW 2024 table. Parenthetical digits indicate uncertainty in the final quoted digits. The electron configuration is the conventional ground-state configuration. Physical state is stated at approximately 298 K and 1 bar.
