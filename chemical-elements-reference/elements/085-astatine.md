# Element 85: Astatine

**Symbol:** At  
**Atomic number:** 85  
**Group / period / block:** 17 / 6 / p  
**Standard atomic weight:** none (no stable isotopes); representative mass number [210]  
**Neutral ground-state configuration:** [Xe] 4f¹⁴ 5d¹⁰ 6s² 6p⁵  
**Ground-state term:** ²P°₃/₂  
**First ionization energy:** 9.3175 eV (NIST/PDG compilation)  
**Representative radionuclides:** ²¹⁰At and ²¹¹At  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; many physical properties are estimated and chemical evidence is limited by atom-scale availability and short half-lives.

## 1. Atomic and electronic structure
Astatine is the heaviest naturally occurring halogen by atomic number, but it has no stable isotopes. The neutral ground configuration is [Xe]4f¹⁴5d¹⁰6s²6p⁵ and the ground term is ²P°₃/₂. Relativistic effects are substantial, and astatine is expected to show more metallic character than lighter halogens. Experimental chemistry at trace scale indicates halogen-like behaviour, particularly similarities to iodine, but the limited number of atoms and rapid radioactive decay constrain direct measurements.

## 2. Nuclear properties and isotopes
Astatine has no stable nuclides and therefore no standard atomic weight. The bracketed mass number [210] is a conventional representative value, not an atomic weight. ²¹⁰At has a half-life of about 8.1 hours, while ²¹¹At has a half-life of 7.214 hours. ²¹¹At decays by alpha emission and electron capture, producing a decay chain that ultimately yields stable ²⁰⁷Pb. Exact branching ratios, alpha energies and daughter data must be taken from evaluated ENSDF/NuDat and radionuclide standards.

## 3. Physical properties and measurement limitations
Astatine is expected to be a solid at 20 °C. The RSC compilation lists approximate melting and boiling points of 300 °C and 350 °C, respectively, but these values are estimates rather than robust direct bulk measurements. Density is not established. Only tiny quantities have been produced, and no conventional macroscopic sample can be handled or studied like a stable element. Any tabulated bulk property must be explicitly labelled estimated, model-derived or uncertain.

## 4. Chemical behaviour
Astatine can exhibit several formal oxidation states, including −1, +1, +3, +5 and +7, although experimental access to its chemistry is extremely limited. Astatide, At⁻, and oxidised astatine species have been studied in trace radiochemical systems. Its speciation is sensitive to oxidation conditions, solvent and ligand environment. Do not assume all iodine chemistry transfers unchanged to astatine; relativistic effects and increasing metallic character can alter bond strengths and redox behaviour.

## 5. Nuclear medicine and research relevance
²¹¹At is a promising alpha-emitting radionuclide for targeted alpha therapy research. Its 7.214-hour half-life and high-linear-energy-transfer alpha emissions can deliver highly localised radiation when appropriately attached to a targeting molecule. The central radiochemical challenge is maintaining astatine attachment and controlling daughter redistribution under physiological conditions. ²¹¹At is produced in specialised facilities, including through alpha irradiation of bismuth targets. This is a research and radiopharmaceutical-development area, not an established routine therapy everywhere.

## 6. Radiological safety
Astatine is dangerously radioactive. Its short half-lives and alpha emissions require specialist radiochemistry infrastructure, secure containment, activity measurement and contamination controls. Risk depends on isotope, chemical form and route of exposure. Work with ²¹¹At requires an authorised radiological laboratory and isotope-specific dosimetry.

## 7. Discovery and nomenclature
Astatine was first synthesised in 1940 by Dale Corson, Kenneth MacKenzie and Emilio Segrè through alpha-particle bombardment of bismuth. Its name derives from Greek *astatos*, meaning unstable. It was later found in trace amounts in natural radioactive decay chains.

## 8. Evidence quality and unresolved work
- **Atomic weight status:** no standard atomic weight; [210] is a representative mass number only.
- **Atomic configuration and term:** NIST/PDG compilation; detailed current atomic-level data remain limited.
- **Physical properties:** RSC values are approximate estimates; density is not established.
- **Pending:** isotope-specific nuclear level and decay evaluations; trace-speciation constants; At-211 labelling stability and daughter-recoil studies; model validation for estimated phase and thermodynamic properties.
