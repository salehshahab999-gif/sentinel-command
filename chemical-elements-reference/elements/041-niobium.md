# Element 41: Niobium

**Symbol:** Nb  
**Atomic number:** 41  
**Group:** 5  
**Period:** 5  
**Block:** d-block  
**Standard atomic weight (CIAAW 2024):** 92.906 37(1)  
**Ground-state electron configuration:** [Kr] 4d⁴ 5s¹  
**Ground-state term (NIST tabulation):** ⁶D₁/₂  
**First ionization energy (NIST tabulation):** 6.7589 eV  
**State at 20 °C and 1 bar:** Solid

## 1. Atomic and periodic context
Niobium is a period-5 d-block transition metal. Its ground-state configuration is [Kr] 4d⁴ 5s¹. The tabulated ground-state term and first ionization energy are reproduced from the NIST periodic-table compilation; the current NIST Atomic Spectra Database should be used for a full level-by-level spectroscopic treatment.

## 2. Isotopic and nuclear data
Natural niobium is effectively monoisotopic: stable ⁹³Nb has a representative amount fraction of 1. Its standard atomic weight is based on the atomic mass of ⁹³Nb. Other niobium isotopes are radioactive and are produced artificially or in nuclear processes.

**Evidence status:** Standard atomic weight and representative isotope abundances are attributed to CIAAW's current tables. This monograph is not a complete evaluated nuclide chart. Half-lives, decay branches, Q-values, radiation energies, and uncertainties for all radioactive isotopes require isotope-by-isotope verification against ENSDF.

## 3. Physical and thermodynamic properties
- **Melting point:** 2750 K (2477 °C)
- **Boiling point:** 5014 K (4741 °C)
- **Density:** 8.57 g cm⁻³ (RSC reference value; temperature and pressure conventions should be checked against the underlying handbook before precision engineering use)
- **Crystal structure at ambient conditions:** Niobium has a body-centred cubic (bcc) crystal structure at ambient conditions. It remains mechanically workable despite its high melting point, and its properties are strongly affected by interstitial oxygen, nitrogen, and hydrogen.

The values above are reference values, not a critical evaluation of all measurements. High-temperature phase transitions, purity, defects, and interstitial contamination can materially alter measured behaviour.

## 4. Chemical behaviour and bonding
Niobium commonly exhibits +5 and +3 oxidation states, with +5 especially stable in oxides and halides. Nb₂O₅ and niobates are important materials. The metal forms a protective oxide layer and displays strong affinity for oxygen, carbon, and nitrogen; carbide and nitride phases are important in metallurgy.

## 5. Materials science and applications
Niobium is used in microalloyed steels, corrosion-resistant alloys, and nickel-based superalloys. Nb-Ti and Nb₃Sn are important superconductors in high-field magnets, including MRI and accelerator systems. Niobium compounds and oxides also appear in optical, dielectric, and catalytic materials.

## 6. Toxicology, safety, and handling
Bulk niobium is comparatively stable, but metal powders and machining residues require combustible-metal precautions. Occupational controls should address dust and compound-specific exposure; hazard profiles differ between metal, oxides, and soluble salts.
