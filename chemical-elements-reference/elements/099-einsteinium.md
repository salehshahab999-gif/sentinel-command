# Element 99: Einsteinium

**Symbol:** Es  
**Atomic number:** 99  
**Group / period / block:** actinoid / 7 / f  
**Standard atomic weight:** none; representative mass number [252]  
**Neutral ground-state configuration:** [Rn] 5f¹¹ 7s²  
**Ground-state term:** ⁴I°₁₅/₂  
**First ionization energy:** 6.36840 eV (NIST ASD 5.12; accessed 2026-11-20)  
**Representative isotopes:** ²⁵²Es, ²⁵³Es and ²⁵⁴Es  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; physical properties and chemistry are limited by very small sample quantities and short isotope half-lives.

## 1. Atomic and electronic structure
Einsteinium is a synthetic actinoid with neutral ground configuration [Rn]5f¹¹7s² and ground term ⁴I°₁₅/₂. The common oxidation state is +3, although +2 chemistry has also been reported in selected conditions. Its 5f shell gives rise to actinoid-specific electronic and spectroscopic behaviour. The quoted ground-state information is from the PDG review of NIST atomic data; a final specialist appendix should cite the exact NIST ASD version and level record.

## 2. Nuclear properties and isotopes
Einsteinium has no stable isotopes and therefore no standard atomic weight. [252] is a representative mass number, not an atomic weight. ²⁵²Es has a half-life of approximately 471.7 days; ²⁵³Es about 20.5 days; ²⁵⁴Es about 275.7 days. These radionuclides decay through alpha and/or electron-capture branches depending on isotope. Exact branching, emission energies and daughter products should be retrieved from current ENSDF/NuDat evaluations. The ²⁵²Es and ²⁵⁴Es values are orientation figures and must not replace evaluated uncertainties.

## 3. Physical properties and measurement limits
No macroscopic bulk sample of einsteinium is available for routine physical-property measurement. A melting point near 860 °C and density estimates appear in some compilations, but these are model-derived or inferred and should not be represented as direct high-precision measurements. Boiling point, thermal conductivity, elastic constants and phase boundaries are not reliably established as standard reference values. Any future material-property entry must label its status as calculated, extrapolated or measured.

## 4. Chemical behaviour
Einsteinium chemistry is predominantly trivalent and resembles that of neighbouring actinoids. Es(III) forms compounds and complexes in radiochemical systems, while the +2 state has been observed under selected reducing conditions. The limited quantity and rapid decay of available isotopes constrain equilibrium studies, crystallography and spectroscopy. Solution speciation depends on pH, oxidation potential, ligands and radiolytic changes.

## 5. Research relevance
Einsteinium has no commercial or routine technological application. Its principal importance is in actinoid chemistry, atomic spectroscopy and the production of heavier elements for nuclear research. Experiments require specialised facilities and careful accounting of isotope mass, radiological activity and daughter products.

## 6. Radiological safety
Einsteinium is intensely radioactive. Internal contamination by inhalation, ingestion or wounds can deliver significant alpha and daughter-product radiation doses. Handling requires licensed radiochemical facilities, sealed containment, remote manipulation where appropriate, contamination monitoring and isotope-specific dosimetry. Its scarcity does not reduce the hazard of a prepared source.

## 7. Discovery and nomenclature
Einsteinium was identified in 1952 in debris from the first thermonuclear test and later confirmed by laboratory investigations. It was named in honour of Albert Einstein. The discovery was announced publicly in 1955 after the initial classified research period.

## 8. Evidence quality and unresolved work

- **Nuclear data:** current ENSDF/NuDat should control all half-lives, branching fractions and emission energies.
