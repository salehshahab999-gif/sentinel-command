# Element 33: Arsenic

**Symbol:** As  
**Atomic number:** 33  
**Group:** 15  
**Period:** 4  
**Block:** p-block  
**Classification:** Post-transition metal / metalloid classification depends on convention  
**Standard atomic weight (CIAAW):** 74.921 595(6)  
**Ground-state electron configuration:** [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p³  
**Physical state at approximately 298 K and 1 bar:** Solid

## 1. Atomic and periodic context
Arsenic is a period-4 p-block element. Its valence-shell configuration is represented by [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p³. The element's bonding and chemistry depend on oxidation state, coordination environment, allotrope, and phase; a single periodic classification does not capture all of these behaviours.

## 2. Isotopic and nuclear context
Naturally occurring arsenic is monoisotopic: essentially all natural arsenic is ⁷⁵As. CIAAW lists its atomic mass as 74.921 595(6) Da and amount fraction 1. Arsenic has numerous radioactive isotopes, but a complete nuclide inventory and decay evaluation is outside this initial monograph.

**Evidence status:** Natural isotope masses and representative amount fractions are attributed to CIAAW. A complete evaluated nuclear chart, decay modes, branching ratios, and half-life uncertainties have not yet been compiled for this element.

## 3. Physical properties
At ordinary pressure, elemental arsenic has several allotropes, with grey arsenic the most stable under ambient conditions. RSC reports that arsenic sublimes at about 616 °C (889 K), rather than exhibiting an ordinary atmospheric-pressure melting point. Its listed density is approximately 5.75 g cm⁻³; density depends on allotrope and sample condition.

**Evidence status:** Reference values are drawn from the RSC periodic-table fact box. These are secondary reference values and are not yet a critical compilation of primary measurements. Phase, allotrope, pressure, purity, and uncertainty must be specified before final release.

## 4. Chemical behaviour and bonding
Common oxidation states include −3, +3, and +5, with +3 and +5 prominent in inorganic chemistry. Arsenic forms oxides, oxyacids, sulfides, and organoarsenic compounds. Redox state and molecular speciation strongly control mobility, reactivity, and toxicity; total arsenic concentration alone is insufficient for environmental interpretation.

## 5. Materials science and applications
Arsenic is used in compound semiconductors, notably gallium arsenide, and in selected alloys and specialized materials. Historical uses in pesticides and wood preservatives have been restricted in many jurisdictions because of toxicity. Semiconductor applications generally involve controlled compound forms rather than bulk elemental arsenic.

## 6. Toxicology and handling
Arsenic and many arsenic compounds are highly toxic. Inorganic arsenic exposure is associated with cancer and multiple systemic effects. Toxicity differs among inorganic As(III)/As(V), methylated species, and organic arsenicals; route, dose, duration, and chemical speciation are critical. Handle under strict occupational and environmental controls.
