# Element 016: Sulfur

**Symbol:** S  
**Atomic number:** 16  
**Group:** 16  
**Period:** 3  
**Block:** p-block  
**Classification:** Nonmetal  
**Standard atomic weight (CIAAW 2024):** [32.059, 32.076]  
**Ground-state electron configuration:** [Ne] 3s² 3p⁴  
**Physical state at approximately 298 K and 1 bar:** Solid

## 1. Atomic and periodic context
Sulfur is element 16. Its valence configuration, [Ne] 3s² 3p⁴, provides the first-order framework for understanding bonding, oxidation-state preferences, and periodic trends. Group trends are guides rather than substitutes for compound-specific thermodynamic and spectroscopic evidence.

## 2. Isotopic and nuclear context
Natural sulfur includes ³²S, ³³S, ³⁴S, and ³⁶S. Sulfur isotope ratios are important tracers in geochemistry, atmospheric science, and biogeochemical cycling. Hydrogen sulfide is acutely toxic. The standard atomic weight is not a nuclide mass and must not be used as a substitute for isotope-specific nuclear data. A complete nuclear appendix will report nuclide masses, natural abundances, decay modes, and half-lives separately.

## 3. Chemical and materials significance
Sulfuric acid manufacture, fertilizer production, vulcanization of rubber, petroleum refining, and chemical synthesis are major uses.

## 4. Data quality and interpretation
The standard atomic weight is taken from the CIAAW 2024 table. An interval denotes the range expected for normal terrestrial materials; parenthetical digits indicate uncertainty in the final quoted digits. The electron configuration is the conventional ground-state configuration. Physical state is stated at approximately 298 K and 1 bar.
