# Element 55: Caesium (Cesium)

**Symbol:** Cs  
**Atomic number:** 55  
**Group / period / block:** 1 / 6 / s  
**Standard atomic weight:** 132.905 451 96(6)  
**Ground-state configuration:** [Xe] 6s  
**Ground-state term:** ²S₁/₂  
**First ionization energy:** 3.8939057 eV (NIST evaluated value)  
**Stable nuclide:** ¹³³Cs (natural abundance effectively 100%)  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; full isotope, thermophysical and nuclear-data audit remains open.

## 1. Atomic and quantum structure
Caesium is an alkali metal with a single 6s valence electron. Its low first ionization energy is consistent with facile formation of Cs⁺ and exceptionally strong reducing behavior in appropriate chemical environments. The neutral-atom ground level is ²S₁/₂. The hyperfine transition of ¹³³Cs defines the SI second: 9,192,631,770 periods of the radiation corresponding to the transition between the two hyperfine levels of its ground state. This metrological definition concerns the isotope and transition, not a generic bulk-metal property.

## 2. Isotopes and nuclear metrology
Naturally occurring caesium is effectively monoisotopic ¹³³Cs, a stable nuclide with nuclear spin I = 7/2. Radioisotope ¹³⁷Cs (half-life about 30.05 y) is a major fission product and environmental radiological tracer; its decay proceeds through ¹³⁷mBa and is associated with the characteristic 662 keV gamma emission from the daughter transition. Exact decay branching, emissions and evaluated uncertainties should be taken from ENSDF/NuDat rather than inferred from simplified decay charts.

## 3. Condensed-matter and physical behavior
At ordinary pressure caesium is a soft, pale-gold metal with a body-centered cubic structure near room temperature. It melts at approximately 28.44 °C, unusually close to ambient conditions. Its density, elastic constants, heat capacity and phase boundaries must be reported with temperature and pressure. High chemical reactivity, low melting point and low mechanical strength constrain handling and engineering use.

## 4. Chemical behavior
The dominant oxidation state is +1. Caesium reacts vigorously with water and oxygen; reaction severity depends on surface condition, geometry and heat/mass transfer. Cs⁺ is a large, weakly hydrated monovalent cation and forms salts with characteristic solubility trends. Caesium compounds are used in specialized optical, vacuum, drilling-fluid and catalytic applications. The chemistry of radioactive caesium in the environment is dominated by Cs⁺, including exchange and fixation on certain clay minerals.

## 5. Applications and safety
The ¹³³Cs hyperfine transition is foundational to primary frequency standards. ¹³⁷Cs is important in radiation calibration, industrial gauges and post-fission environmental monitoring. Soluble caesium salts can be biologically distributed as potassium analogues; radioactive contamination requires isotope-specific dose assessment and controlled remediation.

## 6. Verification priorities
- NIST ASD: level structure, hyperfine constants and ionization energies.
- CIAAW: standard atomic weight and isotopic composition.
- ENSDF/NuDat: ¹³³Cs and ¹³⁷Cs decay data, emissions and uncertainties.
- NIST/critically evaluated materials sources: phase diagram, transport and mechanical properties.
