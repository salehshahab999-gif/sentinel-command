# Element 98: Californium

**Symbol:** Cf  
**Atomic number:** 98  
**Group / period / block:** actinoid / 7 / f  
**Standard atomic weight:** none; representative mass number [251]  
**Neutral ground-state configuration:** [Rn] 5f¹⁰ 7s²  
**Ground-state term:** ⁵I₈  
**First ionization energy:** 6.281878 eV (NIST ASD 5.12; accessed 2026-11-20)  
**Important isotopes:** ²⁴⁹Cf, ²⁵⁰Cf, ²⁵¹Cf and ²⁵²Cf  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; isotope-specific neutron emission and physical-property datasets require careful evaluation.

## 1. Atomic and electronic structure
Californium is a synthetic actinoid with neutral ground configuration [Rn]5f¹⁰7s² and ground term ⁵I₈. The +3 oxidation state is common, while +2 and +4 compounds also occur in specialised chemical environments. Its open 5f shell produces complex magnetic and spectroscopic properties. Chemical behaviour and redox stability depend on the ligand field, oxidation conditions and solid-state structure.

## 2. Nuclear properties and isotopes
Californium has no stable isotopes and therefore no standard atomic weight; [251] is a representative mass number. Important isotopes include ²⁴⁹Cf (half-life about 351 years), ²⁵⁰Cf (about 13.1 years), ²⁵¹Cf (about 898 years) and ²⁵²Cf (about 2.645 years). ²⁵²Cf emits neutrons through spontaneous fission and is an important specialised neutron source. Its exact neutron yield, decay branching and emission spectrum depend on isotope purity and source age; evaluated nuclear data are essential.

## 3. Physical and condensed-matter properties
Californium is a radioactive metal. The RSC compilation lists melting point 900 °C and density 15.1 g cm⁻³; a boiling point is not established in the same fact box. These data should be treated as compiled reference values, given the minute quantities available for measurement. The phase, isotope mix, self-heating and sample history should be stated for any material-property report.

## 4. Chemical behaviour
Californium chemistry is dominated by +3, with +2 and +4 states known in selected compounds. Cf(III) forms oxides, halides and coordination complexes. Its behaviour is important in actinoid separation and the study of f-electron chemistry. Hydrolysis, oxidation-state control and complexation depend on pH and ligand environment. Environmental transport is governed by chemical speciation and particle association.

## 5. Materials science and applications
Californium-252 is a strong neutron source used in specialised applications such as neutron activation analysis, neutron radiography, reactor start-up sources and well-logging instruments. The emitted neutrons can reveal material composition and hydrogen-bearing layers, but these uses require regulated sealed-source systems and calibrated detector setups. Californium is also used in research on the synthesis of heavier elements. Its production is difficult and limited to very small quantities.

## 6. Radiological safety
Californium is highly radioactive. ²⁵²Cf presents both alpha/beta decay and significant neutron-radiation concerns from spontaneous fission. External dose, neutron activation of surrounding materials and internal contamination must be considered in a source-specific safety analysis. Handling requires licensed facilities, shielding, remote handling where appropriate, source inventory control and isotope-specific dosimetry.

## 7. Discovery and nomenclature
Californium was first synthesised in 1950 by Stanley Thompson, Kenneth Street Jr., Albert Ghiorso and Glenn Seaborg at Berkeley by bombarding curium with alpha particles. It was named after California and the University of California, Berkeley.
