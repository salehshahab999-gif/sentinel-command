# Element 73: Tantalum

**Symbol:** Ta  
**Atomic number:** 73  
**Group / period / block:** 5 / 6 / d  
**CIAAW standard atomic weight:** 180.947 88(2)  
**Neutral ground-state configuration:** [Xe] 4f¹⁴ 5d³ 6s²  
**Ground-state term:** ⁴F₃/₂  
**First ionization energy:** 7.54957 eV (NIST Atomic Data Handbook)  
**Naturally occurring isotopes:** ¹⁸¹Ta and the long-lived isomer ¹⁸⁰ᵐTa  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; detailed nuclear-isomer, corrosion and capacitor-material datasets remain open.

## 1. Atomic and electronic structure
Tantalum is a group-5 transition metal with neutral ground configuration [Xe]4f¹⁴5d³6s² and ground term ⁴F₃/₂. The predominant oxidation state in ordinary compounds is +5, though lower oxidation states occur in specialised compounds and clusters. Tantalum(V) forms stable oxides, halides and oxyanions. Its refractory nature and chemical inertness are closely connected to the strong stability of its surface oxide, Ta₂O₅.

## 2. Isotopic composition and nuclear properties
CIAAW lists ¹⁸⁰Ta with representative amount fraction 0.0001176(23) and ¹⁸¹Ta with 0.9998824(23), giving a standard atomic weight of 180.94788(2). The naturally occurring ¹⁸⁰Ta is a long-lived nuclear isomer (commonly written ¹⁸⁰ᵐTa), not the ground state of mass 180. It is a remarkable naturally occurring metastable nuclide with an exceptionally long lifetime. ¹⁸¹Ta is the dominant stable nuclide and has nuclear spin I = 7/2. Nuclear level assignments, decay limits and evaluated uncertainty should be quoted from current ENSDF/NuDat evaluations.

## 3. Physical and thermophysical properties
At ambient conditions tantalum is a dense, ductile, silvery metal with a body-centred cubic structure. The RSC compilation lists melting point 3017 °C, boiling point 5455 °C and density 16.4 g cm⁻³. It has excellent corrosion resistance in many environments because of a stable passive oxide film. Mechanical and corrosion performance depend on oxygen content, grain size, surface finish and processing history.

## 4. Chemical behaviour
Tantalum(V) is the most common oxidation state. Ta₂O₅ is a stable dielectric oxide; tantalum forms halides, oxyhalides, carbides, nitrides and complex oxometalates. In many aqueous environments the passive oxide limits corrosion, although strongly aggressive media can attack the metal or its oxide film. Ta and Nb have similar chemistry, requiring careful analytical separation and speciation when both are present.

## 5. Materials science and applications
A major application is the manufacture of high-capacitance electronic components, particularly tantalum capacitors that use a thin Ta₂O₅ dielectric layer. Tantalum is also used in corrosion-resistant chemical equipment, medical implants and surgical devices, and high-temperature alloys for aerospace or reactor environments. Its biocompatibility and corrosion resistance have led to porous tantalum materials in orthopaedic applications. Device performance depends on oxide quality, porosity, leakage current, electrolyte and manufacturing controls.

## 6. Safety and environmental considerations
Bulk tantalum is relatively inert, but fine powders can present dust and fire hazards. Soluble tantalum compounds and process chemicals require compound-specific hazard assessment. Supply-chain impacts arise from mining and refining of tantalum-bearing ores; responsible sourcing and traceability are important in electronics supply chains.

## 7. Discovery and nomenclature
Tantalum was discovered by Anders Gustaf Ekeberg in 1802 and named after Tantalus from Greek mythology. The name refers to the metal's resistance to being dissolved in acids, an analogy to Tantalus's inability to reach water in the myth.
