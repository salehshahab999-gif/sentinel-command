# Element 110: Darmstadtium

**Symbol:** Ds  
**Atomic number:** 110  
**Group / period / block:** 10 / 7 / d  
**Standard atomic weight:** none; representative mass number [281]  
**Predicted ground-state configuration:** [Rn] 5f¹⁴ 6d⁸ 7s² (model-dependent)  
**Ground-state term:** compiled/predicted term ³D₃  
**First ionization energy:** not experimentally established; theoretical values are model-dependent  
**Representative isotope:** ²⁸¹Ds  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; atomic and nuclear data are sparse and chemical properties have not been established experimentally.

## 1. Atomic and electronic structure
Darmstadtium is a transactinoid in group 10, below nickel, palladium and platinum. Relativistic calculations commonly predict a valence configuration involving 6d and 7s orbitals, often represented as [Rn]5f¹⁴6d⁸7s², but alternative near-degenerate configurations may occur. A compiled ground-term assignment is ³D₃. Because no high-resolution atomic spectrum is available, configuration and term should be labelled predicted or compiled rather than experimentally definitive.

## 2. Nuclear properties and isotopes
Darmstadtium has no stable isotopes and no standard atomic weight. [281] is a representative mass number. CIAAW/NUBASE2020 list ²⁸¹Ds with a half-life of 14(3) seconds. Other isotopes have different decay properties, and identification uses correlated alpha-decay chains. Exact level schemes, decay branching and daughter assignments require current ENSDF/NuDat records.

## 3. Physical properties and measurement limits
No macroscopic sample of darmstadtium has been produced. Density, melting point, boiling point, crystal structure, hardness and transport coefficients are experimentally unknown. Relativistic density-functional calculations can provide predictions, but these are highly method-dependent and should not be reported as measured physical constants.

## 4. Chemical behaviour
No conclusive experimental chemical characterisation of darmstadtium is established. It is expected to resemble group-10 elements, particularly platinum, but relativistic effects may alter the relative stability of oxidation states and bonding. Oxidation states and compound volatilities currently remain theoretical projections and should not be stated as confirmed chemistry.

## 5. Research relevance
Darmstadtium has no commercial or technological use. Its importance is in superheavy-element synthesis, nuclear stability studies and tests of relativistic periodic trends. Experiments produce only a few atoms and identify them through correlated radioactive decays.

## 6. Radiological safety
Darmstadtium isotopes are radioactive and short-lived. Any research is performed in specialised licensed accelerator facilities with containment, contamination monitoring, shielding and isotope-specific dosimetry. No routine handling or bulk chemical safety data are available.

## 7. Discovery and nomenclature
Darmstadtium was first produced in 1994 by a team at GSI in Darmstadt, Germany. It was named after the city and research centre where the discovery took place. IUPAC approved the name in 2003.
