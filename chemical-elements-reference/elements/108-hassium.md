# Element 108: Hassium

**Symbol:** Hs  
**Atomic number:** 108  
**Group / period / block:** 8 / 7 / d  
**Standard atomic weight:** none; representative mass number [269]  
**Predicted/compiled ground-state configuration:** [Rn] 5f¹⁴ 6d⁶ 7s²  
**Ground-state term:** ⁵D₄  
**First ionization energy:** approximately 7.6 eV (PDG 2024 review of NIST/atomic-theory data)  
**Representative isotopes:** ²⁶⁹Hs and ²⁷⁰Hs  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; chemical evidence is limited to atom-at-a-time studies and bulk properties are unknown.

## 1. Atomic and electronic structure
Hassium is a transactinoid in group 8, below iron, ruthenium and osmium. Its predicted neutral configuration is [Rn]5f¹⁴6d⁶7s², with ground term ⁵D₄. A high oxidation state, particularly +8, is expected to be relevant by group analogy. Relativistic effects influence the stability, bonding and volatility of hassium compounds and must be included in any detailed theoretical treatment.

## 2. Nuclear properties and isotopes
Hassium has no stable isotopes and no standard atomic weight. [269] is a representative mass number. CIAAW/NUBASE2020 list ²⁶⁹Hs with half-life 15(7) seconds and ²⁷⁰Hs with 9(4) seconds. The small event counts and short half-lives create large relative uncertainties. Decay modes, alpha energies and daughter nuclides should be taken from current ENSDF/NuDat evaluations.

## 3. Physical properties and measurement limits
No macroscopic sample of hassium has been produced. Density, melting point, boiling point, crystal structure and bulk transport coefficients are experimentally unknown. Computational predictions may provide estimates but should not be presented as measured data. The available experimental domain consists of single-atom production, chemical separation and radioactive decay detection.

## 4. Chemical behaviour
Hassium is the transactinoid for which a significant atom-at-a-time chemical experiment has been reported. In a study involving only seven detected atoms, hassium was rapidly oxidised in oxygen-containing gas to a highly volatile oxide, assigned as HsO₄, and its adsorption behaviour was compared with that of OsO₄. The result supports the expected group-8 chemistry while showing why relativistic effects and measurement uncertainty must be considered. The molecular formula is an experimental assignment under trace conditions, not a bulk compound characterisation.

## 5. Research relevance
Hassium has no commercial or technological use. It is important for testing group-8 periodic trends, relativistic chemistry and gas-phase behaviour of transactinoid elements. Experiments require accelerator-produced isotopes and rapid chemical systems operating on timescales compatible with seconds-long half-lives.

## 6. Radiological safety
Hassium isotopes are radioactive and extremely short-lived. Research must take place in specialised licensed nuclear facilities with contamination controls, appropriate shielding and daughter-product monitoring. No bulk material handling procedures exist.

## 7. Discovery and nomenclature
Hassium was first reported in 1984 by a team at the GSI laboratory in Darmstadt, Germany. The name derives from *Hassia*, the Latin name for the German state of Hesse, where Darmstadt is located. IUPAC adopted the name in 1997.

## 8. Evidence quality and unresolved work
- **Atomic structure:** PDG 2024 review of NIST/atomic-theory data; a specialist edition should preserve theoretical versus measured status.
- **Nuclear data:** CIAAW/NUBASE2020 half-lives; use latest ENSDF data for decay chains and uncertainties.
- **Physical properties:** no bulk measurement; melting point, density and boiling point remain unknown.
- **Pending:** reproducibility and uncertainty of HsO₄ formation; adsorption enthalpy; Hs(IV/VIII) oxidation chemistry; improved isotope half-life and decay data.
