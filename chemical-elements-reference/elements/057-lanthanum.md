# Element 57: Lanthanum

**Symbol:** La  
**Atomic number:** 57  
**Group / period / block:** 3 / 6 / d (often grouped with the lanthanoids)  
**Standard atomic weight:** 138.905 47(7)  
**Ground-state configuration:** [Xe] 5d 6s²  
**Ground-state term:** ²D₃/₂  
**First ionization energy:** 5.5769 eV  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; spectroscopic level catalogue, isotope evaluation and phase-property audit remain open.

## 1. Electronic structure and periodic position
Lanthanum begins the lanthanoid series by atomic-number convention, although its ground-state electron occupies 5d rather than 4f. Its neutral ground configuration is [Xe]5d6s² and the ground term is ²D₃/₂. The +3 oxidation state is overwhelmingly characteristic in ordinary compounds, producing La³⁺ with a xenon-like closed-shell core. This electronic configuration is an important reminder that simple filling-order diagrams do not fully describe observed ground states.

## 2. Isotopes
Natural lanthanum consists principally of stable ¹³⁹La, with a very small fraction of primordial ¹³⁸La, which is radioactive and has an extremely long half-life (about 10¹¹ years). ¹³⁸La decays by electron capture and beta-minus channels. Its branching fractions and evaluated half-life should be taken from ENSDF/NuDat. CIAAW flags geological isotopic variation relevant to the standard atomic weight.

## 3. Metallic and solid-state properties
Lanthanum is a silvery, reactive rare-earth metal. At ambient conditions its crystal structure is commonly reported as double-hexagonal close-packed (dhcp), with pressure- and temperature-dependent structural transitions. A phase-specific source is required before assigning a single crystal structure or density to all conditions. Lanthanum is paramagnetic over ordinary conditions, with magnetic response dependent on temperature and sample history.

## 4. Coordination chemistry and compounds
La³⁺ is a large, hard Lewis acid with high coordination numbers and a strong preference for oxygen-donor ligands. Its chemistry is dominated by ionic bonding and coordination compounds, including oxides, halides, nitrates and organolanthanum complexes. La₂O₃ is a basic oxide and readily reacts with water and atmospheric CO₂; surface hydration and carbonation can alter measured properties.

## 5. Materials science and applications
Lanthanum is used in optical glass, catalysts, hydrogen-storage alloys and nickel-metal hydride battery materials. LaNi₅ is a classic intermetallic hydrogen-storage compound; its absorption/desorption behavior depends on pressure, temperature, activation and cycling. Lanthanum additions also modify perovskite oxides and other functional ceramics. These engineered properties must be assigned to the specific compound or alloy, not elemental La.

## 6. Lanthanoid contraction context
Across the lanthanoid series, increasing nuclear charge is imperfectly screened by 4f electrons, producing a progressive decrease in ionic radii. Lanthanum provides the early-series reference point; comparisons must specify oxidation state and coordination number. The detailed contraction trend and its consequences for bonding and separation chemistry require ionic-radius datasets with consistent conventions.
