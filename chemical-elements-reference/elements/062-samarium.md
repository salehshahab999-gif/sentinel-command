# Element 62: Samarium

**Symbol:** Sm | **Atomic number:** 62 | **Group / period / block:** lanthanoid / 6 / f  
**Standard atomic weight:** 150.36(2)  
**Ground-state configuration:** [Xe] 4f⁶ 6s²  
**Ground-state term:** ⁷F₀  
**First ionization energy:** 5.6437 eV  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; full isotope, spectroscopy and magnetic-property audit remains open.

## Electronic structure
Samarium has a [Xe]4f⁶6s² neutral ground configuration and a ⁷F₀ ground term. The +3 state is common, while +2 is also chemically significant and can be stabilized in selected halides, chalcogenides and intermetallic phases. Sm²⁺ and Sm³⁺ have different 4f occupancies and magnetic responses.

## Isotopic composition
Natural samarium contains multiple isotopes, including ¹⁴⁴Sm, ¹⁴⁷Sm, ¹⁴⁸Sm, ¹⁴⁹Sm, ¹⁵⁰Sm, ¹⁵²Sm and ¹⁵⁴Sm. ¹⁴⁷Sm and ¹⁴⁸Sm are long-lived alpha emitters; ¹⁴⁹Sm has an exceptionally large thermal-neutron capture cross section and is important in reactor physics and the Oklo natural-reactor record. Isotope fractions, decay constants and neutron cross sections must be drawn from CIAAW, ENSDF and evaluated nuclear-data libraries with stated versions.

## Magnetism and condensed matter
Samarium metal and its compounds exhibit complex magnetic behavior. The 4f⁶ configuration of Sm²⁺ and 4f⁵ of Sm³⁺ lead to different multiplet structures; crystal-field effects and exchange coupling can produce nontrivial temperature dependence. Magnetic susceptibility should never be quoted without temperature and phase.

## Chemistry and applications
Sm₂O₃ and other Sm(III) compounds are common. SmCo₅ and Sm₂Co₁₇ are high-performance permanent-magnet phases with high coercivity and useful high-temperature behavior. Samarium compounds are also used in optical materials, catalysts and neutron-absorbing applications. Sm-149's neutron absorption is isotope-specific and must not be attributed indiscriminately to natural bulk samarium without composition data.

## Verification priorities
