# Element 101: Mendelevium

**Symbol:** Md  
**Atomic number:** 101  
**Group / period / block:** actinoid / 7 / f  
**Standard atomic weight:** none; representative mass number [258]  
**Neutral ground-state configuration:** [Rn] 5f¹³ 7s²  
**Ground-state term:** ²F°₇/₂  
**First ionization energy:** approximately 6.58 eV (PDG 2024 review of NIST data)  
**Representative isotope:** ²⁵⁸Md  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; only trace-scale chemistry and nuclear data are available.

## 1. Atomic and electronic structure
Mendelevium is a synthetic actinoid with neutral ground configuration [Rn]5f¹³7s² and ground term ²F°₇/₂. The +3 oxidation state is common, while +2 can be stabilised in solution and certain compounds. The filled-shell limit at nobelium makes the Md/No sequence important for understanding the final filling of the 5f shell. Atomic properties are based on sparse spectroscopic evidence and current compilations should be versioned.

## 2. Nuclear properties and isotopes
Mendelevium has no stable isotopes and no standard atomic weight. [258] is a representative mass number. ²⁵⁸Md has a half-life of approximately 51.6 days; other isotopes have shorter half-lives or different decay modes. Isotope-specific nuclear level, decay and mass data must be retrieved from current ENSDF/NuDat evaluations. Production and detection occur atom-by-atom or at very low atom counts.

## 3. Physical properties and measurement limits
No bulk metal sample of mendelevium has been prepared for routine physical-property measurement. Density, melting point, boiling point, crystal structure and transport coefficients are therefore unknown or theoretical. Any tabulated estimates should clearly state the computational model and should not be presented as measured constants.

## 4. Chemical behaviour
Mendelevium chemistry is studied at tracer scale. The +3 state is common, and the +2 state is unusually accessible for a late actinoid. Redox behaviour helps distinguish Md from neighbouring actinoids and provides evidence about 5f electron localisation. Solvent, counterion, pH, radiolysis and adsorption to container surfaces can influence experimental results.

## 5. Research relevance
Mendelevium has no commercial or technological applications. Its importance is fundamental actinoid chemistry, nuclear structure and the periodic trends of the heaviest elements. Chemical experiments use extremely small numbers of atoms and require specialised radiochemical separation and detection.

## 6. Radiological safety
Mendelevium is radioactive and can only be handled in specialist facilities. Internal contamination is a key concern for alpha-emitting isotopes. Work requires licensed controls, containment, contamination monitoring and isotope-specific dosimetry. There are no ordinary bulk-metal handling conditions.

## 7. Discovery and nomenclature
Mendelevium was first synthesised in 1955 by Albert Ghiorso, Bernard Harvey, Gregory Choppin, Stanley Thompson and Glenn Seaborg at Berkeley. It was named in honour of Dmitri Mendeleev, creator of the periodic table.
