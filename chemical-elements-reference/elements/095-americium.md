# Element 95: Americium

**Symbol:** Am  
**Atomic number:** 95  
**Group / period / block:** actinoid / 7 / f  
**Standard atomic weight:** none; representative mass number [243]  
**Neutral ground-state configuration:** [Rn] 5f⁷ 7s²  
**Ground-state term:** ⁸S°₇/₂  
**First ionization energy:** 5.9738 eV (NIST Atomic Data Handbook)  
**Important isotopes:** ²⁴¹Am and ²⁴³Am  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; isotope-specific nuclear, radiological and materials data require further audit.

## 1. Atomic and electronic structure
Americium is a transuranium actinoid with neutral ground configuration [Rn]5f⁷7s² and ground term ⁸S°₇/₂. The common oxidation state is +3, although +4, +5 and +6 compounds are also known. The 5f shell participates in bonding more readily than lanthanoid 4f electrons, giving americium a rich oxidation-state and coordination chemistry. In many aqueous environments Am(III) is the dominant state.

## 2. Nuclear properties and isotopes
Americium has no stable isotopes and therefore no standard atomic weight. The bracketed mass number [243] is a representative mass, not an atomic weight. ²⁴¹Am has a half-life of approximately 432.2 years and decays predominantly by alpha emission; ²⁴³Am has a half-life of about 7.37×10³ years. Both are important in actinide chemistry and nuclear-material reference work. The exact decay branches, alpha energies and gamma emissions must be taken from current ENSDF/NuDat data.

## 3. Physical and condensed-matter properties
Americium is a silvery radioactive metal. The RSC compilation lists melting point 1176 °C, boiling point 2011 °C and density about 12 g cm⁻³. Its crystal structure and density depend on temperature, pressure and phase. Oxidation and self-irradiation can alter surface conditions and sample properties; material measurements should specify isotope composition, sample age and containment.

## 4. Chemical behaviour
Americium commonly forms Am(III) compounds, including oxides, halides and coordination complexes. Am(IV), Am(V) and Am(VI) are accessible in selected solid-state and aqueous conditions. The chemistry of Am is relevant to actinide separation and waste-management research because oxidation state and complexation affect solubility, adsorption and migration. In carbonate-bearing waters, carbonate complexes can influence americium mobility.

## 5. Applications and research relevance
Americium-241 is used in some sealed ionisation-type smoke detectors, where a very small source provides ionisation current in a controlled assembly. It is also used in selected industrial gauges and neutron-source systems when combined with appropriate materials. These applications are isotope- and device-specific and do not imply that bulk americium metal is a routine material. Americium compounds are also important in actinide chemistry and the synthesis of heavier elements.

## 6. Radiological and chemical safety
Americium is radioactive and chemically toxic. Alpha emission is the principal radiation hazard for internal contamination; inhaled or ingested material can deliver a significant dose. Sealed sources must remain intact and are managed under applicable regulations. Laboratory and industrial handling of unsealed americium requires licensed facilities, radiological containment, contamination monitoring and isotope-specific dosimetry.

## 7. Discovery and nomenclature
Americium was first synthesised in 1944 by Glenn Seaborg and collaborators at the University of California, Berkeley, through nuclear reactions involving plutonium. It was named after the Americas, by analogy with europium's name for Europe. Its discovery extended the actinoid series and helped establish the chemistry of transuranium elements.
