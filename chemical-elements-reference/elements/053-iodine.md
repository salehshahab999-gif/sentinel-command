# Element 53: Iodine

**Symbol:** I  
**Atomic number:** 53  
**Group / period / block:** 17 / 5 / p  
**Standard atomic weight:** 126.904 47(3)  
**Ground-state electron configuration:** [Kr] 4d¹⁰ 5s² 5p⁵  
**Ground-state term:** ²P°₃/₂  
**First ionization energy:** 10.4513 eV  
**Ambient molecular solid:** orthorhombic molecular crystal, built from I₂ units  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; nuclear, spectroscopic and thermodynamic details need full source-level audit.

## 1. Atomic and quantum properties
Iodine is a heavy halogen with a 5p⁵ valence shell. Its neutral atomic ground state is ²P°₃/₂; the spin-orbit split ²P°₁/₂ level is a key feature of its atomic spectrum. The first ionization energy is 10.4513 eV in the PDG electronic-structure compilation. Detailed transition wavelengths, oscillator strengths, hyperfine/isotope effects and level uncertainties should be extracted directly from the current NIST ASD.

## 2. Nuclear composition
Naturally occurring iodine is effectively monoisotopic: CIAAW lists **¹²⁷I with representative amount fraction 1** and standard atomic weight 126.904 47(3). ¹²⁷I is stable. Radioiodine nuclides such as ¹²⁹I and ¹³¹I are important in environmental monitoring, nuclear medicine and reactor studies, but are not part of the stable natural isotope abundance entry. Their half-lives, decay schemes, emission probabilities and evaluated uncertainties must be quoted from current ENSDF/NuDat records, not inferred from clinical shorthand.

## 3. Condensed phase and thermophysical behavior
At ordinary pressure, solid iodine is a molecular crystal composed of I₂ molecules; it sublimes appreciably and produces a violet vapor. Its orthorhombic crystal structure and weak intermolecular cohesion distinguish it from extended-covalent solids. Approximate normal-pressure melting and boiling points are 386.9 K and 457.4 K. Reported values should specify pressure and purity; sublimation enthalpy and vapor-pressure correlations require a defined temperature interval.

## 4. Chemical bonding and redox chemistry
Iodine exhibits oxidation states from −1 to +7, with −1, +1, +3, +5 and +7 relevant in different compounds. Iodide (I⁻), molecular iodine (I₂), triiodide (I₃⁻), iodate (IO₃⁻) and periodate species have distinct thermodynamic and kinetic behavior. Iodine's redox chemistry is strongly medium-dependent; standard potentials must identify the exact half-reaction, solvent, ionic strength, pH and reference electrode.

## 5. Isotopes and applications
¹³¹I is a beta/gamma-emitting radionuclide used in thyroid diagnostics and therapy under regulated medical protocols; ¹²⁹I is a long-lived fission and activation product used as an environmental tracer. These applications require isotope-specific dosimetry and radiochemical purity. Stable iodine nutrition and radioactive iodine exposure are separate topics and must not be conflated.
