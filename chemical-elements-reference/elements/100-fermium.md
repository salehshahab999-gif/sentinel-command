# Element 100: Fermium

**Symbol:** Fm  
**Atomic number:** 100  
**Group / period / block:** actinoid / 7 / f  
**Standard atomic weight:** none; representative mass number [257]  
**Neutral ground-state configuration:** [Rn] 5f¹² 7s²  
**Ground-state term:** ³H₆  
**First ionization energy:** approximately 6.50 eV (PDG 2024 review of NIST data)  
**Representative longest-lived isotope:** ²⁵⁷Fm  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; bulk material properties are not established at high confidence.

## 1. Atomic and electronic structure
Fermium is a synthetic actinoid with neutral ground configuration [Rn]5f¹²7s² and ground term ³H₆. Its chemistry is expected to be dominated by the +3 oxidation state, with +2 observed or predicted under selected reducing conditions. The 5f electronic shell is strongly correlated, and actinoid relativistic effects contribute to the ordering of atomic states. Ground-state data should be linked to the specific NIST/PDG compilation used.

## 2. Nuclear properties and isotopes
Fermium has no stable isotopes and no standard atomic weight. [257] is a representative mass number, not a standard atomic weight. ²⁵⁷Fm is the longest-lived commonly referenced isotope, with a half-life of approximately 100.5 days. Other isotopes include ²⁵³Fm (about 3 days) and ²⁵⁵Fm (about 20 hours). Decay modes and half-lives differ by isotope and must be checked against current ENSDF/NuDat. Production quantities are extremely small and source age is important for sample accounting.

## 3. Physical properties and measurement limits
No macroscopic sample of fermium has been prepared for ordinary bulk property measurement. Melting point, density, thermal conductivity, elastic constants and phase diagram remain uncertain or theoretical. Some periodic-table datasets give predicted melting values, but they are not direct measurements and may differ substantially by computational model. The correct reference status for most bulk properties is therefore “not experimentally established.”

## 4. Chemical behaviour
Fermium is generally expected to form Fm(III) in aqueous and solid-state chemistry. Fm(II) can be stabilised in selected reducing environments, and its behaviour is important for understanding the late actinoid sequence. Chemical experiments are conducted at tracer scale and must account for radiolysis, adsorption losses and decay during separation. Stability constants and redox potentials have larger uncertainties than for common elements.

## 5. Research relevance
Fermium has no commercial or routine industrial use. Its importance is in fundamental actinoid chemistry and as a product or target in studies of heavier nuclei. The small number of atoms available makes each experimental result valuable but difficult to reproduce. Claims about physical or chemical behaviour should specify isotope, carrier conditions and experimental method.

## 6. Radiological safety
Fermium is radioactive and requires specialist radiochemical facilities. Internal contamination is the central hazard for alpha-emitting isotopes. Containment, contamination monitoring, remote handling and isotope-specific dose assessment are essential. No routine laboratory handling assumptions apply.

## 7. Discovery and nomenclature
Fermium was identified in 1952 in debris from the first thermonuclear test and later confirmed in laboratory work. It was named after physicist Enrico Fermi. Its discovery, together with einsteinium, opened a new period in the study of the heaviest actinoids.
