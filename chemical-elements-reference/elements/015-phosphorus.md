# Element 015: Phosphorus

**Symbol:** P  
**Atomic number:** 15  
**Group:** 15  
**Period:** 3  
**Block:** p-block  
**Classification:** Nonmetal  
**Standard atomic weight (CIAAW 2024):** 30.973 761 998(5) [1]
**Ground-state electron configuration:** [Ne] 3s² 3p³   [3]
**Ground-state term symbol:** ⁴S°₃/₂ [3]  
**Physical state at approximately 298 K and 1 bar:** Solid [4]

## 1. Atomic and periodic context
Phosphorus is element 15. Its valence configuration, [Ne] 3s² 3p³, provides the first-order framework for understanding bonding, oxidation-state preferences, and periodic trends. Group trends are guides rather than substitutes for compound-specific thermodynamic and spectroscopic evidence.

## 2. Isotopic and nuclear context
Natural phosphorus is monoisotopic ³¹P. White phosphorus is pyrophoric and highly toxic; red and other allotropes have different reactivities. Phosphorus is an essential component of nucleic acids, ATP, and biological membranes. The standard atomic weight is not a nuclide mass and must not be used as a substitute for isotope-specific nuclear data. A complete nuclear appendix will report nuclide masses, natural abundances, decay modes, and half-lives separately.

## 3. Chemical and materials significance
Phosphate fertilizers, food and biological chemistry, detergents, flame retardants, and specialty chemicals are key application domains. [4]

## 4. Data quality and interpretation
The standard atomic weight is taken from the CIAAW 2024 table. An interval denotes the range expected for normal terrestrial materials; parenthetical digits indicate uncertainty in the final quoted digits. The electron configuration is the conventional ground-state configuration. Physical state is stated at approximately 298 K and 1 bar.
