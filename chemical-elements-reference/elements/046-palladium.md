## 1. Atomic structure and quantum properties
Palladium is a period-5 d-block element. Its tabulated ground-state electron configuration is [Kr] 4d¹⁰. ## 3. Physical properties and phase behaviour
- **Melting point:** 1828 K (1554.8 °C)
- **Boiling point:** 3236 K (2963 °C)
- **Density:** 12.0 g cm⁻³
- **Ambient crystal structure:** fcc, space group Fm-3m (No. 225) under ambient conditions

These are reference values, not a critical thermodynamic compilation. Density and transition temperatures depend on purity, porosity, isotopic composition where relevant, pressure, and measurement convention. The hcp/fcc assignments describe the ambient elemental metal, not all compounds or metastable thin-film phases.

## 4. Chemical thermodynamics, oxidation states, and coordination chemistry
Palladium chemistry is dominated by Pd(0) and Pd(II), with Pd(IV) accessible in selected systems. Pd(0)/Pd(II) interconversion underpins oxidative addition, migratory insertion, and reductive elimination in cross-coupling catalysis. Palladium absorbs hydrogen into interstitial sites, forming α and β hydride phases over composition- and temperature-dependent ranges; this behaviour is important in hydrogen separation, storage research, and materials science.

A final thermodynamic dataset should provide standard-state formation enthalpies, Gibbs energies, entropies, heat capacities, redox potentials, aqueous speciation constants, and temperature/pH dependence with traceable primary or critically evaluated sources. No unverified numerical thermodynamic constants are inserted in this draft.

## 5. Materials science and catalytic function
Palladium is extensively used in catalytic converters and chemical catalysis, including carbon-carbon and carbon-heteroatom cross-coupling reactions such as Suzuki-Miyaura, Heck, and Sonogashira chemistry. Other applications include hydrogen purification membranes, electrical contacts, multilayer ceramic capacitor electrodes, dental alloys, and jewellery. Catalyst performance depends on particle size, support, oxidation state, and reaction environment.

For engineering use, catalytic activity must be reported with reaction conditions, support composition, loading, particle-size distribution, space velocity, feed composition, conversion, selectivity, and deactivation history. Bulk elemental properties alone cannot predict supported-catalyst performance.

## 6. Toxicology, radiological safety, and handling
Palladium metal and palladium compounds have different hazard profiles. Some soluble palladium salts can cause skin and respiratory sensitization; catalyst powders and process residues require dust control. Exposure assessment should be compound-specific and should distinguish metallic palladium from soluble salts and supported catalysts.
