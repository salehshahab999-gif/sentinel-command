## 1. Atomic and electronic structure
Antimony is a group-15 pnictogen with a half-filled 5p subshell in its neutral ground state. The tabulated ground term is ⁴S°₃/₂. Relativistic and spin-orbit effects become increasingly relevant down the p block; a full spectroscopic appendix should extract level energies, classifications, transition probabilities and uncertainties from the current NIST Atomic Spectra Database (ASD), rather than extrapolating from the compact periodic-table entry.

## 2. Nuclear composition
CIAAW's 2024 representative isotopic composition lists two naturally occurring stable nuclides: **¹²¹Sb = 0.5721(5)** and **¹²³Sb = 0.4279(5)**. The CIAAW standard atomic weight is 121.760(1). These are evaluated representative terrestrial values; isotopic composition can vary in exceptional geological or processed materials. Nuclear spins, magnetic moments, excited-state schemes and neutron-capture data require isotope-specific evaluation through ENSDF and specialist nuclear databases.

## 3. Condensed-matter and thermophysical properties
At ambient conditions, elemental antimony adopts the A7 rhombohedral structure, a Peierls-distorted structure related to a simple-cubic network. This structure is central to its semimetallic electronic character. Commonly tabulated bulk values place its melting point near 903.8 K and boiling point near 1860 K; values depend on pressure and on the adopted reference compilation. A final data table should state measurement temperature, pressure, purity and uncertainty for density, heat capacity, thermal conductivity, elastic constants and electrical transport.

## 4. Chemical bonding and speciation
Common oxidation states are −3, +3 and +5, with +3 and +5 prominent in inorganic chemistry. Sb(III) compounds often display a stereochemically active 5s² lone pair; Sb(V) chemistry includes oxo-species such as antimonates. The chemistry of Sb(III)/Sb(V) depends strongly on pH, redox potential and ligand environment. Environmental assessments must distinguish dissolved species, particulate phases and mineral-bound antimony; total concentration alone is not a speciation model.

## 5. Materials and applications
Antimony is used in lead-alloy hardening, selected semiconductor materials and flame-retardant systems (often as synergistic additives rather than as a stand-alone flame retardant). Materials performance depends on phase composition and microstructure. Toxicological and environmental handling requires attention to compound-specific hazards; properties of elemental Sb must not be transferred indiscriminately to all antimony compounds.
