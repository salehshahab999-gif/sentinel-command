# Element 022: Titanium

**Symbol:** Ti  
**Atomic number:** 22  
**Group:** 4  
**Period:** 4  
**Block:** d-block  
**Classification:** Transition metal  
**Standard atomic weight (CIAAW 2024):** 47.867(1) [1]
**Ground-state electron configuration:** [Ar] 3d² 4s² [3]  
**Ground-state term symbol:** ³F₂ [3]
**Physical state at approximately 298 K and 1 bar:** Solid [4]

## 1. Atomic and periodic context
Titanium is element 22. Its ground-state electron configuration, [Ar] 3d² 4s², is the conventional starting point for interpreting valence, bonding, and periodic behavior. Transition-metal chemistry is particularly sensitive to oxidation state, coordination environment, ligand field, and phase.

## 2. Isotopic and nuclear context
Titanium occurs naturally as five stable isotopes. Its high strength-to-weight ratio and passivating oxide film underpin its engineering value. Fine titanium powders can be combustible. Standard atomic weight summarizes naturally occurring material and is not interchangeable with the mass of any individual nuclide. Isotope-specific masses, abundances, and decay data belong in the dedicated nuclear-data tables.

## 3. Chemical and materials significance
Aerospace alloys, chemical-process equipment, biomedical implants, pigments (TiO₂), and corrosion-resistant components are major uses.

## 4. Data quality and interpretation
The standard atomic weight is reproduced from the CIAAW 2024 table. Parenthetical digits indicate uncertainty in the final quoted digits. The electron configuration is the conventional ground-state configuration. Physical state is stated at approximately 298 K and 1 bar.
