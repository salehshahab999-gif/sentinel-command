# Element 017: Chlorine

**Symbol:** Cl  
**Atomic number:** 17  
**Group:** 17  
**Period:** 3  
**Block:** p-block  
**Classification:** Halogen  
**Standard atomic weight (CIAAW 2024):** [35.446, 35.457]  
**Ground-state electron configuration:** [Ne] 3s² 3p⁵  
**Physical state at approximately 298 K and 1 bar:** Gas

## 1. Atomic and periodic context
Chlorine is element 17. Its valence configuration, [Ne] 3s² 3p⁵, provides the first-order framework for understanding bonding, oxidation-state preferences, and periodic trends. Group trends are guides rather than substitutes for compound-specific thermodynamic and spectroscopic evidence.

## 2. Isotopic and nuclear context
The stable isotopes are ³⁵Cl and ³⁷Cl. Chlorine gas is toxic and corrosive; risk assessment must distinguish elemental chlorine from chloride salts and organochlorine compounds. The standard atomic weight is not a nuclide mass and must not be used as a substitute for isotope-specific nuclear data. A complete nuclear appendix will report nuclide masses, natural abundances, decay modes, and half-lives separately.

## 3. Chemical and materials significance
Disinfection of drinking water and pools, production of PVC and other chlorinated chemicals, bleaching, and industrial synthesis are major applications.

## 4. Data quality and interpretation
The standard atomic weight is taken from the CIAAW 2024 table. An interval denotes the range expected for normal terrestrial materials; parenthetical digits indicate uncertainty in the final quoted digits. The electron configuration is the conventional ground-state configuration. Physical state is stated at approximately 298 K and 1 bar.
