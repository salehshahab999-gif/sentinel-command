# Element 111: Roentgenium

**Symbol:** Rg  
**Atomic number:** 111  
**Group / period / block:** 11 / 7 / d  
**Standard atomic weight:** none; representative mass number [282]  
**Predicted valence configuration:** [Rn] 5f¹⁴ 6d⁹ 7s² (model-dependent)  
**Compiled ground-state term:** ²S₁/₂  
**First ionization energy:** not experimentally established; theoretical estimates are model-dependent  
**Representative isotope:** ²⁸²Rg  
**Evidence status:** structured research draft; no bulk physical properties or established experimental chemical data.

## 1. Atomic and electronic structure
Roentgenium is a transactinoid in group 11, below copper, silver and gold. Relativistic atomic calculations predict a valence configuration based on 6d and 7s orbitals, commonly represented as [Rn]5f¹⁴6d⁹7s², while the compiled ground-state term is ²S₁/₂. The electronic structure is strongly affected by relativistic and spin-orbit effects. Because no high-resolution atomic spectrum is available, configuration and term assignments should be treated as theoretical or compiled rather than as a complete spectroscopic measurement.

## 2. Nuclear properties and isotopes
Roentgenium has no stable isotopes and no standard atomic weight. [282] is a representative mass number. CIAAW/NUBASE2020 list ²⁸²Rg with a half-life of 130(50) seconds. Other isotopes have shorter or different decay chains. Exact half-life, alpha energies, spontaneous-fission branches and daughter assignments should be obtained from current ENSDF/NuDat evaluations.

## 3. Physical properties and measurement limits
No macroscopic sample of roentgenium has been produced. Density, melting point, boiling point, crystal structure, thermal conductivity and mechanical constants are unknown experimentally. Periodic trend projections are theoretical and should not be presented as measured properties. Single-atom production and radioactive decay detection are the only available experimental modes.

## 4. Chemical behaviour
No conclusive chemical experiment has established roentgenium's bulk or atom-at-a-time chemistry. Group-11 behaviour and possible +1 or +3 oxidation states are theoretical expectations based on periodic trends and relativistic calculations. These predictions require experimental verification and should be clearly labelled as such.

## 5. Research relevance
Roentgenium has no commercial or technological use. It is studied for nuclear stability, relativistic electronic structure and the periodic trends of the heaviest group-11 element. Experiments rely on heavy-ion accelerators, recoil separators and correlated decay-chain detection.

## 6. Radiological safety
Roentgenium isotopes are radioactive and short-lived. Work is restricted to specialised accelerator facilities with licensed radiological controls, contamination monitoring and isotope-specific dosimetry. No ordinary chemical handling or bulk-material safety data exist.

## 7. Discovery and nomenclature
Roentgenium was first reported by a team at GSI Darmstadt in 1994 and later confirmed through additional experiments. IUPAC approved the name in 2004, honouring Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen, discoverer of X-rays.

## 8. Evidence quality and unresolved work
- **Atomic structure:** PDG/NIST compilations provide the ground-term assignment; detailed atomic spectroscopy is not available.

- **Physical and chemical properties:** no direct bulk data; group-11 chemistry is theoretical.
