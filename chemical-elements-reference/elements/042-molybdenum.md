# Element 42: Molybdenum

**Symbol:** Mo  
**Atomic number:** 42  
**Group:** 6  
**Period:** 5  
**Block:** d-block  
**Standard atomic weight (CIAAW 2024):** 95.95(1)  
**Ground-state electron configuration:** [Kr] 4d⁵ 5s¹  
**Ground-state term (NIST tabulation):** ⁷S₃  
**First ionization energy (NIST tabulation):** 7.0924 eV  
**State at 20 °C and 1 bar:** Solid

## 1. Atomic and periodic context
Molybdenum is a period-5 d-block transition metal. Its ground-state configuration is [Kr] 4d⁵ 5s¹. The tabulated ground-state term and first ionization energy are reproduced from the NIST periodic-table compilation; the current NIST Atomic Spectra Database should be used for a full level-by-level spectroscopic treatment.

## 2. Isotopic and nuclear data
CIAAW representative natural isotopic abundances are: ⁹²Mo 0.14649(106), ⁹⁴Mo 0.09187(33), ⁹⁵Mo 0.15873(30), ⁹⁶Mo 0.16673(8), ⁹⁷Mo 0.09582(15), ⁹⁸Mo 0.24292(80), and ¹⁰⁰Mo 0.09744(65). ¹⁰⁰Mo is a naturally occurring, extremely long-lived double-beta-minus emitter (half-life approximately 7.1×10¹⁸ years). ⁹²Mo is observationally stable; double-beta-plus/electron-capture decay has been investigated, but no decay has been observed and only lower limits on its half-life are established. The other listed isotopes are stable for practical purposes. The standard atomic weight is 95.95(1).

## 3. Physical and thermodynamic properties
- **Melting point:** 2895 K (2622 °C)
- **Boiling point:** 4912 K (4639 °C)
- **Density:** 10.2 g cm⁻³ (RSC reference value; temperature and pressure conventions should be checked against the underlying handbook before precision engineering use)
- **Crystal structure at ambient conditions:** Molybdenum is body-centred cubic (bcc) at ambient conditions. It has a very high melting point and retains useful strength at elevated temperatures, although oxidation in air becomes significant at high temperature.

The values above are reference values, not a critical evaluation of all measurements. High-temperature phase transitions, purity, defects, and interstitial contamination can materially alter measured behaviour.

## 4. Chemical behaviour and bonding
Molybdenum displays a wide range of oxidation states, from negative or low formal states in selected complexes to +6 in molybdates and MoO₃. Molybdenum forms refractory carbides, nitrides, sulfides, and oxides. Molybdenum disulfide (MoS₂) is a layered solid lubricant; molybdenum cofactors are also central to several biological enzymes.

## 5. Materials science and applications
Most molybdenum is used as an alloying addition in steels and superalloys to improve strength, hardenability, and resistance to corrosion and high-temperature creep. It is also used in catalysts, electrodes, furnace components, lubricants, and as a micronutrient in biological and agricultural systems.

## 6. Toxicology, safety, and handling
Molybdenum metal has relatively low acute toxicity compared with many metal compounds, but occupational exposure to dust and soluble molybdenum compounds should be controlled. Excessive exposure can disturb copper metabolism; toxicity depends on chemical species, route, and dose.
