# Elon Musk Financing Network
## Consolidated public-record research report

**Research snapshot:** 5 November 2026  
**Period in focus:** 2015–2017, with earlier background where directly documented  
**Status:** Initial baseline; not a complete reconstruction of all private credit arrangements

---

## 1. Executive summary

The records reviewed so far reveal two distinct directions of financing:

- **Financing received by Musk personally or by his revocable trust**, including securities-backed or margin-style credit from banks.
- **Financing received by companies in the Musk orbit**, including corporate credit facilities and debt securities purchased by Musk, his trust, or SpaceX.

These flows must not be conflated. A bank's corporate loan to Tesla or SolarCity does not establish that it also extended personal credit to Musk. Likewise, a bond purchase by SpaceX is not a personal loan by Musk.

## 2. Personal credit to Elon Musk and the revocable trust

### 2.1 August 2015 disclosure

Tesla's August 2015 prospectus supplement identifies two direct credit relationships:

**Goldman Sachs Bank USA**
- Legal lender: Goldman Sachs Bank USA.
- Borrowers: Elon Musk and the Elon Musk Revocable Trust dated 22 July 2003.
- Disclosed aggregate credit extensions: $275 million.
- Relationship began: June 2011.
- Purpose disclosed in part: some proceeds were used by Musk to purchase Tesla shares in public offerings and private placements.
- Security: pledges included Tesla shares and shares of unrelated entities owned by Musk and the trust.
- Recourse: the filing describes the loans as full recourse against Musk and the trust.

**Morgan Stanley Smith Barney LLC**
- Legal lender: Morgan Stanley Smith Barney LLC.
- Borrower: Elon Musk.
- Disclosed aggregate loan: $200 million.
- Security: Tesla shares and shares of unrelated entities owned by Musk and the trust.
- Recourse: full recourse, according to the filing.

Tesla expressly stated that it was not a party to these personal loans and that their terms were negotiated directly between Musk and the respective lending entities.

Primary source: [Tesla 2015 prospectus supplement, filed August 2015](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1318605/000119312515288963/d84889d424b5.htm).

### 2.2 May 2016 disclosure

Tesla's May 2016 prospectus supplement provides a more detailed update:

- Morgan Stanley Smith Barney LLC had made various extensions of credit to Musk beginning in May 2011.
- After giving effect to a further $36.5 million borrowing used to pay the exercise price for stock options, the outstanding balance was expected to be approximately **$299.0 million**.
- Goldman Sachs Bank USA had made credit extensions to Musk and the trust beginning in June 2011, but Musk had **no outstanding borrowing** with Goldman at the time of the filing.
- In addition, Musk had received a loan of approximately **$187.0 million from another financial institution**.
- The filing says this other financial institution was **not involved in the offering**.
- The disclosed loans were secured by portions of Tesla shares owned by Musk and the trust, plus shares of unrelated entities owned by them.
- The personal loans were full recourse; Tesla was not a party to them.

Primary source: [Tesla 2016 prospectus supplement, dated 18 May 2016](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1318605/000119312516594471/d185970d424b5.htm), especially the risk-factor disclosure concerning Musk's pledged shares and personal borrowing.

**Unresolved:** The identity of the financial institution behind the approximately $187 million loan is not disclosed in this filing.

### 2.3 March 2017 disclosure

Tesla's March 2017 prospectus supplement states:

- Morgan Stanley Smith Barney LLC's outstanding balance was approximately **$344.4 million as of 28 February 2017**.
- Goldman Sachs Bank USA had previously extended credit to Musk and the trust, but Musk had no outstanding borrowings with Goldman at the time of the filing.
- Musk also had loans of approximately **$279.9 million in the aggregate as of 28 February 2017 from other financial institutions**.
- Those other financial institutions were described as not involved in the offering.
- The loans were secured by pledges of Tesla common stock owned by Musk and the trust.
- The prospectus does not identify the institutions or give a lender-by-lender allocation.

Primary source: [Tesla 2017 prospectus supplement, dated 16 March 2017](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1318605/000119312517086221/d535691d424b5.htm).

### 2.4 What the two unnamed amounts do and do not establish

| Disclosure | What is explicit | What remains unknown |
|---|---|---|
| Approximately $187.0 million, May 2016 | One other financial institution; loan to Musk; not involved in that offering; collateral included Tesla and unrelated-company shares held by Musk and the trust | Institution's legal name, loan date, facility size, balance methodology, interest rate, maturity, exact collateral schedule |
| Approximately $279.9 million aggregate, 28 February 2017 | Loans from other financial institutions; aggregate amount; collateral included Tesla shares held by Musk and the trust; institutions not involved in that offering | Names, exact number of lenders, individual balances, whether the 2016 lender is included, refinancing or overlap |

The filings do **not** justify adding $187.0 million and $279.9 million. The $187.0 million is a May 2016 disclosure; $279.9 million is a later aggregate as of 28 February 2017. Until a contract, account statement, or later disclosure resolves the relationship, they remain separate dated observations.

## 3. JPMorgan Chase and Wells Fargo: corporate credit to Tesla

### 3.1 Tesla asset-based revolving credit facility

The original Tesla asset-based revolving credit facility was entered into on 10 June 2015. Tesla's August 2015 prospectus described an initial facility of up to $500 million, subject to conditions that could increase availability to $750 million.

On 9 February 2016, Tesla and Tesla Motors Netherlands B.V. entered into a third amendment increasing total revolving commitments to **$1.0 billion**.

The amendment identifies:
- Tesla Motors, Inc. and Tesla Motors Netherlands B.V. as borrowers.
- Deutsche Bank AG, New York Branch as Administrative Agent and Collateral Agent.
- JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. as an Issuing Lender and Lender.
- Wells Fargo Bank, National Association as an Issuing Lender and Lender.

The February 2016 commitment schedule states:

| Legal lender | Revolving commitment |
|---|---:|
| Deutsche Bank AG, New York Branch | $147.5 million |
| Goldman Sachs Bank USA | $175.0 million |
| Citibank, N.A. | $147.5 million |
| **JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A.** | **$147.5 million** |
| Morgan Stanley Senior Funding, Inc. | $147.5 million |
| Bank of America, N.A. | $110.0 million |
| **Wells Fargo Bank, National Association** | **$100.0 million** |
| Credit Suisse AG, Cayman Islands Branch | $25.0 million |
| **Total** | **$1.0 billion** |

The schedule records **commitments**, not amounts individually drawn by each bank. Tesla's 2016 prospectus separately reported that $565.0 million was outstanding under the overall facility as of 31 March 2016; it did not allocate that outstanding amount lender by lender.

Primary sources:
- [Tesla third amendment to ABL Credit Agreement, 9 February 2016](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1318605/000156459016013195/tsla-ex1028c_207.htm).
- [Tesla 2016 prospectus supplement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1318605/000119312516594471/d185970d424b5.htm).

### 3.2 What this establishes

- JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. was a direct corporate lender and issuing lender under Tesla's ABL facility.
- Wells Fargo Bank, National Association was also a direct corporate lender and issuing lender under the same facility.
- Their commitments were $147.5 million and $100 million, respectively.

### 3.3 What this does not establish

It does not establish that either bank lent money to Musk personally, the Elon Musk Revocable Trust, or the unnamed lender pool behind the $187.0 million and $279.9 million disclosures.

The 2015 prospectus lists **J.P. Morgan Securities LLC** and **Wells Fargo Securities, LLC** among the underwriters of the 2015 stock offering. Those are legal entities distinct from **JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A.** and **Wells Fargo Bank, National Association**. Corporate-family affiliation should not erase legal-entity distinctions.

**Current classification:** JPMorgan Chase and Wells Fargo are confirmed Tesla corporate lenders; direct personal lending to Musk remains unverified.

This is a **SolarCity corporate financing relationship**. It is not evidence that these institutions made personal loans to Musk.

### 4.2 SolarCity subsidiary term loan

Tesla's 2017 prospectus also reports that, in January 2016, a SolarCity subsidiary entered into a **$160 million term loan** with certain lenders, including Bank of America, N.A. The loan was secured by substantially all assets of the subsidiary, including interests in certain financing funds.

Primary source: [Tesla 2017 prospectus supplement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1318605/000119312517086221/d535691d424b5.htm), SolarCity financing disclosure.

The exact lender allocation and complete lender list for this subsidiary facility require further extraction from the underlying January 2016 agreement and amendments.

### 4.3 Important entity and role distinction

The April 2016 SolarCity amendment identifies the named lenders above. It does **not** list JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. or Wells Fargo Bank, National Association among the signed lenders shown in that amendment.

A prior working note suggested a Wells Fargo trustee/indenture role in a separate SolarCity bond instrument. That separate instrument has not yet been verified to the standard required for this report; it is therefore **not treated here as a confirmed SolarCity lending relationship**. The Tesla ABL role of Wells Fargo remains independently confirmed.

## 5. Debt securities purchased by Musk, the trust, and SpaceX

This is the reverse financing direction: money flowed to SolarCity from Musk, his trust, and SpaceX. These transactions are debt investments or notes, not bank loans to Musk.

### 5.1 Elon Musk Revocable Trust

In December 2015, SolarCity issued **$10 million principal amount of zero-coupon convertible senior notes due December 2020** to the Elon Musk Revocable Trust dated 22 July 2003. Tesla's 2017 proxy statement says the notes were issued on the same terms offered to an unaffiliated purchaser.

### 5.2 Elon Musk personally

In August 2016, SolarCity issued **$65 million of 6.50% Solar Bonds due February 2018** to Elon Musk, on the same terms and conditions offered to the public.

In April 2017, Musk agreed to exchange those bonds for a **$65 million promissory note bearing 6.5% interest**, on substantially identical terms, including remaining maturity.

### 5.3 SpaceX

Tesla's 2017 proxy statement reports that SpaceX purchased SolarCity 4.40% Solar Bonds:
- Approximately $90 million in March 2016, due March 2017.
- Approximately $75 million in June 2016, due June 2017.
- The March 2017 bonds held by SpaceX were repaid in full in March 2017.

These were purchases by SpaceX as a corporate entity. They should not be described as personal loans made by Musk.

Primary source for all three related-party disclosures: [Tesla 2017 definitive proxy statement, filed 27 April 2017](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1318605/000119312517130746/d318894ddef14a.htm), Related Party Transactions, pp. 46–47.

## 8. Litigation allegation: $475 million credit lines

A 6 September 2016 shareholder complaint alleged that Musk had $475 million in personal credit lines from Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley and had pledged Tesla and SolarCity shares.

This is a **plaintiff allegation in a complaint**, not a court finding. It is consistent in broad magnitude with the 2015 Tesla prospectus disclosure of $275 million from Goldman Sachs Bank USA and $200 million from Morgan Stanley Smith Barney LLC, but the complaint must not be presented as independent proof of the underlying loan contracts.

## 10. Corrections and unresolved issues carried forward

1. **Commitment is not drawdown.** The $147.5 million JPMorgan and $100 million Wells Fargo amounts are commitments under Tesla's $1 billion facility. The filing's $565 million figure is the total facility balance outstanding at 31 March 2016; it is not the amount drawn from either bank individually.
2. **Legal entities matter.** J.P. Morgan Securities LLC is not the same legal entity as JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A.; Wells Fargo Securities, LLC is not the same as Wells Fargo Bank, National Association.
3. **The $187 million and $279.9 million amounts are not added.**
4. **The unnamed lenders remain unnamed.** No bank is attributed to either amount without a primary document.
5. **SolarCity lender relationships are corporate.** They are not personal loans to Musk.
6. **Musk's SolarCity notes and bonds are debt investments.** They are not bank loans received by Musk.
7. **SpaceX bond purchases are corporate transactions.** They are not personal funds unless a separate source establishes that characterization.
8. **The Wells Fargo SolarCity trustee lead remains unverified** and is not included as a confirmed role in the institution matrix.
9. **The 2009 SpaceX loan lead remains outside the core period** pending document-level re-verification.

## 12. Research limitations

This report is a public-source baseline assembled from SEC filings, a shareholder complaint and court-record leads. It is not a bank-confirmed account history, an audit of private financial records, or a complete ledger of every Musk-related entity.

The SEC prospectuses are particularly important because they disclose the existence and broad collateral structure of personal loans while withholding the names of certain institutions. Public filings may not contain the private loan agreements, lender schedules, margin terms, or complete collateral records.

A missing name in the records reviewed is not proof that an institution had no relationship. Conversely, a bank's appearance in a corporate credit agreement is not proof of a personal loan to Musk.

**Publication rule:** Add a lender to the “confirmed personal lender” category only when a primary document or reliable court record explicitly connects the exact legal lending entity to Musk or the trust as borrower or guarantor.
