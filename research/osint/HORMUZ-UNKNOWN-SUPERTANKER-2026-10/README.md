**Case ID:** HORMUZ-UNKNOWN-SUPERTANKER-2026-10  
**Research date:** 2026-11-02  
**Language:** English  
**Method:** Open-source intelligence (OSINT)  
**Status:** Preliminary desk-research dossier

## Executive summary

On 10 October 2026, Iranian reporting attributed to the IRGC Navy said that a crude-oil supertanker carrying approximately or more than two million barrels struck a sea mine while attempting to leave the Strait of Hormuz through a route described by Iranian authorities as unauthorized. The statement said the vessel's navigation/positioning systems had been switched off. The vessel was not named, and the public statement did not provide an IMO number, MMSI, flag, owner, or independently verifiable coordinates.

Separately, UK maritime reporting described an outbound tanker whose master reported an impact on the port side by an unknown projectile, followed by a fire; the crew were reported safe. Available public reporting does **not yet establish conclusively that this UKMTO-reported event and the IRGC-reported mine incident are the same event**. They must remain separate evidence records until time, position, vessel particulars, and imagery can be reconciled.

**No vessel identity is assigned in this dossier.** No exact impact coordinate, mine attribution, or vessel track is claimed as independently verified.

## Evidence grading and cautions

- **High:** original official notice or raw data independently retrieved and reproducibly checked.
- **Medium:** attributable institutional statement or reputable reporting, with original underlying record not yet archived.
- **Low:** secondary interpretation, unattributed imagery, or approximate location.
- **Unverified:** identity, exact coordinates, weapon/mine mechanism, and linkage between the UKMTO and IRGC accounts.

Do not identify a vessel from capacity alone. Do not infer that AIS silence proves deliberate concealment. Do not treat a later AIS position as the impact point. Do not merge the 10 October report with other supertanker incidents reported later in October.

### Geolocation workflow for the next researcher
1. **Anchor the event:** obtain the original UKMTO notice and original Iranian statement/media. Extract the event time, publication time, exact wording, named geographic references, and any coordinate or distance statement. Keep event time separate from publication time.
2. **Define a search polygon:** only after the primary notice is archived, convert its stated location/range into a geographic polygon. Record the coordinate reference system (WGS84), derivation method, and uncertainty. Do not manufacture a point from a broad description.
3. **AIS candidate search:** query raw AIS messages for outbound crude-oil VLCC/ULCC candidates for 8–11 October 2026. Preserve message time and receiver time separately, plus MMSI, lat/lon, SOG, COG, heading, navigation status, provider, and reception gaps. Resolve MMSI-to-IMO only through documented cross-checks. AIS silence alone is not evidence of intentional concealment.
4. **Vessel identity test:** compare candidate dimensions, deck/bridge geometry, flag, owner/manager, cargo and voyage context with original video/stills and authoritative vessel records. Require multiple independent matches before naming a vessel.
5. **Sentinel-1 SAR:** search Copernicus Data Space for all scenes intersecting the polygon and time window. Log product ID, platform, acquisition UTC, mode, polarization, orbit direction, incidence angle, footprint, and processing level. Inspect calibrated imagery and land/sea masks; do not call an unprocessed bright return a confirmed ship or oil slick.
6. **Sentinel-2 optical:** search the same time/area. Record product ID, acquisition UTC, tile, cloud percentage, relevant bands, quality masks, and georeferencing. Optical non-detection under cloud/night/poor resolution is not evidence of absence.
7. **Sentinel-3 and NASA:** examine Sentinel-3 SLSTR/OLCI and NASA FIRMS VIIRS/MODIS for broad thermal/ocean context. Record sensor, pixel size, acquisition time, confidence/quality flags and fire radiative power where available. A thermal anomaly is not automatically a ship fire; absence of a detection does not rule one out.
8. **Terrain and basemap context:** maritime terrain is secondary, but use authoritative shoreline, bathymetry, navigation chart and anchorage references to validate named places and distances. Record dataset edition and scale.
9. **Wind, waves and currents:** extract hourly ERA5 wind and wave variables and Copernicus Marine surface-current fields for the bounded period and polygon. Preserve grid cell, units, depth, timestamp, dataset version and interpolation method. Do not infer oil-slick drift without a validated starting time/location and an explicit drift model.
10. **Video/visual evidence:** locate the earliest original upload; preserve URL, uploader, upload time, retrieval time, file hash, frame timestamps and any available metadata. Geolocate using stable vessel features and independently identifiable environmental cues. Do not infer camera coordinates from vessel identity alone.
11. **Coordinate reduction:** maintain a candidate table with each coordinate, source, timestamp, derivation, uncertainty radius/ellipse, and confidence. Only reduce the search area when independent evidence converges. Report a polygon or radius when a point would overstate precision.
12. **Independent review:** publish contradictory evidence and invite replication. Keep raw inputs, processing scripts/parameters, and derived outputs distinct.

### 2. Geographic and temporal framing

A maritime incident must be reconstructed in space **and time**. A ship's last AIS position, a news article's publication time, the time of an alleged impact, and the time a satellite passed overhead are four different observations. They must never be substituted for one another.

The first spatial anchor must come from the original UKMTO notice or another primary record that states a location, bearing, distance, or coordinate. If that record provides only a broad area, retain a broad area. Do not create a precise point by pinning the centre of a named sea lane or by copying a later vessel position.

The initial temporal window of 8–11 October 2026 is a search window, not a confirmed event interval. Replace or narrow it only when the original source provides an event time and timezone.

**Important sensor limitation:** a shipboard fire may be missed because of overpass timing, cloud, pixel size, thermal contrast, viewing geometry, or a short-lived event. A FIRMS detection is a thermal-anomaly observation, not proof of a vessel fire; no detection is not proof that no fire occurred. Sentinel-2 optical imagery may be unavailable or obscured. Sentinel-1 radar returns can be affected by sea state, wind, incidence angle, ship orientation, and processing choices.

For every scene actually acquired, record: platform, product ID, sensing start/end UTC, processing baseline/level, orbit direction, mode, polarization or bands, footprint, cloud/quality flags where relevant, download URL, retrieval UTC, file checksum, software, and processing parameters.

### 6. Evidence-to-location method

The investigation should maintain separate candidate records for (a) the Iranian-reported mine incident and (b) the UKMTO-described projectile/fire incident. They may be merged only if independent evidence matches event time, vessel particulars, position, and physical observations.

A candidate coordinate can enter the ledger only with:
- the original source or dataset identifier;
- observation/event time in UTC;
- whether the coordinate is observed, reported, interpolated, or analyst-derived;
- derivation method and CRS;
- uncertainty radius or ellipse;
- confidence grade and contradictory evidence.

The final map may legitimately show a broad polygon, several candidate tracks, or **no defensible location**. A point is not a required deliverable if the evidence cannot support one.
