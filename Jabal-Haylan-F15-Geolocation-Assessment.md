# Analytical Geolocation Assessment of the Suspected F-15 Crash Site near Jabal Haylan, Yemen

## Summary

This document presents an independent open-source geolocation assessment concerning the suspected location of an F-15 aircraft crash in the Jabal Haylan area of Yemen.

The assessment was developed by combining publicly available geographic, geological, meteorological, satellite-derived, topographic, and open-source information. Multiple independent clues were compared in order to progressively reduce the area of interest.

The result is an **analytical estimate, not an officially confirmed crash-site coordinate**.

## Estimated Location

**Primary estimated coordinate:**

**15.49761° N, 45.07517° E**

Approximate elevation associated with the investigated location:

**1,898 meters above sea level**

The estimated search area is approximately:

**1.5 km radius around the primary coordinate**

This should therefore be treated as an area of investigation rather than as a confirmed exact impact point.

## Geographic Reference

The investigated area is located in the broader Jabal Haylan volcanic region of Yemen.

A secondary geographic reference used during the analysis was the Jabal Haylan summit area:

**15.52417° N, 45.07472° E**

Approximate summit elevation referenced in the collected geographic material:

**2,002 meters**

These reference coordinates were used to compare terrain, elevation, slope orientation, and the relative position of the estimated crash area.

## Methodology

The investigation used a multi-source geolocation approach.

The main categories of information considered included:

- Geographic coordinates from public geographic databases
- Geological and volcanic information
- Topographic and elevation information
- Meteorological and atmospheric information
- Satellite-derived observations
- Open-source intelligence (OSINT)
- Public photographs and visual references
- Mapping databases
- Public discussions and forum material
- Open-source repositories and datasets
- Terrain and geographic relationships between known reference points

The objective was not to rely on a single source, but to compare independent clues and determine whether they converged on the same geographic area.

## Coordinate Reduction

During the investigation, several independently derived coordinate references were examined.

The collected information gradually reduced the broader geographic area toward the southern/eastern side of the Jabal Haylan region.

The coordinate:

**15.49761° N, 45.07517° E**

became the final analytical reference point used for the approximately 1.5 km-radius investigation area.

This coordinate should not be interpreted as an official government, military, aviation, or accident-investigation coordinate.

## Terrain and Elevation

Terrain was an important part of the analysis.

The investigated region contains significant elevation changes and mountainous terrain associated with the Jabal Haylan volcanic field.

Elevation, slope direction, mountain edges, ridgelines, and the relative position of surrounding terrain were considered when evaluating the estimated location.

## Satellite and Remote-Sensing Information

Satellite-derived information was considered as part of the broader investigation.

Relevant categories included:

- Sentinel imagery and derived geographic information
- MODIS and other freely available satellite datasets
- NASA-related remote-sensing resources
- Geological and terrain datasets
- Public satellite mapping services
- Publicly available imagery and geographic layers

These datasets were used primarily for terrain comparison, geographic context, and consistency checks.

The currently available public material does **not** independently establish the exact aircraft impact point.

## Meteorological Information

Meteorological conditions were also considered as supporting information.

Weather-related factors can be useful in a geolocation investigation because wind direction, visibility, cloud conditions, atmospheric conditions, and local terrain can affect the interpretation of imagery and eyewitness observations.

However, the available public material does not provide sufficiently precise meteorological measurements to independently determine the crash location.

Therefore, meteorological information should be treated as supporting evidence rather than as a determining factor.

## Confidence and Limitations

This assessment should be interpreted carefully.

The coordinate **15.49761° N, 45.07517° E** is an **independent analytical estimate**.

It is not presented as:

- an officially confirmed crash coordinate,
- an officially released military coordinate,
- an accident-investigation conclusion,
- or a verified impact point measured at meter-level accuracy.

The approximately **1.5 km radius** represents the remaining uncertainty of the analysis.

Further reduction of the area would require additional high-resolution imagery, independently verifiable visual evidence, or other primary-source information.
