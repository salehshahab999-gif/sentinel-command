# Analytical Geolocation Assessment of the Suspected F-15 Crash Site near Jabal Haylan, Yemen

## Summary

This document presents an independent open-source geolocation assessment concerning the suspected location of an F-15 aircraft crash in the Jabal Haylan area of Yemen.

The assessment was developed by combining publicly available geographic, geological, meteorological, satellite-derived, topographic, and open-source information. Multiple independent clues were compared in order to progressively reduce the area of interest.

The result is an **analytical estimate, not an officially confirmed crash-site coordinate**.

## Methodology

The investigation used a multi-source geolocation approach.

The main categories of information considered included:

- Geographic coordinates from public geographic databases
- Geological and volcanic information
- Topographic and elevation information
- Meteorological and atmospheric information
- Satellite-derived observations
- Open-source intelligence (OSINT)
- Public photographs and visual references
- Mapping databases
- Public discussions and forum material
- Open-source repositories and datasets
- Terrain and geographic relationships between known reference points

The objective was not to rely on a single source, but to compare independent clues and determine whether they converged on the same geographic area.

Meteorological conditions were also considered as supporting information.

However, the available public material does not provide sufficiently precise meteorological measurements to independently determine the crash location.

Therefore, meteorological information should be treated as supporting evidence rather than as a determining factor.

# Post-Publication Follow-up: Wreckage Disposition and Aircrew Status

**Research update:** 11 November 2026  
**Scope:** Publicly available reporting and media records reviewed after publication of the original geolocation assessment.  
**Status:** Open-source research log. This is not an official accident investigation, a confirmed recovery record, or a determination of the final custody of the wreckage.

## 1. Purpose and Important Distinction

The original assessment narrowed the suspected crash-area search region to an approximately 1.5 km radius. This follow-up addresses a separate question: what can public evidence establish about the physical wreckage after the first footage was released, and what became publicly known about the two-person aircrew?

Three different questions must not be conflated:

1. **Where was the footage recorded?** BBC Verify later geolocated the wreckage footage to an isolated area in the hills approximately 40 km west of Marib.
2. **Who had access to visible wreckage?** The Houthi-released footage shows armed personnel examining debris and lifting a large tail section at the filmed location.
3. **Who ultimately recovered, retained, moved, or examined the remaining aircraft components?** The public sources reviewed for this update do not establish the final disposition of the complete wreckage or its sensitive components.

The presence of personnel beside debris, or the handling of one large component, does not by itself prove that the complete aircraft was recovered or transported.

## 2. Executive Findings

- Publicly released footage dated 16 September 2026 shows Houthi personnel examining aircraft debris in Marib Governorate. A large tail section bearing Saudi markings is visible.
- BBC Verify reported on 17 September that it matched terrain features and the position of the sun in the footage against satellite imagery. It placed the filmed wreckage in isolated hills about 25 miles (40 km) west of Marib. BBC Verify also described a new dark mark visible in satellite imagery from 16 September as consistent with possible scorching or burn damage.
- Reuters published an expert assessment on 17 September in which Justin Bronk of RUSI identified visible structural features as consistent with an F-15SA. Reuters noted that the footage did not show the aircraft falling and therefore did not establish whether the crew ejected.
- A Sanaa-based military-media statement claimed that Saudi F-15 and Typhoon formations were sent to search for the wreckage and were forced to withdraw. This remains a party-to-the-conflict claim; the sources reviewed do not independently verify the claimed engagement or its outcome.
- On 18–19 September, a Sanaa-based official responsible for prisoners' affairs reportedly said that Saudi Arabia was seeking information about the two aircrew through a third-party mediator. The public report did not establish whether either crew member was alive, captured, rescued, or deceased.
- On 16 October, Sanaa News published a claim that one Saudi crew member had been captured and interviewed. The article did not provide a verifiable identity, the original interview recording, recording metadata, or independent confirmation. A near-verbatim republication on another site is not independent corroboration.
- **No independently verifiable public record was located in the sources reviewed showing that the complete wreckage was later removed, transported to Sana'a, displayed in a military exhibition, returned to Saudi Arabia, or transferred to the United States or the manufacturer.** This is a statement about the public evidence located for this update, not proof that no recovery or transfer occurred.

**Interpretation:** The strongest defensible conclusion is that Houthi personnel had access to, and publicly documented, at least some of the visible debris. The subsequent custody of the complete wreckage and individual components remains unresolved. Lack of later public imagery must not be treated as proof of a secret transfer, a successful recovery, or the disappearance of the wreckage.

## 5. Aircrew Status: Evidence Assessment

The aircraft is described in the reporting as a two-seat F-15 variant. The public record reviewed here does not establish the final status of both crew members.

- **Initial reporting:** BBC Verify and Reuters described the crew's fate as unknown in their initial coverage.
- **Mediator statement:** Reports on 18–19 September attributed to Al-Murtada a statement that Saudi Arabia was seeking information about the two crew members through a third-party mediator. The report did not identify the mediator or establish the crew's location or condition.
- **Later capture allegation:** Sanaa News reported on 16 October that one crew member had been captured and interviewed. The report, as located for this update, did not provide the primary interview recording or independently verifiable identity details. A second site reproduced the claim but did not supply separate evidence.
- **Second crew member:** No independently verified public update identifying the second crew member's status was located in this review.

The capture allegation should therefore remain classified as **SOURCE CLAIM / UNVERIFIED**, not as an established fact. No private medical information, patient records, personal identifiers, or non-public details about either crew member are included in this research log.

## 7. Open Research Questions and Public-Data Priorities

The following areas may help future contributors improve this record. They are research directions, not claims that relevant records have already been found.

1. **Public civil-defence and fire-service reporting:** Search for dated, public incident summaries or official statements concerning a major fire, aircraft debris, firefighting, or emergency response in the relevant governorate. Preserve the distinction between an incident report and proof that it concerns this aircraft.
2. **Public hospital and health-authority statements:** Use only official public statements and aggregate reporting. Do not seek, publish, or infer identities from patient registers, admission logs, medical records, personal health data, or private family information.
3. **Sanaa-based official communications:** Monitor public statements by the military spokesperson, Saba, Yemen Military Media, and the National Committee for Prisoners' Affairs. Record the original publication date and preserve the difference between an official claim and independent verification.
4. **Saudi official communications:** Check the Saudi Ministry of Defence, Saudi Press Agency, coalition spokespersons, and other official public channels for any acknowledgment, denial, missing-aircraft notice, crew statement, or recovery announcement.
5. **US and manufacturer statements:** Check public releases from the US Department of Defense, CENTCOM, the US Embassy, and the manufacturer for explicit statements about the incident or publicly acknowledged recovery activity. Do not infer participation from routine defence cooperation or maintenance contracts.
6. **Public satellite and remote-sensing archives:** Compare dated, openly accessible imagery where available. Record the sensor, acquisition time, publication time, product type, spatial resolution, cloud cover, and known limitations. A before/after visual difference should be described first as an observation; its cause should remain a separate inference.
7. **Local reporting, photographs, and forums:** Seek original posts and original media rather than reposts. Check whether multiple accounts are repeating the same source. Forum commentary can generate leads but must not be counted as independent confirmation.
8. **Wreckage custody and transport:** Look for verifiable public images or official reporting that actually document collection, loading, transport, display, or transfer of identifiable aircraft components. Do not infer a transfer solely from the later absence of images at the original location.

### Evidence labels

Use one of the following labels for each new entry:

- **OBSERVED FACT:** Directly visible in an authenticated image, video, document, or dataset.
- **SOURCE CLAIM:** A statement made by a named person, organization, government, or media outlet that has not been independently verified.
- **ANALYTICAL INFERENCE:** An interpretation derived from one or more observations. State the assumptions and uncertainty.
- **UNRESOLVED:** A question for which the reviewed evidence is insufficient or contradictory.

## 9. Current Follow-up Conclusion

As of 11 November 2026, public evidence supports the conclusion that Houthi personnel inspected and filmed at least some of the aircraft debris at the location later geolocated by BBC Verify. The available public record does not establish the final custody of the complete wreckage, whether major components were subsequently transported, or the independently verified status of either crew member.

The later claim that one crew member was captured remains unverified in the absence of the original interview recording and independent corroboration. The alleged Saudi inquiry through a third-party mediator is a reported statement by a Sanaa-based official, not confirmation of the crew's status.

The next useful improvement to this research will come from new, independently verifiable primary evidence: a dated official statement, authenticated original media, a documented public recovery record, or a time-matched satellite observation. Until then, these questions should remain explicitly open.
